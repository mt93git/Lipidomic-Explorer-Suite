# 2. PHOSPHATIDYLINOSITOL PHOSPHATES (POLYPHOSPHOINOSITIDES) IN ANIMALS

## A. STRUCTURE AND OCCURRENCE
- Discovered by Mable and Lowell Hokin in the 1950s. PI is phosphorylated on the inositol ring at the 3-, 4- and/or 5-positions by specific kinases to give seven phosphoinositides with vital signalling and metabolic roles.
- Present at low levels (~0.5–1% of total lipids) in the inner leaflet of eukaryotic membranes; very low in prokaryotes.
- Turnover is very rapid.

## B. BIOSYNTHESIS AND INTRACELLULAR DISTRIBUTION (THE PHOSPHOINOSITIDE CYCLE)
Diagram labels: PI synthesis; CDP-DAG (rate-limiting); CDP-diacylglycerol (CDP-DAG); CTP; PPi; PI synthase (PIS); CMP; Phosphatidylinositol (PI); Plasma membrane; Endosomes; Golgi network; Endoplasmic reticulum; Mitochondria; Nucleus
- PI is synthesised on the endoplasmic reticulum (ER), especially at mitochondria-associated membranes (MAM).
- PIS can catalyse the reverse reaction; net synthesis depends on concentrations.
- PI is transported to other membranes by vesicles, tubular carriers and by lipid transfer proteins (PITPs) and 'Nir2' family transporters.

Subcellular (general rules)
- Most mono-phosphorylations occur in endomembranes (Golgi, endosomes).
- Di- and tri-phosphorylations occur primarily at the plasma membrane.
- Distinct PIP composition in each compartment supports specific functions.

## C. FUNCTIONS OF MAJOR PHOSPHOINOSITIDES

## PI(4)P
- Abundant in Golgi and plasma membrane.
- Regulates Golgi structure, vesicle trafficking, and formation of secretory vesicles.
- Recruits and activates effector proteins via PH domains.

Diagram labels: Golgi membrane

## PI(4,5)P2
- Major PIP in plasma membrane (low level).
- Substrate for PLC → IP3 + DAG.
- Regulates actin cytoskeleton, ion channels, and vesicle fusion and endocytosis.

Diagram labels: PLC; IP3; DAG

## PI(3,4,5)P3
- Produced from PI(4,5)P2 by PI3-kinase.
- Recruits proteins with PH domains (e.g., Akt/PKB, PDK1).
- Essential for cell survival, growth, metabolism, immune signalling.

Diagram labels: Akt; PDK1

## PI(3)P
- Early endosome marker.
- Regulates endosomal trafficking and autophagy initiation.
- Precursor of PI(3,5)P2.

Diagram labels: Endosome

## PI(3,5)P2
- Late endosomes and lysosomes.
- Regulates ion channels (TPCs) and membrane trafficking.

Diagram labels: Lysosome

## D. THE PHOSPHATIDYLINOSITOL CYCLE

Key
- Kinase
- Phosphatase
- Phospholipase

PI = phosphatidylinositol
PIP = phosphoinositide
K = kinase
Pase = phosphatase
PLC = phospholipase C

- 18 inter-conversion reactions mediated by ≥20 kinases and ≥34 phosphatases (8 and 10 classes).
- Enzymes are highly specific, often in large protein complexes >700 kDa.
- PI4K isoforms (PI4KA, PI4KB, PI4K2A, PI4K2B) act in different membranes.

## E. DISEASE ASSOCIATIONS AND BIOMARKERS

## Diseases
- Cancer (altered PI3K/Akt signalling; PIP levels and enzymes).
- Diabetes and insulin resistance.
- Liver disease (NAFLD, cirrhosis).
- Neurodegenerative disorders.
- Cardiovascular disease.
- Inflammatory and immune diseases.

## Biomarkers
- Altered PIP profiles in plasma, CSF, or tissues.
- Used for early diagnosis and monitoring therapy.
- Lipidomics by LC-MS/MS enables sensitive quantification.

## Therapeutic targets
- PI3K, PTEN, PIP kinases/phosphatases.
- IP3 receptors, DAG kinases.
- Modulators of PI cycle are in clinical trials for cancer, inflammation, and metabolic diseases.

## F. KEY POINTS
- PI is the precursor of seven phosphoinositides (PIPs).
- PIPs are produced and interconverted by specific kinases, phosphatases, and phospholipase C.
- They are concentrated in the inner leaflet and in specific intracellular membranes.
- PIPs act as second messengers, recruit proteins, and regulate trafficking, cytoskeleton, and gene expression.
- Dysregulation of the PI cycle is implicated in many human diseases.

## KEY MESSAGE
Phosphatidylinositol phosphates are dynamic, low-abundance lipids that orchestrate diverse cellular processes through highly regulated cycles of phosphorylation and dephosphorylation. Spatial and temporal control of these lipids is central to cell signalling and homeostasis.
