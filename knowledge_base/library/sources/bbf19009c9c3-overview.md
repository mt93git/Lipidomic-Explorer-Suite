# NON-ACIDIC DI- AND OLIGOGLYCOSPHINGOLIPIDS IN ANIMALS

## (untitled panel)
Non-acidic di- and oligoglycosphingolipids, i.e., with two to many more carbohydrate moieties attached to a ceramide unit, are vital components of cellular membranes of most eukaryotic organisms and some bacteria. Of these, lactosylceramide (diglycosyl) has crucial functions of its own, but it is also the biosynthetic precursor of most of the non-acidic oligoglycosylceramides, and the acidic sulfatides and gangliosides, which are discussed in separate web pages for practical convenience, although the biosynthetic mechanisms and metabolism of all are inextricably linked. These lipids are amphipathic, with a variable hydrophilic carbohydrate-rich head group and a hydrophobic ceramide tail. Although usually present in low abundance, they are required for viability of cells, notably during embryogenesis and development.

## 1. LACTOSYLCERAMIDE (LacCer) AND OTHER DIOSYLCERAMIDES IN ANIMALS
The most important and abundant diosylceramide is β-D-galactosyl-(1→4)-β-D-glucosyl-(1→1')-ceramide (Galβ1-4Glcβ1Cer), more conveniently termed lactosylceramide (LacCer), using the trivial name of the disaccharide.
In the early literature, it was termed 'cytolipin H'.

### STRUCTURE OF LACTOSYLCERAMIDE
Diagram labels: Galactose (β1→4); Glucose (β1→1'); Ceramide; Lactosylceramide (Galβ1-4Glcβ1Cer; LacCer)

In animal tissues, the precursor of LacCer is glucosylceramide, which is transported by the sphingolipid transport protein FAPP2 from the site of synthesis in the cis-Golgi to the trans-Golgi, where it must first cross from the cytosolic side of the membrane possibly via the action of a flippase. Biosynthesis of LacCer then involves addition of the second monosaccharide unit as its activated nucleotide derivative (UDP-galactose) to monoglucosylceramide on the luminal side of the Golgi apparatus in a reaction catalysed by β-1,4-galactosyltransferases (B4GALT1 and B4GALT2). The LacCer produced can be further glycosylated, or it can be transferred to the plasma membrane mainly by a non-vesicular mechanism that is poorly understood, but it cannot be translocated back to the cytosolic leaflet. Deletion of the LacCer synthase is embryonically lethal.

## OTHER DIOSYLCERAMIDES (ANIMAL TISSUES)
- Galabiosylceramide (Galα1→4Galβ1-1'Cer) is found in small amounts in kidney, pancreas and cerebrospinal fluid; it accumulates in Fabry's disease and is the precursor of the minor 'gala' series of oligoglycosylceramides.
- Other diosylceramides containing mannose units may be present in some primitive animals and in a few plants.
- Several insects contain mannose linked to GlcCer (βMan(1→4)Glc).
A starfish contains gentiobiosyl- and cellobiosylceramide as well as LacCer; other marine invertebrates contain melibiosylceramides.

## LACTOSYLSPHINGOSINE (LYSO-LACOSYLCERAMIDE)
The deacylated (lyso) form of LacCer has been detected at low levels in brain where it may have some unspecified function.

## KEY PHYSIOLOGICAL AND PATHOLOGICAL ASPECTS
- Forms micro-domains on neutrophils and macrophages (recognize, engulf and eliminate pathogens).
- Interaction with growth factor receptors regulates their physiological properties.
- Activates NADPH oxidase → ROS → signalling cascades affecting proliferation, adhesion, migration and angiogenesis.
- PLA2 activation → arachidonic acid → prostaglandins and other eicosanoids.
- Disruption of pathways exacerbates cancer and cardiovascular diseases; affects neural damage states (e.g., multiple sclerosis).
- Elevated levels may contribute to cardiomyocyte dysfunction in hypoplastic left heart syndrome in infants and are a biomarker of intestinal barrier dysfunction in IBD.

## FUNCTIONS OF LACTOSYLCERAMIDE

Assists in stabilizing membranes and activating receptor molecules in raft micro-domains.
Requires very-long chain fatty acids (e.g., 24:0, 24:1). Interacts with gangliosides (e.g., GM3).

Binds to pathogenic bacteria and fungi via carbohydrate–carbohydrate interactions (β-glucans, lipoarabinomannans). Receptor for fungal lectins (Candida, Cryptococcus sp.).

Activation of signalling pathways (proliferation, adhesion, migration, angiogenesis, prostaglandin synthesis)

## Roles in health and disease
- Regulates growth factor receptors.
- Participates in neutrophils (70% of GSLs) and macrophages.
- Promotes chemotaxis, phagocytosis and superoxide generation via Src family kinase (e.g., Lyn) and other enzymes.
- Dysregulation linked to cancer, cardiovascular diseases, neural damage (e.g., multiple sclerosis), atherothrombosis and other inflammatory states.
- Plasma d18:1/16:0 LacCer is a biomarker of intestinal barrier dysfunction in inflammatory bowel disease.

## SUMMARY
- LacCer is the most important diosylceramide in animals.
- Synthesised in the Golgi from GlcCer by β-1,4-galactosyltransferases.
- Central intermediate for biosynthesis of complex glycosphingolipids.
- Essential roles in membrane organization, receptor regulation, innate immunity and signalling.
- Dysregulation is linked to multiple diseases; metabolism of LacCer is a potential therapeutic target.
