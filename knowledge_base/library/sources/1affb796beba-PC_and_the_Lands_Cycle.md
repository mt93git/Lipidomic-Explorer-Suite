# 3. REMODELLING OF PHOSPHATIDYLCHOLINE – THE LANDS’ CYCLE

## (untitled panel)
The fatty acid and molecular species compositions of phosphatidylcholine (PC) in animal tissues are determined post-synthesis by the Lands’ cycle – orchestrated hydrolysis, acyl-CoA synthesis and re-acylation (or transacylation) reactions. Similar cycles operate with all glycerophospholipid classes and generate the selective compositions required for vital functions and health.

Diagram labels: Phosphatidylcholine (PC); sn-1; sn-2; sn-3

## Key points
- Remodelling (Lands’ cycle) determines fatty acid position and molecular species composition.
- Most active in nervous and immune systems.
- Dysregulation is linked to cancer, hepatotoxicity and other diseases.
- Intermediates (e.g., lysophospholipids) are bioactive signalling molecules.

## A. LANDS’ CYCLE – MAIN MECHANISM IN ANIMALS (part 1: de-acylation)
## 1 De-acylation by phospholipase A2 (PLA2)
Hydrolyses the sn-2 ester bond specifically.

- ≥15 PLA2 families (Ca2+-dependent or independent; secreted or intracellular).
- Generates free fatty acid (FFA) and LPC.
- Products used in metabolism and signalling.

Diagram labels: Phosphatidylcholine (PC); Lysophosphatidylcholine (LPC); PLA2; H2O; Free fatty acid

## B. LANDS’ CYCLE – MAIN MECHANISM IN ANIMALS (part 2: re-acylation)
Diagram labels: Lysophosphatidylcholine (LPC); Acyl-CoA; LPCAT; CoA-SH; Phosphatidylcholine (PC, remodelling product)

## Major LPCATs involved in PC remodelling in animals
| Enzyme (alias) | Primary location | Main features / fatty acid preference |
|---|---|---|
| LPCAT1 (LPLAT7) | ER, PM, mitochondria | Prefers stearate > palmitate at sn-1 position. |
| LPCAT3 (LPLAT12 or MBOAT5) | ER, PM, mitochondria | Prefers linoleoyl (18:2) and arachidonoyl (20:4) in sn-2. Maintains lipid homeostasis; intestine absorption, lipoprotein secretion, lipogenesis. |
| LPCAT2 (LPLAT9) | ER, PM | Uses 1-acyl and 1-alkyl LPC; enriched in inflammatory cells (macrophages, neutrophils); contributes to oxylipin precursor generation. |
| LPCAT4 (LPLAT10) | ER | Prefers insertion of unsaturated fatty acids at sn-1 (atypical LPCAT). |
| LPCAT1 (LPLAT8) | Lung (ER) | High specificity for palmitoyl-CoA; generates dipalmitoyl PC of lung surfactant. |

## Additional aspects
- PC species rich in PUFA are maintained more by de novo synthesis than by remodelling.
- Over-expression of LPCAT genes is associated with cancer and other pathologies.

Cell types: Macrophage; Neutrophil

Lysophospholipid intermediates (e.g., LPC) and free fatty acids are bioactive signalling molecules.

## D. REMODELLING IN PLANTS AND YEASTS
## Plants
- PC is central to glycerolipid metabolism.
- Major entry point for acyl groups exported from plastid to ER.
- Supplies fatty acids for TAG synthesis in developing seeds.
- Provides DAGs for thylakoid lipids (e.g., galactosyldiacylglycerol).
- In Arabidopsis, LPCAT1 and LPCAT2 remodel, preferring sn-2 with plastid-exported fatty acids.
- Strong preference for C18-unsaturated acyl chains over 16:0 in many plants.
- Remodelling responds to abiotic stresses.

## Functions / significance
- Alters molecular composition without total synthesis.
- Regulated with other lipid pathways; affects growth.
- Gpc1 is specific for PC in yeasts (does not act on other phospholipids).

## E. OVERALL IMPORTANCE AND IMPLICATIONS
## Biological roles of PC remodelling
Nervous system
- Maintains membrane fluidity and curvature.
- Supplies PUFA for synaptic signalling.

Immune system
- Generates oxylipin precursors.
- Modulates inflammatory responses.

Liver and metabolism
- Regulates lipid absorption, lipoprotein secretion and de novo lipogenesis.

Membrane integrity
- Maintains optimal acyl composition.
- Supports protein function and trafficking.

## Pathological consequences of dysregulation
- Cancer progression and metastasis
- Hepatotoxicity and fatty liver
- Neurodegenerative diseases
- Inflammatory disorders
- Metabolic syndrome

## Summary
A balance of de novo synthesis and the Lands’ cycle ensures the precise molecular architecture of PC in each tissue – essential for membrane structure, signalling and overall cellular homeostasis.
