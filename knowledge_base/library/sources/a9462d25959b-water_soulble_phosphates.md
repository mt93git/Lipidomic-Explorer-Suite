# 3. WATER-SOLUBLE INOSITOL PHOSPHATES (AND DIACYLGLYCEROLS)

## A. PHOSPHOLIPASE C AND DIACYLGLYCEROL (DAG) GENERATION
- Phosphoinositide-specific phospholipase C (PLC) hydrolyses PI phosphates.
- Six PLC families (≥13 isoenzymes): β, γ, δ, ε, η, ζ.
- PLCs possess pleckstrin homology (PH) domains (~120 aa) that bind phosphoinositides in membranes.
- Activated by GPCRs, RTKs, Ras-like GTPases and Ca2+ → translocate from cytosol to plasma membrane.
- PLCβ is most widely implicated; PLCγ1 in cancer; PLCγ2 in neurodegeneration; PLCε in cardiovascular system.

Diagram labels: Plasma membrane (inner leaflet); PLC (activated); sn-1; sn-2; sn-1,2-Diacylglycerol (DAG); Plus; Inositol 1,4,5-trisphosphate (IP3)

- DAG remains in membrane → activates protein kinase C (PKC) isoforms.
- IP3 diffuses in cytosol → releases Ca2+ from ER stores.

## B. INOSITOL PHOSPHATES (IPs)
- Up to ~60 inositol phosphates possible; ≥37 identified in nature.
- Generated by PLC and by specific kinases and phosphatases.
- Act as second messengers and regulators of many cellular processes.
- Produced in specific membrane/nuclear domains → act locally and transiently.

Diagram labels: Common inositol phosphates; Inositol 1-phosphate (IP1); Inositol 1,4-bisphosphate (IP2); Inositol 1,4,5-trisphosphate (IP3); Inositol 1,3,4,5-tetrakisphosphate (IP4); Inositol 1,2,3,4,5,6-hexakisphosphate (IP6, phytic acid)

## C. IP3–Ca2+–DAG SIGNALING AT ER–PLASMA MEMBRANE CONTACT SITES
1. Agonist binds GPCR/RTK → activates PLC.
2. PLC hydrolyzes PI(4,5)P2 → DAG + IP3.
3. IP3 diffuses to ER and binds IP3 receptor (IP3R).
4. IP3R opens Ca2+ channel → Ca2+ released from ER lumen.
5. Ca2+ rise + DAG → activate PKC and other Ca2+-dependent targets.

Diagram labels: Extracellular agonist; GPCR; PLC; DAG; PKC; PI(4,5)P2; IP3; IP3R; Downstream effects; ER (Ca2+ store); Ca2+

Contact sites couple the plasma membrane and ER to control Ca2+ dynamics and cell responses.

## D. MAJOR FUNCTIONS OF INOSITOL PHOSPHATES
- Regulate intracellular Ca2+, ion fluxes and cell secretion.
- Control actin cytoskeleton dynamics and cell shape.
- Mediate vesicle trafficking and membrane fusion/fission.
- Modulate gene transcription, RNA processing and nuclear export.
- Scaffolding/cofactor role in protein–protein interactions.
- Act as 'molecular switches' via specific phosphorylation patterns.

Examples of functions

| IP | Function |
|---|---|
| IP3 | Releases Ca2+ from ER via IP3 receptors. |
| IP4 (Ins(1,4,5,6)P4) | Regulates Cl− secretion in intestinal epithelial cells; Ca2+-independent roles. |
| IP5 | Modulates transporter activity and vesicle trafficking. |
| IP6 (phytic acid) | Antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, lowers serum cholesterol; binds metals and proteins. |
| Pyrophosphates (InsP7, InsP8) | High-energy signals; regulate kinase/phosphatase activities and chromatin structure. |

Sites of action: Plasma membrane, endosomes, Golgi, nucleus, mitochondria, cytoskeleton, and enzyme complexes.

## F. OTHER SOURCES AND ROLES OF DIACYLGLYCEROL
- DAG also produced from phosphatidylcholine by phospholipase C and from TAG via DAG lipases.
- Acts with Ca2+ to activate conventional and novel PKC isoforms.
- Also activates RasGRP, Munc13, and other DAG-binding proteins.

## H. KEY POINTS
- PLC-generated IPs and DAGs are central second messengers.
- IPs act rapidly and locally to control Ca2+, enzymes, trafficking and gene expression.
- DAG activates PKC and multiple signalling proteins.
- Phosphoinositide metabolism forms a tightly regulated, compartmentalized cycle essential for cell function and health.
