# Stereoisomers of diacyl-sn-glycerols

## (untitled panel)
Diacylglycerols (or “diglycerides”) are esters of the trihydric alcohol glycerol in which two of the hydroxyl groups are esterified with long-chain fatty acids. They can exist as three stereochemical isomers (see our web document on Triacylglycerols (part 1) for a discussion of nomenclature). When the stereochemistry is unknown or when the mixture is racemic, sn-1,2- and 2,3-diacylglycerols are sometimes termed α,β-diacylglycerols while sn-1,3-diacylglycerols may be designated α,α′-diacylglycerols.

## (untitled panel)
Diacylglycerols are formed in animal and plant tissues as intermediates in the biosynthesis of triacylglycerols and other glycerolipids and during the hydrolysis of these by lipases. While their presence is of technological relevance in commercial seed oils as small amounts can have a profound influence on the physical properties, the function of sn-1,2-diacylglycerols derived from phospholipids as signalling mediators in animal tissues is of special importance for human health and wellbeing and is a focus of this web page.

## 1. METABOLISM OF sn-1,2-DIACYLGLYCEROLS IN ANIMALS
sn-1,2-Diacylglycerols tend to be minor components of most tissues and membranes, but in animal cells, they are required as:

- intermediates in the biosynthesis of many glycerolipids
- signalling molecules (second messengers) that respond to external stimuli to modulate vital biochemical mechanisms.

They are the only one of the three stereoisomers that act in this way. They are synthesized and metabolized by numerous enzymes at spatially different cellular locations, each with distinct enzymatic properties and selectivities.

## Sources of sn-1,2-diacylglycerols
- Intermediates in the biosynthesis of glycerolipids
- Hydrolysis of glycerolipids by lipases (intracellular and digestive)
- Sphingomyelin formation (exchange with ceramide)

## Lingual lipase in the mouth
Scottish thistle

- Hydrolyses sn-3 position of some dietary TAGs preferentially
- Generates mainly sn-1,2-diacylglycerols
- Pancreatic lipase → racemic mixture of sn-1,2- and 2,3-DAGs
- Adipose tissue lipase → racemic 1,3-DAGs and 2,3-DAGs

- To form major glycerolipids

## 4. PHYSICAL PROPERTIES AND ROLES OF sn-1,2-DIACYLGLYCEROLS IN MEMBRANES
### Intercalation in membranes
Small head group → inverted conical shape → negative curvature stress

Diagram labels: Fission; Fusion

### Membrane effects
- Induce negative curvature → promote membrane fission or fusion
- Facilitate vesicle trafficking and organelle dynamics
- Essential for nuclear envelope assembly after mitosis (cis-Golgi)

### Protein interactions
Diagram labels: Protein

Bind to and activate specific proteins (e.g., PKC, RasGRP)

### Trans-bilayer movement
- Rapid flip-flop in fluid membranes
- Slower across ordered raft-like domains (rich in sphingomyelin and cholesterol)

Diagram labels: Raft domain (ordered)

### Key DAG species
- Mainly 1-stearoyl-2-arachidonoyl-sn-glycerol in many cells
