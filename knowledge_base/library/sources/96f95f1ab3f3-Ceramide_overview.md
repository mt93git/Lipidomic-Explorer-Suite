# CERAMIDES

## (untitled panel)
Ceramides are central sphingolipid intermediates and potent lipid mediators that regulate a wide range of biological processes in eukaryotic cells.

## STRUCTURE OF A CERAMIDE
Diagram labels: Sphingoid base (long-chain base); Fatty acyl chain

n = usually 12–20 (often 18)
m = usually 14–24 (often 16–24)

Key features
- Amide-linked long-chain (sphingoid) base and fatty acid.
- >200 molecular species exist in mammalian cells.
- Free ceramides are present in small amounts but are important mediators.
- Precursors of all complex sphingolipids and of sphingosine-1-phosphate and ceramide-1-phosphate.

## FUNCTIONS OF CERAMIDES
Lipid mediators
- Regulate apoptosis, cell cycle arrest, differentiation, senescence, stress responses and inflammation.

Membrane structure
- Promote membrane rigidity, lipid raft formation and membrane domain organization.

Metabolic regulators
- Modulate insulin signalling, calcium homeostasis, autophagy and mitochondrial function.

Precursors of signalling lipids
- Sphingosine-1-phosphate (S1P) promotes survival and proliferation.
- Ceramide-1-phosphate (C1P) is involved in cell growth and stress responses.

Skin barrier lipids
- Ceramides are major components of the stratum corneum; essential for epidermal barrier and water retention.

Disease relevance
- Dysregulation is linked to cancer, metabolic disorders, neurodegeneration, inflammatory and skin diseases.

## THE CENTRAL ROLE OF CERAMIDE IN SPHINGOLIPID METABOLISM
Diagram labels: Sphingolipid biosynthesis; Complex sphingolipids (sphingomyelin, glycosphingolipids, gangliosides); De novo synthesis (in ER); CERAMIDE; Polar head group removal (lysosomes) recycling; Ceramide breakdown (catabolism); Sphingoid bases (sphingosine, etc.)

Ceramide is at the hub of the ceramide (sphingomyelin) cycle.

## 3. KEY PHYSIOLOGICAL ROLES
- Apoptosis induction via mitochondrial outer membrane permeabilization.
- Cell differentiation and senescence.
- Regulation of cell proliferation and tumour suppression.
- Modulation of inflammation and immune responses.
- Maintenance of skin barrier and hydration.
- Lipid raft organization and membrane curvature.
- Role in neurodegeneration and lysosomal storage disorders.

## 4. DISEASE ASSOCIATIONS
- Cancer – altered ceramide metabolism.
- Neurodegenerative diseases (e.g., Alzheimer's, Parkinson's).
- Metabolic disorders – insulin resistance, obesity, diabetes.
- Lysosomal storage diseases (e.g., Gaucher, Niemann–Pick).
- Skin diseases – atopic dermatitis, ichthyosis.
- Cardiovascular diseases and atherosclerosis.

## Key message
Ceramides are bioactive sphingolipids at the crossroads of metabolism and signalling. Their diverse molecular species, tissue-specific roles and tightly regulated metabolism underlie essential physiological functions and link their dysregulation to many human diseases.

## Outlook
New lipidomics tools are revealing the complexity of ceramide species and compartmentalization, opening avenues for targeted therapies that modulate ceramide pathways.
