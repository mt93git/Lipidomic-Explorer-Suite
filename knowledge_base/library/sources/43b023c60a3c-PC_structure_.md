# PHOSPHATIDYLCHOLINE (PC) – STRUCTURE AND OCCURRENCE

## (untitled panel)
Phosphatidylcholine or 1,2-diacyl-sn-glycero-3-phosphocholine is a neutral (zwitterionic) phospholipid over a wide pH range. It is usually the most abundant glycerophospholipid in animals and plants, often ~50% of total phospholipids. In nucleated cells it is concentrated (80–90%) in the outer leaflet of the plasma membrane and is the major phospholipid in plasma lipoproteins (especially HDL).

## Chemical structure (diacyl species)
- R1, R2 = fatty acyl chains (vary in chain length and degree of unsaturation)
- Zwitterionic head group (net charge = 0)
- Forms bilayers; major component of outer membrane leaflet

Diagram labels: sn-1; sn-2; sn-3

## Ether analogue: Platelet-activating factor (PAF)
1-alkyl-2-acetyl-sn-glycero-3-phosphocholine (see separate webpage)

Diagram labels: R (alkyl)

## Occurrence
- Abundant in animals and plants (~50% of phospholipids).
- Key structural lipid of membranes; major in plasma lipoproteins.
- Less common in bacteria (~10% of species); absent in E. coli and B. subtilis.
- Functions partially overlap with sphingomyelin, but PC has many unique roles.

## Distribution in cell membranes
Nucleated cell plasma membrane

Erythrocyte membrane

Diagram labels: Outer leaflet (PC-rich: 80–90%); Inner leaflet (PC low, PS, PE-rich); PC almost exclusively in outer leaflet

## Other related lipids
- Lysophosphatidylcholine (LPC): one fatty acyl chain.
- Platelet-activating factor (PAF): ether analogue with potent bioactivity.
- Lysophosphatidylcholine and related lipids have signalling and other biological roles (see below).

## (untitled panel)
Saturated fatty acids predominate in position sn-1, while di- and polyunsaturated fatty acids are enriched in position sn-2. Long-chain polyunsaturated fatty acids (20:4, 22:6) are exclusively in sn-2 in most animal tissues.
