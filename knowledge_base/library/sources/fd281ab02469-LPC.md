# 6. LYSOPHOSPHATIDYLCHOLINE (LPC)

## Structure
Diagram labels: sn-1; sn-2

## Occurrence and sources
- Trace in most tissues; high in plasma (150–500 µM).
- Hepatocytes secrete LPC bound to albumin.
- Formed by LCAT: transfers sn-2 fatty acid from PC to cholesterol → cholesterol esters + LPC.
- Also from endothelial lipase action on PC in HDL.
- Produced in tissues by phospholipase A2 (PLA2) as part of the de-acylation/re-acylation (Lands) cycle.
- In intestines: from dietary & biliary PC → absorbed as LPC and re-esterified before export in chylomicrons.

## BIOPHYSICAL PROPERTIES
- High concentrations → detergent properties; can disrupt membranes.
- Low concentrations → readily inserts into bilayers and alters membrane curvature and protein function indirectly.
- In plasma, bound to albumin/lipoproteins → reduced free concentration.

## PATHOPHYSIOLOGICAL ROLES (mainly pro-inflammatory)
- Component of oxidized LDL in plasma and atherosclerotic lesions (generated by overexpression or enhanced PLA2 activity).
- Elevated in joint fluids of rheumatoid arthritis patients.
- Major component of platelet-derived microvesicles: activates platelet receptor → vascular inflammation and plaque instability.
- Blocks early hemifusion intermediates in cell-cell fusion, enhances parasite invasion, inhibits nitric oxide production (e.g., in Chagas disease).
- High levels in cervical cancer (potential diagnostic marker).
- Reduced in some malignancies; protective during chemotherapy.
- Stearoyl-LPC is anti-inflammatory and protective against lethal sepsis in experimental animals.
- Precursor of lysophosphatidic acid (LPA) via autotaxin in plasma; some effects attributed to LPA.
- Biomarker: elevated 26:0-LPC in Zellweger spectrum disorders (e.g., X-linked adrenoleukodystrophy).
- Maternal PC → sn-2-LPC at placenta (via endothelial lipase) facilitates transfer of PUFAs to fetus.

## SIGNALLING AND CELLULAR FUNCTIONS
Possible receptors

GPCRs: GPR119, GPR40, GPR55, G2A (GPR132)

Also: TLRs (Toll-like receptors)

Ion channels: TRPV4, others

- Activates PLC → DAG + IP3 → ↑ intracellular Ca2+ → activates PKC.
- By binding to GPR119 → regulates glucose-dependent insulin secretion.
- Stimulates MAPK in certain cells; promotes demyelination in the nervous system.
- Through TRPV4 in keratinocytes → persistent itching and chronic pain; 18:0-LPC elevated in burn pain.
- Peroxisomal PLA2γ generates 2-arachidonoyl-LPC → relevant to eicosanoid generation; enriched in carotid atheroma of type 2 diabetic patients.
- Induces COX-2 in vascular endothelial cells.
- Enhances macrophage phagocytosis in the presence of T lymphocytes (beneficial for innate immunity).

## REGULATION OF LPC LEVELS
- LPC levels are tightly regulated.
- Enzymes (cytosolic serine hydrolases):
  – LYPLA1 (lysophospholipase A1): removes sn-1 acyl chain.
  – LYPLA2 (lysophospholipase A2): removes sn-2 acyl chain.
- Alkaline sphingomyelinase removes phosphocholine → 2-monoacylglycerol (inert).

Diagram labels: Lysophosphatidylcholine (LPC); H2O; LYPLA1; H2O; LYPLA2; Glycerophosphocholine (GPC); 2-Monoacylglycerol

## Fate of glycerophosphocholine (GPC)
- Enters Lands’ cycle (re-acylation) → LPC → PC.
- Further degradation → free choline + glycerol phosphate.

## Physiological roles of LPC in digestion
- Derived from hydrolysis of dietary/biliary PC.
- Absorbed and re-esterified before chylomicron secretion.
- Aids intestinal lipid handling and absorption.

## Therapeutic considerations
- Anti-cancer ether analogues (alkyl-LPCs) induce apoptosis; used in experimental therapy and anti-parasitic treatments.
- Some beneficial effects reported, but interpretation depends on adequate carrier protein levels in studies.

# 7. OTHER PHOSPHATIDYLCHOLINE ANALOGUES ANDMETABOLITES

## A. Phosphatidylsulfocholine (PSC)
- Sulfonium analogue of PC: two methyl groups on sulfur instead of three on nitrogen.
- Discovered in the diatom Nitzschia alba; replaces PC completely as the major phospholipid.
- Now found in some other marine diatoms and algae.
- Isotopic studies: both methyl groups and sulfur derived from methionine.
- Related lipid: phosphatidyl-S,S-dimethylpropanethiol reported from several algae.

Diagram labels: Phosphatidylsulfocholine (PSC); Diatom; Nitzschia alba

## B. Alkylphosphocholines / alkylphosphoethanolamines
Example:
1-alkyl-2-acyl-sn-glycero-3-phosphocholine (alkylphosphocholine)

- Saturated/monoenoic ethers (C15–C25) accumulate in Sjögren-Larsson syndrome (deficiency of fatty aldehyde dehydrogenase).
- Proposed origin: from fatty alcohols via sphingomyelin synthases.
- Serve as diagnostic biomarkers.
- Synthetic ether analogues show strong anti-cancer activity (trigger apoptosis) and treat parasitic infections (e.g., leishmaniasis).

## C. Phosphatidylarsenocholine (minor in marine organisms)
- Minor component of lipids of several marine organisms.
- Discussed in the web page on arsenolipids.

## E. Emerging and diagnostic aspects

## Biomarkers and disease
- Changes in PC or LPC species in plasma/tissues reported in liver, cardiovascular, kidney, lung, brain diseases, cancer, sepsis and diabetes.
- Altered PC biosynthesis linked to NAFLD, liver failure, poor regeneration.
- Imbalance of PC/PE ratio affects insulin sensitivity and glucose homeostasis.

## Newborn screening
- Elevated 26:0-LPC in dried blood spots identifies Zellweger spectrum disorders (e.g., X-linked adrenoleukodystrophy) early and can prevent disease onset.

## Neuro- and cognitive roles
- Glycerophosphocholine (GPC) in kidney has osmoprotective properties.
- Available as a pharmaceutical in some countries.
- Claimed benefits: cognitive dysfunction, cardiometabolic disorders, anti-aging.

## Key message
Lysophosphatidylcholine is a bioactive lipid with multiple sources and diverse functions. Its levels are tightly controlled, and both excess and deficiency can have important physiological and pathological consequences.
