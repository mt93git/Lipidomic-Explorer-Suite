# PHOSPHATIDIC ACID (PA)

## The central intermediate and lipid mediator
Phosphatidic acid (1,2-diacyl-sn-glycero-3-phosphate) is a key intermediate in the biosynthesis of glycerophospholipids and triacylglycerols, and a precursor of signalling molecules. Although present at low levels in cells (often < picomolar), it modulates membrane dynamics, protein functions and many signalling and cellular processes.

## Key features
- Simplest diacyl-glycerophospholipid with a phosphomonoester head group (anionic lipid, net −1 charge at physiological pH).
- Not an abundant lipid but essential as a biosynthetic intermediate and potent lipid mediator.

## 1C. Enzymes and Organelles in Mammals

### Step 1: GPAT (acylation of sn-1)
Glycerol-3-phosphate acyltransferases (GPATs)

Diagram labels: Location; Mitochondrial outer membrane; Endoplasmic reticulum; GPAT1; GPAT2; GPAT3; GPAT4

Key points
- GPAT1: highly expressed in liver & adipose tissue; regulated by SREBP-1 (feeding status).
- GPAT3 & 4: required for TAG storage; regulated by CHP1 for stability, activity and lipid droplet location.
- GPAT4: major contributor to LPA synthesis in liver and brown adipose tissue

### Step 2: LPLAT (acylation of sn-2)
Acyl-CoA:lysophosphatidic acid acyltransferases (LPLATs)

Diagram labels: Endoplasmic reticulum; Mitochondrial outer membrane; LPLAT1; LPLAT2; LPLAT3; LPLAT4; LPLAT5

Key points
- LPLAT1: prefers 14:0-, 16:0-, 18:2-CoAs
- LPLAT2: prefers 20:4-CoA
- LPLAT3: yields 22:6(n-3)-containing PA (retina, testes)
- LPLAT4 & 5: prefer 18:1-CoA & PUFA-CoAs; roles in synthesis & remodelling (Lands' cycle)

Biosynthesis occurs in both ER and mitochondria.
Traffic of PA between organelles enables remodelling and synthesis of other lipids.

## 1D. Functions of Phosphatidic Acid
- Membrane structure & dynamics: Affects curvature, thickness and fluidity
- Protein interactions: Binds proteins to modulate activity, conformation
- Vesicular trafficking: Regulates budding, fusion, tethering and fission
- Signalling lipid: Precursor of lysophosphatidic acid (LPA) and other mediators
- Enzyme modulation: Modulates kinases, phosphatases and other enzymes

## 1-acyl metabolite: Lysophosphatidic acid (LPA)
A potent signalling molecule acting through specific G protein–coupled receptors.

### Plants (chloroplast & ER)

### Other organisms
- Yeast fungi: Kennedy pathway in ER; majority of PA for glycerophospholipids synthesized in mitochondria.
- Bacteria: Kennedy pathway on cytoplasmic membrane; some synthesize PA from acyl-phosphate via acyl-phosphate:diacylglycerol acyltransferase (PlsY).

## 1G. Physiological and Pathophysiological Relevance

### Physiological roles
- Cell growth and proliferation
- Membrane biogenesis
- Energy metabolism and lipid storage
- Stress responses
- Signal transduction

### Associated with dysregulation
- Insulin resistance, obesity, NAFLD
- Cardiovascular disease
- Neurodegenerative disorders
- Cancer (tumor growth, invasion, metastasis)

### Analytical note
PA is easily generated by enzymatic hydrolysis during inappropriate sample handling; accurate quantification requires careful extraction and storage conditions.
