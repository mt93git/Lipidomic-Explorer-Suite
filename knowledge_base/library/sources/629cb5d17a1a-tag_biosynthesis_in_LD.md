# 2.3. TRIACYLGLYCEROL METABOLISM IN ADIPOCYTES AND LIPID DROPLETS

## (untitled panel)
Adipose tissue and the adipocytes are characterized by accumulations of triacylglycerols, which act as the main energy store for animals while cushioning and insulating the body. Triacylglycerols stored when there is a surplus of nutrients are mobilized for energy production during starvation, but adipose tissue serves also as a reservoir of bioactive lipids and when required provides structural components, including fatty acids, cholesterol and retinol, for membrane synthesis and repair. By buffering against fatty acid accumulation that might exceed their capacity, non-adipose cells defend themselves against lipotoxicity while providing a rapid source of energy and essential metabolites by sensing and responding rapidly to changes in the systemic energy balance. Dysfunction contributes to numerous diseases, including hepatic steatosis, lipodystrophy, and cardiovascular, intestinal and infectious diseases. Cytosolic lipid droplets and their associated metabolic processes are found in most eukaryotic cells, including those of Drosophila melanogaster, in the plastids of plants and in the cytoplasm of some prokaryotes.

## 1. LIPID DROPLET STRUCTURE – A DYNAMIC ORGANELLE
Diagram labels: Surface monolayer; Core (hydrophobic); Triacylglycerols (TAG); Cholesterol esters; Other neutral lipids; Associated proteins

- Phospholipids (mainly PC, PE)
- Free cholesterol

Associated proteins:

- Perilipins (PLIN1–5)
- ADRP (adipophilin)
- Caveolins, CIDE proteins
- Acyltransferases, lipases
- Other metabolic enzymes

- Derived from the cytosolic leaflet of the ER membrane.
- Size: up to ~200 µm in adipocytes; ~50 nm in other cell types.
- Lipid droplets are now recognized as complex organelles with rich metabolism.
- Functions: store energy; supply fatty acids; detoxify harmful lipids; coordinate signalling and crosstalk with multiple organ systems.

## 3. TRIACYLGLYCEROL LIPOLYSIS (Mobilization of stored fat)
### 1. Activation
Hormonal signals

- Epinephrine (β-adrenergic)
- Glucagon
- Atrial natriuretic peptide

- Receptor → Gs protein
  - → Adenylyl cyclase
  - → ↑ cAMP
  - → PKA activation

### 2. Perilipin phosphorylation
Diagram labels: PKA; P

PKA phosphorylates perilipins (PLIN1) → conformational change → releases CGI-58 and lipases

### 3. Lipase action
- ATGL + CGI-58 intiiates: TAG → DAG + FA
- HSL continues: DAG → MAG + FA
- MGL completes: MAG → Glycerol + FA

### 4. Products released
- Free fatty acids (bound to FABP)
- Glycerol (to liver via blood)
- Transport to tissues for β-oxidation or re-esterification

## Regulation
### Stimulate lipolysis
- Fasting, exercise, cold exposure
- Catecholamines, glucagon, natriuretic peptides
- Low insulin

### Inhibit lipolysis
- Insulin
- High nutrient availability

Lipolysis provides rapid energy and substrates for gluconeogenesis and lipid synthesis.

## 4. ADIPOSE TISSUE TYPES AND SPECIAL FEATURES
### White adipose tissue (WAT)
- Unilocular lipid droplet (large single droplet)
- Main energy storage depot
- Endocrine organ – secretes adipokines (leptin, adiponectin, resistin, etc.)

### Brown adipose tissue (BAT)
- Multilocular droplets, many mitochondria
- UCP1 (uncoupling protein 1) – thermogenesis
- Important in neonates; maintained in adults

### Beige (brite) adipose tissue
- Inducible from white fat by cold, exercise, β3-adrenergic stimulation, irisin, etc.
- Thermogenic – similar to brown fat

### Bone marrow adipocytes
Bone marrow adipocytes – occupy ~70% of marrow space; regulate hematopoiesis and bone metabolism.

## KEY TAKE-HOME MESSAGES
- Lipid droplets are dynamic organelles, not inert fat blobs.
- They store energy as TAGs and sequester potentially harmful lipids.
- Assembly occurs in ER subdomains via coordinated enzymes, seipin and protein machinery.
- Lipolysis is tightly regulated by hormones and occurs mainly at the droplet surface.
- Adipose tissue communicates with multiple organs via adipokines and lipid mediators.
- Dysregulation of storage or lipolysis contributes to metabolic diseases (obesity, diabetes, fatty liver, CVD).
- Brown and beige fat provide thermogenic capacity.
- Lipid droplets exist in most eukaryotic cells, plants and some prokaryotes.
