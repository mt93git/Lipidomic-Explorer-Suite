# 6. CARNITINE, β-OXIDATION AND HEALTH

## (untitled panel)
Carnitine shuttles acyl groups for β-oxidation and removes excess acyl-CoAs. Defects in this system cause toxic acyl-CoA accumulation and disease. Acylcarnitine profiling in blood/urine is a powerful diagnostic tool and has implications for many disorders and therapeutic strategies.

## 6.1 ROLE OF CARNITINE AND ACYLCARNITINES
- Carnitine transports fatty acyl groups into mitochondria for β-oxidation and removes surplus acyl groups from mitochondria and peroxisomes.
- Maintains balance of acyl-CoA/CoA ratio and prevents accumulation of toxic acyl-CoAs.
- Acylcarnitines with highly polyunsaturated acyl chains are less cardiotoxic than saturated/monounsaturated analogues.
- Plasma acylation state of carnitine reflects cytosolic acylcarnitine pool → marker of acyl-CoA/acylcarnitine equilibrium.
- Alterations in acylcarnitine profiles can affect the tissue lipidome.

## ACYLCARNITINES AS BIOMARKERS
- Accumulation of unusual acylcarnitines indicates specific defects in β-oxidation.
- Chain length and degree of unsaturation help identify the enzymic block.
- Used in newborn screening and diagnosis of metabolic diseases.

## 6.2 SELECTED INHERITED DISORDERS OF β-OXIDATION AND CARNITINE METABOLISM
| Disorder (defective step) | Accumulated acylcarnitines | Typical features | Inheritance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary Carnitine Deficiency (OCTN2 transporter defect) | Free carnitine ↓ | Hypoketotic hypoglycemia, cardiomyopathy, muscle weakness, infertility | Autosomal recessive |
| Medium-Chain Acyl-CoA Dehydrogenase Deficiency (MCAD) | C6, C8, C10:1 medium-chain | Hypoketotic hypoglycemia, vomiting, lethargy, seizures; triggered by fasting/illness | Autosomal recessive |
| Very-Long-Chain Acyl-CoA Dehydrogenase Deficiency (VLCAD) | C14:1, C14, C16:1 long-chain | Cardiomyopathy, muscle weakness, rhabdomyolysis, hypoketotic hypoglycemia | Autosomal recessive |
| Carnitine Palmitoyltransferase II Deficiency (CPT II) | C16, C18, OH-C16, OH-C18 | Muscle cramps, myoglobinuria, exercise intolerance, cardiomyopathy | Autosomal recessive |
| Multiple Acyl-CoA Dehydrogenase Deficiency (MADD/GA II) | Multiple acylcarnitines (broad profile) | Hypoketotic hypoglycemia, lactic acidosis, neurodevelopmental defects | Autosomal recessive |
| Peroxisomal Biogenesis Disorders (e.g., Zellweger, X-linked ALD) | VLC acylcarnitines, pristanic, phytanic | Hypotonia, seizures, organomegaly, neurologic degeneration | Autosomal recessive or X-linked |

>30 inherited metabolic diseases detectable by acylcarnitine profiling in newborns. Overall incidence ~1 in 10,000 live births.

## 6.3 CLINICAL AND PATHOPHYSIOLOGICAL IMPACT
- Brain uses ketone bodies as major energy source during fasting. Acylcarnitine abnormalities associated with encephalopathy and neurodevelopmental issues.
- Cardiac and skeletal muscle rely on fatty acid oxidation; defects cause cardiomyopathy, arrhythmias, exercise intolerance.
- Liver: impaired β-oxidation → hypoketotic hypoglycaemia, fatty liver; contributes to MAFLD and metabolic syndrome.
- Acylcarnitines detected in obesity, type 2 diabetes, CVD, diseases of the eye.
- Lipotoxicity from accumulated acyl species → inflammation, insulin resistance, organ dysfunction.
- Gut microbiota converts carnitine → TMA → TMAO, associated with atherosclerosis, MI and stroke risk.

Dysregulation of CPT1 and carnitine cycle implicated in cancer, ferroptosis, and many cardiometabolic disorders.

## 6.4 HEALTH BENEFITS AND NEUROPROTECTION
- L-Acetyl-carnitine crosses the BBB. Modulates acetylcholine, serotonin, dopamine and neurotrophic factors.
- Marketed for neuropathic pain.
- Evidence for benefit in cognitive and mood disorders; may help in early Alzheimer's disease.
- Supports lipid metabolism in brain (membrane synthesis, signalling).
- May protect against traumatic brain injury and neurodegeneration.
- Elevated palmitoyl-carnitine in aging mice → mitochondrial dysfunction.

## POTENTIAL MECHANISMS
- Antioxidant and anti-inflammatory actions
- Epigenetic effects (histone acetylation)
- Mitochondrial biogenesis and function
- Regulation of membrane lipid composition

## 6.5 CARNITINE AND ACYLCARNITINE SUPPLEMENTATION
Common Supplements and Functions

| Compound | Key functions | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| L-Carnitine (levocarnitine) | Supports fatty acid oxidation, energy production, removes excess acyl groups | Essential in rare deficiencies; may help in angina, arrhythmias, dialysis patients, liver and heart disease |
| Acetyl-L-carnitine | Energy metabolism, neurotransmitter modulation, acetate donor | Better absorbed; crosses BBB readily; may support cognition, mood, neuropathic pain |
| Propionyl-L-carnitine | Anaplerotic (propionyl-CoA → succinyl-CoA); supports energy | Potential benefit in vascular disease, peripheral neuropathy |
| Octanoyl-L-carnitine | Shuttles medium-chain acyl groups; energy | Shown beneficial in models of long-chain FAO disorders |

- Useful as adjunct therapy in selected patients (cardiac, metabolic liver disease, dialysis, some neuropathies).
- Important and lifesaving in genetic carnitine or FAO disorders.
- Potential benefits for exercise performance and recovery (evidence mixed).
- High doses may cause GI discomfort, fishy body odour, or seizures in predisposed individuals.

## 6.6 KEY TAKE-HOME MESSAGES
- Carnitine is essential for mitochondrial and peroxisomal β-oxidation and removal of excess acyl groups.
- Acylcarnitine profiling is a sensitive diagnostic tool for inherited metabolic diseases and other disorders.
- Defects in carnitine transport or β-oxidation lead to toxic metabolite accumulation and organ dysfunction.
- Ketone bodies are vital alternative fuels during fasting and in disease, with signalling roles beyond energy.
- Peroxisomal β-oxidation handles VLC, branched and dicarboxylic fatty acids and protects against lipid accumulation.
- Carnitine and acylcarnitine supplementation may benefit certain clinical conditions and support brain and heart health, but therapy should be individualized.
