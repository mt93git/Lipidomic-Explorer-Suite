# 2. BIOCHEMISTRY AND FUNCTION OF BIS(MONOACYLGLYCERO)PHOSPHATE (BMP)

## A. BIOSYNTHESIS
Primary precursor is phosphatidylglycerol (PG). The fatty acids are exchanged during biosynthesis. The exact mechanism and the order of reactions remain under investigation.

## (i) Proposed model 1: in the endosomal/lysosomal system
Diagram labels: Phosphatidylglycerol (PG); Lysophosphatidylglycerol (LPG); R,S-bis(monoacylglycero) phosphate (R,S-BMP); S,S-bis(monoacylglycero) phosphate (S,S-BMP); Lysosomal PLA2; removes sn-2 acyl (rate-limiting); Transacylase (CLN5); + acyl donor (1-monoacylglycerol?) energy-independent; Lysosomal lipase(s); removes sn-1 acyl; PLD3/4 (PLD); + 1-monoacylglycerol exchange at sn-1; sn-1; sn-2; sn-3

- CLN5 knockout cells show marked loss of BMP and accumulation of LPG in lysosomes.
- Products: (R,S)-BMP first, then stereo-inversion at sn-1 → (S,S)-BMP (major in lysosomes).

## (ii) Proposed model 2: ER → endolysosomal pathway (alternative)
Diagram labels: Phosphatidylglycerol (PG); Lysophosphatidylglycerol (R,S-LPG); Lysonosphatidylglycerol (S,S-LPG); S,S-bis(monoacylglycero) phosphate (S,S-BMP); PLA2; Acyltransferase (CLN8) (in ER); cyclic intermediate (stereo-inversion); Transacylase (CLN5) or other enzyme (in endolysosome); sn-1; sn-2; sn-3

- Deletion of CLN8 → no BMP detected.
- Two-step pathway via stereo-inversion of LPG, or direct formation of S,S-BMP by transacylation after inversion.

## (iii) Isomerization between 2,2′- and 3,3′-diacyl-BMP
Diagram labels: 2,2′-diacyl-BMP (proposed as native form by many researchers); Acidic (slow) or alkaline (faster) conditions (in vitro); 2,3′-intermediate; favoured at pH ≥ 7; 3,3′-diacyl-BMP (thermodynamically more stable)

Proteins, metal ions, nucleotides and pH (most important in vivo) are proposed to regulate isomerization. This may be the first step in catabolism by phospholipase A2.

## C. OCCURRENCE
- Ubiquitous in animal tissues (~1–2% of total phospholipids).
- Highly enriched in lysosomal membranes: 15–70% of lysosomal phospholipids depending on tissue/cell type (e.g., ~11% of total in macrophage/microglial cell lines).
- Trace levels in plasma: ~40% in lipoprotein fractions and ~60% in lipoprotein-deficient compartment.
- Not found in plants.
- Reported in some bacteria (e.g., Agrobacterium tumefaciens, some alkalophilic Bacillus sp.), but stereochemistry uncertain.

## Endo-lysosomal pathway (BMP enrichment)

## Notable facts
- Marker lipid of lysosomes.
- Only lipid (of >1200 studied) that accumulates in tissues of mice and humans during aging.

## Key message
Bis(monoacylglycero)phosphate (BMP) is a unique animal phospholipid with distinctive stereochemistry and fatty acid compositions, highly enriched in lysosomal and late endosomal membranes. It promotes membrane curvature, vesicle formation and enzyme function, and may serve as a signalling lipid when hydrolysed to LPA.

## B. PHYSICAL AND CHEMICAL PROPERTIES
Molecular shape and membrane behaviour
- Cone-shaped molecule with a small hydrated, negatively charged head group.
- Strongly non-bilayer forming → promotes membrane curvature, fusion and inward budding (invagination).
- Enriched in late endosomes and lysosomes → aids formation of intraluminal vesicles and multi-vesicular bodies.

Diagram labels: Bilayer membrane; + BMP; Inward budding / vesicle formation

## Enzymatic stability
- Unique stereochemistry → resistant to most phospholipases.
- Protects lysosomal membranes from self-digestion.
- Fatty acyl chains can turn over by transacylation, but glycerophosphate backbone is stable.
- Putative catabolism under acidic conditions via PLA2-like enzymes (identity and regulation not fully established).
- Stereochemistry may help evade immunorecognition by antibodies.

## Parameters favouring BMP function in lysosomes
- Acidic pH (4.6–5.0)
- High content of unsaturated long-chain fatty acids
- Association with positively charged lysosomal proteins (hydrolases, coactivators, etc.)

Together these factors promote membrane dynamics, enzyme function and vesicle formation in the endo-lysosomal system.

## Comparative stability
- S,S-(2,2′-diacyl)-BMP is more tightly packed but less thermodynamically stable than S,S-(3,3′-diacyl)-BMP.
- 3,3′-BMP is more rapidly degraded by lipases than 2,2′-BMP.

## Functions (established and proposed)
- Structural lipid of lysosomal and late endosomal membranes.
- Essential for development of intraluminal vesicles and multi-vesicular bodies.
- Modulates activity and localization of lysosomal enzymes.
- Involved in lipid trafficking, membrane repair and exosome biogenesis.
- May act as a signalling lipid after hydrolysis to lysophosphatidic acid.

## Exosomes
BMP is enriched in exosomal membranes.
- Released by most cells.
- Small vesicles (<200 nm) that mediate cell–cell communication.
- Carry lipids, proteins, nucleic acids and bioactive molecules.

## D. BIOLOGICAL SIGNIFICANCE
Membrane dynamics and trafficking
- Promotes curvature, budding and fusion events.
- Essential for endosome maturation and intra-luminal vesicle formation.

Enzyme function
- Enhances optimal activity and fusion of lysosomal hydrolases.
- May influence enzyme localization and cofactor binding in acidic lumen.

Exosome biogenesis and secretion
- Enriched in exosomal membranes.
- Important for vesicle formation and cargo sorting.

Signalling potential
- Hydrolysis → lysophosphatidic acid (LPA), a bioactive signalling lipid.
- Potential roles in cell communication and inflammation.

## E. KEY POINTS
- BMP is a unique stereochemical phospholipid of animals.
- Main structural lipid of lysosomes and late endosomes.
- Highly enriched in these membranes.
- Forms non-bilayer structures that enable vesicle budding and membrane fusion.
- Resistant to most phospholipases → protects lysosomal membranes.
- Involved in lipid trafficking, enzyme function and exosome biogenesis.
- Distinct fatty acid compositions are tissue- and organelle-specific.
- New therapeutic potential is emerging in lysosomal storage and inflammatory diseases.

## Outlook
Understanding BMP biosynthesis and function may lead to new interventions for lysosomal storage disorders, neurodegeneration and inflammatory diseases.
