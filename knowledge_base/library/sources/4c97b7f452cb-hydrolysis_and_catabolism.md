# 3. SPHINGOMYELIN HYDROLYSIS AND CATABOLISM

## A. INTESTINAL DIGESTION OF DIETARY SPHINGOMYELIN
- Most dietary SM is hydrolysed at the intestinal brush border.
- Alkaline SMase (optimum pH 8.5–9) → ceramide.
- Neutral ceramidase → free fatty acid + sphingosine.
- Enzymes are also secreted in bile from liver.
- Bile salts aid hydrolysis of SM and other phospholipids.

Diagram labels: Dietary sphingomyelin (SM); ceramide; Ceramide; Phosphocholine (P-Cho); Neutral ceramidase (pH ~7); Sphingosine; Fatty acid (R-COOH); Intestinal brush border (lumenal side)

Absorption and fate:
- Sphingosine is absorbed into enterocytes.
- Partly re-N-acylated → ceramide → complex sphingolipids.
- Remainder → sphingosine-1-phosphate → palmitic acid (major product) → esterified into TAG of chylomicrons.
- Some intermediates act as anti-inflammatory signalling mediators.

## Ectoenzyme alkaline SMase
- Member of NPP family (ecto-nucleotidepyrophosphatase–phosphodiesterase).
- Crosses plasma membrane via N- or C-terminal domain; mainly extracellular.
- Also acts as phospholipase C on PAF and lysophosphatidylcholine → anti-inflammatory effects.

## B. CATABOLISM IN OTHER TISSUES – SPHINGOMYELINASES

| Enzyme (type) | Optimum pH | Location | Key features and roles |
|---|---|---|---|
| Acid SMase (lysosomal) | ~5 | Endo-lysosomes (→ plasma membrane upon stimulation) | Zn2+-dependent; contains built-in N-terminal saposin domain. House-keeping enzyme: membrane turnover, lipoprotein remodelling. Stimulated → traffics to plasma membrane; generates ceramide for signalling (apoptosis, stress responses, cilia formation). Broad phospholipase C activity in vitro (e.g., ceramide-1-phosphate, glyco-, glycerophospholipids, BMP). |
| Secreted acid SMase | ~5–7 | Extracellular (plasma membrane outer leaflet; e.g., endothelial cells) | Same gene as lysosomal form; different glycosylation. Zn2+-dependent; participates in signalling and SM turnover. |
| Neutral SMases (NSM 1–4) | ~7.4 | NSM-1: ER, nucleus; NSM-2: Golgi, plasma membrane; NSM-3: Plasma membrane (bone, cartilage); MA-NSM (NSM-4): Mitochondria | NSM-1: Broad substrate specificity; signalling role appears limited. NSM-2: Activated by phosphatidylserine. Major driver of ceramide regulation in plasma membrane. Critical for ceramide homeostasis and signalling (esp. brain). NSM-3: Essential for mineralization; also in striated & cardiac muscle. MA-NSM: Generates ceramide; precise function less well understood. |
| Bacterial SMases | Varies | Extracellular | Many lyse host RBCs. Pseudomonas aeruginosa SMase can act as a SM synthase in vitro. |

## Clinical significance
Targeting SMases (inhibitors or activators) is being explored for cancer therapy, inflammatory diseases, neurodegenerative and viral infections. Lifestyle factors and diet can influence sphingolipid metabolism and disease risk.

## C. OVERALL HYDROLYSIS OF SPHINGOMYELIN
Diagram labels: Sphingomyelinases (phosphodiesterases); Sphingomyelin; H2O; Sphingomyelinase (asmase / nSMase); Ceramide; Phosphocholine (P-Cho)

Phosphocholine is a valuable nutrient source in cells.

## Major functions of sphingomyelinases
- Generate ceramides → powerful lipid second messengers.
- Ceramides → sphingosine → sphingosine-1-phosphate: regulates proliferation, apoptosis, inflammation, metabolism, etc.
- Enzyme location determines site and nature of signalling.

## D. ACID SPHINGOMYELINASE AND DISEASE
Deficiency of acid sphingomyelinase → Niemann–Pick disease.

| Type | Cause | Major features |
|---|---|---|
| A | Severe deficiency (infants) | Massive accumulation of SM in lysosomes → foam cells, organomegaly, neurodegeneration, early death. |
| B | Partial deficiency (children/adults) | Hepatosplenomegaly, pulmonary disease, neurological symptoms. |
| C | Defects in NPC1 or NPC2 proteins | Impaired cholesterol transport; SM accumulation; variable phenotypes. |

↑ SM → ↑ cholesterol; lack of ceramide may contribute to pathology. SM degradation is important for cholesterol homeostasis.

## E. REGULATORS AND ACTIVATORS
Mediators that activate SMases / induce ceramide production
- Chemotherapeutic agents
- Tumour necrosis factor-α (TNF-α)
- 1,25-dihydroxy-vitamin D3
- Endotoxin (LPS)
- Gamma-interferon (IFN-γ)
- Interleukins (ILs)
- Nerve growth factor (NGF)
- Oxidative stress
- Ionizing irradiation
- Other cellular stress conditions

Consequences: apoptosis, growth arrest, differentiation, inflammation, immune responses, autophagy, membrane remodelling, etc.

## E. REGULATION OF BIOSYNTHESIS AND TURNOVER
Regulation of synthesis
- CERT transfers ceramide from ER → trans Golgi (ATP-dependent, non-vesicular).
- DAG produced by SMS activates PKD → phosphorylates CERT → reduces ceramide flux to Golgi.
- Phosphoinositides and OSBP1 (oxysterol-binding protein 1) also inhibit CERT → repress SM synthesis.
- SMS1 (Golgi) and SMS2 (plasma membrane) have phospholipase C activity in absence of ceramide → regulate PC and DAG levels.

Regulation of hydrolysis
- SMases are stimulated by stress signals (listed below).
- Acid SMase translocation to plasma membrane is tightly controlled.
- SM hydrolysis is balanced by use of ceramide in glycosphingolipid synthesis and by SMS2 (plasma membrane), which reconverts ceramide to SM.

## F. PHYSIOLOGICAL AND PATHOPHYSIOLOGICAL ROLES
- Maintain membrane integrity, rigidity and barrier function.
- Essential for myelin sheath formation and neuronal function.
- Organise lipid rafts with cholesterol and proteins → signalling platforms.
- Participate in lipoprotein structure and cholesterol transport.
- Dysregulation linked to cancer, inflammatory diseases, atherosclerosis, Niemann–Pick disease, metabolic disorders, neurodegeneration, viral and bacterial infections.

## G. KEY POINTS SUMMARY
- Dietary SM is hydrolysed by alkaline SMase at intestinal brush border.
- In tissues, SMases hydrolyse SM → ceramide + phosphocholine.
- Acid SMase (lysosomal / secreted) and neutral SMases act in different compartments with distinct roles.
- Ceramide generation is central to signalling: apoptosis, stress responses, inflammation, metabolism and more.
- SMases also regulate membrane composition and cholesterol homeostasis.
- Alterations in SM metabolism are associated with many human diseases.

## H. KEY ENZYME FAMILIES
- Acid sphingomyelinase: Zn2+-dependent hydrolase; has built-in saposin domain.
- Secreted acid SMase: extracellular Zn2+-dependent; operates at neutral pH.
- Neutral SMases (1–4): Mg2+-dependent phosphodiesterases in ER, Golgi, plasma membrane and mitochondria.
- Alkaline SMase (intestinal): member of NPP family (ectoenzyme).
