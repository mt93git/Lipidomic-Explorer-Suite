# 3. β-OXIDATION OF FATTY ACIDS IN MITOCHONDRIA

## (untitled panel)
Mitochondrial β-oxidation is the principal pathway for fatty acid catabolism, producing acetyl-CoA, NADH and FADH2 for energy. It is especially important during fasting and in energy-tissues such as liver, heart and skeletal muscle. The liver can convert acetyl-CoA to ketone bodies to fuel extrahepatic tissues, including the brain.

## OVERVIEW
- Main pathway for degradation of fatty acids.
- Occurs in mitochondrial matrix (after transport via carnitine shuttle).
- Repeats cycles of four reactions → progressive removal of two-carbon units as acetyl-CoA.
- Present in all eukaryotes and most prokaryotes.
- Many isoenzymes adapt to different chain lengths and substrates.
- Long-chain fatty acids (>C8) use carnitine-dependent transport; short/medium chains can cross membrane without carnitine.

## Figure 2. β-Oxidation of a long-chain saturated fatty acyl-CoA (example: palmitoyl-CoA, C16:0)
Diagram labels: Start: Palmitoyl-CoA (C16:0); 1 Acyl-CoA dehydrogenase; FAD; FADH2; trans-Δ2-Enoyl-CoA (C16:1-Δ2); 2 Enoyl-CoA hydratase; + H2O; L-3-Hydroxyacyl-CoA (3-hydroxypalmitoyl-CoA); 3 3-Hydroxyacyl-CoA dehydrogenase; NAD+; NADH + H+; 3-Ketoacyl-CoA (3-oxopalmitoyl-CoA); 4 3-Ketoacyl-CoA thiolase; + CoA-SH; Products of 1st cycle; Acetyl-CoA; Acyl-CoA (C16:0); Cycle repeats (n/2 − 1) times until two acetyl-CoA remain (for even-chain fatty acids).

Net result per cycle: 1 FADH2 + 1 NADH + 1 Acetyl-CoA + Acyl-CoA shortened by 2 carbons.

## KEY POINTS
- Each cycle shortens the acyl chain by 2 carbons.
- FADH2 and NADH feed the electron transport chain → ATP.
- Final products (even-chain): multiple acetyl-CoA.
- Odd-chain fatty acids yield propionyl-CoA in final cycle → converted to succinyl-CoA.
- Enzymes 2–4 are assembled in a trifunctional enzyme complex on the inner mitochondrial membrane.

## ENERGY YIELD (example: palmitate, C16:0)
- 7 cycles → 7 FADH2 + 7 NADH + 8 Acetyl-CoA
- TCA yield per acetyl-CoA: 3 NADH + 1 FADH2 + 1 GTP (≈10 ATP equivalents)
- Total ≈ 129–131 ATP per palmitate
- Fatty acids yield ~2× more energy than glucose (39 kJ g−1 vs 15 kJ g−1).

## REGULATION
- Product inhibition: each enzyme inhibited by its own acyl-CoA product.
- High NADH/NAD+ or acetyl-CoA/CoA inhibits β-oxidation.
- Malonyl-CoA inhibits CPT I → blocks entry of fatty acyl-CoA into mitochondria.
- Transcriptional control: PPARs + coactivator PGC-1α (with RXR) ↑ expression of genes for β-oxidation enzymes.

Delivery of electrons: FADH2 → ETF → CoQ ; NADH → Complex I → → ATP via oxidative phosphorylation.

Acetyl-CoA fates: (1) Enters TCA cycle for energy or (2) Converted to acetylcarnitine (by carnitine acetyltransferase) for export from mitochondria.

## PHYSIOLOGICAL AND CLINICAL NOTES
- Carnitine maintains acyl-CoA/CoA balance and removes excess acyl groups.
- Defects in carnitine transport or β-oxidation enzymes cause hypoketotic hypoglycemia, cardiomyopathy, muscle weakness, rhabdomyolysis, exercise intolerance.
- Acylcarnitine profiling in plasma/tissues is a key diagnostic tool for inborn errors of metabolism.
- Carnitine and acylcarnitines also export products from peroxisomes (e.g., short-chain acyl groups).
