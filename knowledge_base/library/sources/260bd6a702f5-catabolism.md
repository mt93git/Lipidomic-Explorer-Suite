# 5. CATABOLISM OF OLIGOGLYCOSYLCERAMIDES IN ANIMALS AND GENETIC DISEASE

## (untitled panel)
Oligoglycosphingolipids are degraded in lysosomes within the cell by water-soluble hydrolases, aided by activator proteins (saposins) and anionic lipids such as bis(monoacylglycero)phosphate (BMP). Sections of plasma membrane containing glycosphingolipids are endocytosed and transported to lysosomes where exohydrolases cleave sugar residues sequentially from the non-reducing end. The cleaved fragments (sugars and ceramides) leave the lysosome and are either degraded further or re-enter biosynthetic pathways. In certain bacteria and leeches, a ceramide glycanase can hydrolyse the β-glucosidic bond between glucose and ceramide, yielding ceramide and an oligosaccharide.

## KEY POINTS
- Degradation occurs in lysosomes by sequential action of exohydrolases from the non-reducing end.
- Activator proteins (saposins) and anionic lipids (e.g., BMP) are required for optimal enzyme activity.
- Cleaved sugars and ceramides exit the lysosome for further metabolism or recycling.
- Alternative ceramide glycanase pathway cleaves the β-glucosidic bond to give ceramide + oligosaccharide.

## C. FABRY'S DISEASE (α-GALACTOSIDASE A DEFICIENCY)
Diagram labels: Normal pathway; Gb3 (globotriaosylceramide); α-Galactosidase A removes terminal Gal; In Fabry disease; α-Galactosidase A absent/deficient; Cer

Accumulation of Gb3, galabiosylceramide and lactosylceramide in lysosomes of kidney, heart, brain and other tissues

Diagnosis
- Elevated Gb3 in urine or lyso-Gb3 in serum

Treatment
Enzyme replacement therapy (agalsidase α or β) improves outcomes.

Clinical features
- Renal failure (proteinuria)
- Cardiomyopathy
- Cerebrovascular disease (stroke)
- Neuropathic pain, angiokeratomas
- Corneal opacities, hearing loss
- Reduced life expectancy
