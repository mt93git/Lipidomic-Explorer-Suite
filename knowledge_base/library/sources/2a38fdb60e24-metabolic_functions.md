# 4. METABOLIC FUNCTIONS OF SPHINGOMYELIN

## A. OTHER FUNCTIONS OF SPHINGOMYELIN
- Inhibits phospholipase A2α → ↓ eicosanoid biosynthesis.
- Required for transferrin internalization → iron uptake.
- Essential for many membrane proteins: ion channels, receptors, transporters.
- Most abundant sphingolipid in the nucleus → involved in chromatin assembly, dynamics and nuclear matrix.
- C18-acyl sphingomyelin binds coat protein p24 → formation of membrane vesicles.
- Acts as receptor for actinoporins (pore-forming toxins from sea anemones).
- Accumulates during brown adipocyte differentiation → regulates UCP1-mediated non-shivering thermogenesis.
- Amyloid β (Aβ) binds SM → promotes Aβ aggregation (implicated in Alzheimer's disease).
- Ganglioside GM1 may protect against Aβ aggregation.

## B. THE SPHINGOMYELIN (OR CERAMIDE) CYCLE
Diagram labels: SPHINGOMYELIN (SM); Ceramide synthases (reverse reaction); SMS (1/2) (uses PC) Golgi / Plasma membrane; Sphingomyelinases (acid, neutral, alkaline); CERAMIDE (Cer); PHOSPHORYLCHOLINE (P-Cho); Phosphodiesterases (reverse reaction); LONG-CHAIN BASES (sphingosine, sphinganine); CERAMIDE-1-PHOSPHATE (Cer-1-P); SPHINGOSINE-1-PHOSPHATE (S1P)
- Precursors of S1P and other lipids.
- Potent lipid second messenger.

Key points
- SM is a central hub → source of ceramide and other signalling sphingolipids.
- Reactions are reversible and compartment-specific (Golgi, plasma membrane, endosomes/lysosomes, nucleus).
- Ceramides generated form distinct membrane domains and trigger diverse cellular responses.

## C. BIOLOGICAL CONSEQUENCES OF SM HYDROLYSIS
Diagram labels: SM-rich membrane (ordered domain); After SM hydrolysis → ceramide generation; Sphingomyelin; Sphingomyelinase; Ceramide; Cholesterol; Other phospholipids; Protein
- Ceramide-rich domains exclude cholesterol.
- Recruits/excludes specific proteins → alters membrane signalling platforms.
- Triggers: apoptosis, cell cycle arrest, stress responses, differentiation.
- Influences cancer, neurodegeneration, inflammation and more.

## D. SM AND DIACYLGLYCEROL (DAG) CROSS-TALK
SM synthesis reaction
Ceramide + Phosphatidylcholine → (SMS) Sphingomyelin + DAG

Roles of DAG
- Activates protein kinase D (PKD).
- Pro-survival and mitogenic mediator.
- Competitor to ceramide-induced apoptosis.
- Links glycerolipid and sphingolipid signalling pathways.

DAG provides feedback regulation of SM synthesis and participates in many metabolic and signalling pathways.

## E. SPHINGOMYELINASES – TYPES, LOCATION AND ROLES

| Enzyme (type) | Optimum pH | Location | Key features | Major roles / Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Alkaline SMase (intestinal) | 8.5–9.0 | Intestinal brush border (luminal side). Also in bile (liver secretion) | Member of NPP family (ectoenzyme). Crosses membrane via N- or C-terminus. Acts as phospholipase C. | Hydrolyses dietary SM → ceramide. Limits cholesterol uptake. Anti-inflammatory effects. |
| Acid SMase (lysosomal) | ~5.0 | Endo-lysosomes. Stored intracellularly; translocates to plasma membrane upon stimulation | Zinc-dependent; built-in saposin domain. Broad substrate specificity (SM, PC, Cer-1-P, BMP, etc.). | Membrane turnover and remodelling. Generates ceramide for signalling (apoptosis, stress responses). Implicated in Niemann–Pick A/B disease. |
| Secreted acid SMase | 5–7 (works at neutral pH) | Extracellular space; outer leaflet of cell membrane | Derived from SMPD1 gene; different glycosylation. Zn2+-dependent. | Cellular signalling; endothelial function. Involved in inflammatory and metabolic processes. |
| Neutral SMases (NSM-1 to -4) | ~7.4 | ER, Golgi, plasma membrane; one in mitochondria | Mg2+-dependent. Distinct isoforms with diverse roles. | NSM-1 (ER/nucleus): limited signalling role. NSM-2 (Golgi/PM): key in ceramide synthesis; hormones secretion; brain. NSM-3 (plasma membrane): bone/cartilage mineralization. MA-NSM (mitochondria): precise role less understood. |
| Bacterial SMases | Varies | Extracellular | Lyse host RBCs (many pathogens). Some (e.g., P. aeruginosa) act as SM synthase in vitro. | Virulence factor; hijack host lipids. |

Phosphocholine (P-Cho) released from SM hydrolysis is an important nutrient and signalling molecule.

## F. SM IN HEALTH AND DISEASE

| Disease / condition | Role of sphingomyelin / metabolite |
|---|---|
| Cancer | High plasma SM (esp. 16:0-SM) → poor prognosis. Source of ceramide → apoptosis; SM synthase supports pro-survival signalling. |
| Cardiovascular disease | High plasma SM associated with atherosclerosis. May retain cholesterol in arterial wall; impairs reverse cholesterol transport. |
| Neurodegenerative diseases | Aβ–SM interaction promotes aggregation (Alzheimer's). Acid SMase activation → SM depletion → disrupted lipid–protein interactions and signalling. |
| Lysosomal storage disorders | Niemann–Pick A/B: acid SMase deficiency → SM accumulation, foam cells. Niemann–Pick C: NPC1/2 defects. |
| Inflammation & immune disorders | SMases modulate inflammatory signalling pathways. Dysregulation linked to IBD, sepsis, etc. |
| Infectious diseases | Chlamydiae acquire SM from host (essential for growth). Bacteria (P. aeruginosa, N. gonorrhoeae) hijack SM metabolism. Viruses (HIV, HCV) exploit host SM. |

Altered sphingomyelin levels or species composition are being explored as biomarkers for many diseases.

## G. NUTRITIONAL ASPECTS
- Dietary sources: egg, meat, dairy foods.
- Estimated intake (USA): 0.3–0.4 g/day per capita.
- Infants: present in milk; may support myelin formation and brain development (evidence from animal studies).
- Poorly absorbed intact; hydrolysed and catabolized.
- Dietary sphingolipids can:
  - Reduce intestinal absorption of cholesterol and other lipids.
  - Lower serum lipid concentrations.
  - Inhibit colon carcinogenesis; may alleviate IBD symptoms.

## H. KEY TAKEAWAY MESSAGE
Sphingomyelin is far more than a structural membrane lipid. It is a central metabolic hub connecting membrane organisation, lipid signalling, stress responses and disease. Through the actions of sphingomyelinases and biosynthetic enzymes, it regulates the balance between pro-survival and pro-apoptotic pathways and influences metabolism, immunity, infection and many chronic diseases.

## Summary
Sphingomyelin metabolism integrates membrane structure, lipid signalling and metabolic regulation. Its enzymes and metabolites are crucial determinants of health and disease.
