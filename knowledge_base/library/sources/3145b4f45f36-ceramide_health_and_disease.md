# 4. CERAMIDES IN HEALTH AND DISEASE

## (untitled panel)
Ceramides are bioactive lipid second messengers. Their effects depend on fatty acyl chain length, long-chain base composition, site of generation and duration. Long-chain ceramides are often harmful, while very-long-chain ceramides are frequently relatively benign or protective.

## A. MAJOR SOURCES AND MECHANISMS OF GENERATION
Rapid generation (seconds–minutes)
- Sphingomyelinases (SMases) activated by stress, cytokines, UV, drugs, etc.
  - Acid SMase (lysosomes / PM)
  - Neutral SMase (PM, Golgi, ER, mito.)
  - Alkaline SMase (PM, ER, Golgi)
- Ceramide synthases (de novo) (minutes–hours)

Diagram labels: Plasma membrane / ER / Golgi / Mitochondria; Sphingomyelinase (SMase); Ceramide; De novo synthesis (ER); Salvage / recycling (lysosomes); Complex sphingolipids (SM, GSLs); Degradation (ceramidases)

All stresses can activate ceramide production
- Oxidative stress
- DNA damage
- Cytokines (TNF-α, FasL)
- Chemotherapy drugs
- UV / ionizing radiation
- Growth factor deprivation
- Inflammation

Ceramide levels are controlled by synthesis, degradation and conversion to complex lipids.

## B. MANY CERAMIDES – DISTINCT FUNCTIONS
| Fatty acyl chain length | Medium (≤ C14) | Long (C16–C18) | Very-long (≥ C20) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Typical roles | Membrane structure; Signalling (context-dependent) | Pro-apoptotic; Growth arrest; Metabolic dysfunction | Barrier / structural roles; Often protective; Less inflammatory |
| Examples | C8, C12, C14 | C16 (CerS6), C18 | C20, C22, C24, C24:1, C26 |
| Disease associations | Lysosomal storage (Farber); Some neuropathies | Cancer, diabetes, obesity, insulin resistance, atherosclerosis | Skin barrier, myelin integrity, neuroprotection |

## C. CERAMIDES AS SIGNAL MOLECULES
- Activate / inhibit protein kinases (e.g., PKCζ, JNK, p38 MAPK, PP2A).
- Alter phosphatase activities (PP2A, PP1) – affect glycogen synthesis, insulin signalling.
- Inhibit phospholipase D (PLD) in plasma membrane → ↓ phosphatidic acid signalling.
- Promote cell cycle arrest (G0/G1, G2/M) and senescence.
- Regulate gene expression via transcription factors (e.g., NF-κB, p53, FOXO).
- Modulate inflammation and immune responses.
- Act locally in ceramide-rich membrane microdomains (rafts/platforms).

## D. CERAMIDES AND APOPTOSIS
Extrinsic pathway (plasma membrane)

Death ligands (TNF-α, FasL, TRAIL) bind to receptors → activates acid SMase → ceramide clusters in rafts → receptor aggregation → DISC formation → caspase-8 activation → apoptosis.

Diagram labels: Death ligand; Ceramide-rich domain (raft); DISC; Caspase-8

Intrinsic pathway (mitochondria)

Stress (ER → MAMs → mitochondria) → ↑ acid SMase → ↑ ceramide in mitochondrial outer membrane → mitochondrial permeability → cytochrome c release → Apaf-1 / caspase-9 activation → caspase-3 / -7 → apoptosis.

Other ceramide actions in apoptosis
- Promote Bax translocation and inhibit Bcl-2.
- Induce ER stress and Ca2+ release.
- Facilitate autophagy or necroptosis (context-dependent).

## Diet [illegible]
- High saturated fat and fructose ↑ ceramide synthesis.
- Omega-3 PUFAs can lower ceramide levels.
- Caloric restriction and exercise reduce ceramides.
- Phyto-ceramides (from plants) may support skin barrier.
- Mediterranean-style diet associated with lower ceramides.

## E. HEALTH AND DISEASE ASSOCIATIONS
| Disease / Condition | Role of ceramides | Key points |
|---|---|---|
| Cancer | ↑ Long-chain ceramides promote tumour suppression | Induce apoptosis, inhibit proliferation, angiogenesis; many tumours down-regulate ceramide synthesis or up-regulate degradation. |
| Metabolic diseases (obesity, diabetes, insulin resistance) | ↑ C16–C18 ceramides impair insulin signalling | Accumulate in muscle, liver and adipose tissue; ceramide:diacylglycerol ratio correlates with insulin resistance. |
| Cardiovascular disease | Pro-atherogenic effects | Endothelial dysfunction, inflammation, foam cell formation, plaque instability. |
| Neurodegenerative diseases | Altered ceramide metabolism | Parkinson's, Alzheimer's, Huntington's: increased ceramides in affected brain regions. |
| Lysosomal storage diseases | Ceramide accumulation | Farber disease (acid ceramidase deficiency); Niemann–Pick disease (SM accumulation). Cer(d18:1/26:0) biomarker in Farber disease. |
| Skin disorders | Altered ceramide levels or composition | Atopic dermatitis (↓ ceramides); psoriasis; ichthyosis (defects in synthesis / processing). |
| Infectious diseases | Modulate pathogen entry | Ceramide-rich domains facilitate many viral infections (e.g., JEV, Ebola, Norovirus). High ceramide can inhibit HIV entry. |
| Autoimmune / inflammatory | Regulate immune cell function | Influence T-cell activation, cytokine production, macrophage polarization. |
| Ageing | Ceramide accumulation | Associated with cellular senescence, frailty and chronic diseases. |

## F. KEY POINTS
- Ceramides are versatile lipid mediators with a central role in cell signalling and metabolism.
- Different ceramide species have distinct and sometimes opposing functions.
- Generated rapidly by SMases under stress or synthesized de novo.
- Control growth arrest, senescence, apoptosis, inflammation and metabolism.
- High levels of long-chain ceramides are linked to many diseases.
- Very-long-chain ceramides usually support structural and protective functions.
- Their levels are tightly regulated by enzymes of synthesis, interconversion and degradation.
- Targeting ceramide pathways offers therapeutic potential in cancer, metabolic and inflammatory diseases.
- Lifestyle and diet can influence ceramide metabolism and disease risk.

Disease-associated changes (examples)

| Condition | Change in ceramide profile | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| Obesity / T2D | ↑ C16:0, ↑ C18:0 in muscle, liver | Insulin resistance |
| NAFLD (fatty liver) | ↑ C16:0, C18:0, C18:1 | Inflammation, fibrosis |
| Alzheimer's disease | ↑ Very-long-chain (C24, C24:1) | Synaptic dysfunction |
| Atopic dermatitis | ↓ Ceramides (especially EOS) | Impaired skin barrier |
| Psoriasis | Altered CER ratios | Hyperproliferation |
| Farber disease | ↑ C26:0-ceramide | Lysosomal pathology |

Values are approximate and vary with species, diet, age and analytical method.
Most sphingoid bases are d18:1; phytosphingosine predominates in plants.

## Key takeaway
The balance between ceramide synthesis, interconversion and degradation is essential for cellular homeostasis. Dysregulation contributes to a wide spectrum of human diseases, but also offers opportunities for intervention.
