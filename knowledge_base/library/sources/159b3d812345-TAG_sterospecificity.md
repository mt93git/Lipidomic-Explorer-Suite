# 3. REGIO- AND STEREOSPECIFIC ANALYSIS PROCEDURES FOR TRIACYLGLYCEROLS

## (untitled panel)
The compositions of the fatty acids linked to the different positions of the glycerol moiety of triacylglycerols (TAGs) of animals, plants and microorganinans can be very different. Natural TAGs invariably exist as enantiomers because the glycerol backbone is chiral. Positions are [illegible] numbering (sn) system as sn-1, sn-2 and sn-3 from a Fischer projection (Figure 1). Analytical determination of these positional distributions is essential for studies of physical properties, biosynthesis, metabolism and function of TAGs in tissues.

### Fischer projection of L-glycerol derivative
Diagram labels: 1; 2; 3

In natural L-glycerol derivatives, the secondary OH (at C-2) is on the left. Numbering from top to bottom gives sn-1, sn-2 and sn-3.

## Key points
- Position sn-2 is secondary (β); sn-1 and sn-3 are primary (α and α′).
- Each position can have a distinctive fatty acid composition.
- TAGs are enantiomers due to the chiral glycerol backbone.
- Regiospecific analysis (e.g., pancreatic lipase) determines sn-2 composition.
- Stereospecific analysis is required for sn-1 and sn-3 compositions.
- Analytical data are vital for understanding biosynthesis, metabolism and function.

## General approaches
- Regiospecific analysis (simpler): determines composition of sn-2 only (e.g., pancreatic lipase, chemical or MS/NMR methods).
- Stereospecific analysis (more complex): determines compositions of sn-1 and sn-3 by degradative + synthetic steps followed by enzymatic hydrolysis and chromatographic separations (including chiral methods).
- Reference reviews: Kuksis & Itabashi [1,2].

## Surprising natural examples from stereospecific analyses
Milk fat shows extreme asymmetry: all short-chain fatty acids (butyric, 4:0 and hexanoic, 6:0) are concentrated in position sn-3 with none in sn-1 or sn-2.

## 3.1. REGIOSPECIFIC ANALYSIS OF TRIACYL-sn-GLYCEROLS BY PANCREATIC LIPASE HYDROLYSIS
Porcine pancreatic lipase (with colipase, bile salts and Ca2+) hydrolyses the ester bonds at the primary positions (sn-1 and sn-3) of triacylglycerols to release free fatty acids (FFAs) and leave a 2-monoacyl-sn-glycerol (2-MAG). The fatty acid of the 2-MAG represents the composition of position sn-2 in the native TAGs.

### Figure 2. Regiospecific analysis of triacylglycerols with pancreatic lipase
Diagram labels: Triacyl-sn-glycerol (TAG); Pancreatic lipase (+ colipase, bile salts, Ca2+, buffer, 40 °C); ~50% hydrolysis; R1 → COO−; 2-Monoacyl-sn-glycerol (2-MAG)

## Important features of the reaction
- Requires micellar substrate: vigorous shaking essential.
- Bile salts (e.g., taurodeoxycholate) and Ca2+ ions are essential.
- pH optimum ~8; temperature ~40 °C.
- No preference for sn-1 vs sn-3 (hydrolysed at same rate).
- Most straight-chain saturated and mono-, di-, trienoic acids hydrolysed at similar rates.
- Some polyunsaturated or hindered acids hydrolysed more slowly (e.g., 22:6n-3, trans-3-16:1, γ-linolenic acid).
- TAGs with short-chain acyl groups are hydrolysed faster than those with only long-chain acyls.
- Stop reaction at ~50–60% hydrolysis to minimize acyl migration and side reactions.

## Semi-micro procedure (Luddy et al. [3])
1. Mix TAG sample with buffer (pH 8) containing bile salt and CaCl2 at 40 °C; pre-incubate 5 min.
2. Add pancreatin (lipase + colipase); shake vigorously 1–2 min (~50% hydrolysis).
3. Stop reaction: extract with diethyl ether–isopropanol (3:1, v/v) containing antioxidant (e.g., BHT).
4. Separate ether-soluble (FFAs) and insoluble (2-MAG + residual TAG) by partition or TLC.
5. Isolate 2-MAG band by TLC or HPLC; trans-esterify to FAMEs.
6. Analyse FAMEs by gas chromatography → composition of sn-2.

## Advantages and limitations
### Advantages
- Simple, rapid and widely used.
- Good reproducibility with appropriate conditions.
- Representative of sn-2 composition for most natural TAGs (normal chain lengths).
- Small sample amounts (mg) sufficient.

### Limitations
- Cannot determine sn-1 and sn-3 compositions.
- Reduced rates for some PUFA and hindered acyls.
- Requires micelles; unsuitable for highly polar TAGs without carriers or pre-incubation.
- Acyl migration if incubation is prolonged or conditions not optimal.

## Analytical methods for sn-2 analysis
- Classical: pancreatic lipase hydrolysis → 2-MAG → TLC/HPLC → GC.
- Chemical methods (e.g., periodate oxidation followed by hydrolysis) can also give sn-2 data.
- Modern approaches: mass spectrometry (MS) and NMR spectroscopy.
