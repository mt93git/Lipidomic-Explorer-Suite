# 2. CARNITINE, ACYLCARNITINES AND FATTY ACID TRANSPORT INTO MITOCHONDRIA

## (untitled panel)
Carnitine transports long-chain fatty acyl groups (>C8) into the mitochondrial matrix for β-oxidation and helps remove surplus acyl groups. It maintains the balance between acyl-CoA and free CoA and is essential for energy production and metabolic homeostasis.

## 2.1 SOURCES AND ACTIVATION OF FATTY ACIDS
Sources of fatty acids
- Exogenous: diet and mobilization from other tissues.
- De novo synthesis from acetyl-CoA.
- Hydrolysis of triacylglycerols and phospholipids.

Activation to acyl-CoA

Fatty acid + CoA + ATP → Acyl-S-CoA + AMP + PPi

Diagram labels: Acyl-CoA synthetase

Cost: equivalent of 2 ATP.
- Acyl-CoAs bind to proteins (acyl-CoA binding domains).
- Directed to pathways: storage, oxidation, or other metabolism depending on cellular energy status.
- Long-chain acyl-CoAs cannot cross the inner mitochondrial membrane.

Carnitine enables transport into mitochondria and removal of excess acyl groups; prevents accumulation of acyl-CoAs, which is potentially toxic.

## 2.2 CARNITINE AND ACYLCARNITINES: ROLES
- Carnitine (3-hydroxyl) esterifies with acyl-CoA → acylcarnitines.
- Acylcarnitines are more lipophilic than carnitine.
- Functions:
  - Transport long-chain fatty acyl groups into mitochondria.
  - Export surplus acyl groups from mitochondria and peroxisomes.
  - Export acetyl- and other short-chain acyl groups.
  - Regulate acyl-CoA/CoA ratio and CoA homeostasis.
  - Aid excretion of acyl residues from amino acid metabolism (especially branched-chain amino acids).
- Homocarnitine can be esterified similarly.

Carnitine acetyltransferase (CrAT)

Found in mitochondrial matrix, peroxisomal matrix, ER and nucleus.
- Regulates acetyl-CoA / CoA ratio.
- During high workload (e.g., exercise): exports excess acetyl groups as acetylcarnitine → preserves free CoA.
- When workload ends: reconnects acetylcarnitine → acetyl-CoA for Krebs cycle.
- In peroxisomes: exports products of β-oxidation.

Overall reaction of carnitine shuttle:
Acyl-CoA (cytosol) + Carnitine (matrix) ⇌ Acylcarnitine (matrix) + CoA-SH (cytosol)

## Step 1: CPT I (outer membrane)
- Transfers acyl group from acyl-CoA to carnitine → acylcarnitine.
- Inhibited by malonyl-CoA.

## Step 2: Translocation (inner membrane)
- CACT exchanges acylcarnitine (in) for carnitine (out).
- Maintains mitochondrial carnitine level.

## Step 3: CPT II (matrix side)
- Transfers acyl group from acylcarnitine back to CoA → acyl-CoA.
- Carnitine is released and recycled.

## Key features of the carnitine transport system
- Long-chain fatty acids (>C8) require this shuttle; they cannot cross the inner membrane as CoA-thioesters.
- Rate-controlling step for mitochondrial β-oxidation.
- Three proteins: CPT I, CACT, CPT II.
- Requires intact membrane potential for efficient exchange.

## CPT I isoforms and regulation
- CPT IA – liver, kidney
- CPT IB – heart, skeletal muscle
- CPT IC – brain (may act as sensor of lipid metabolism in neurons)
- Malonyl-CoA (from ACC2) binds with high affinity to CPT I isoforms → inhibits acyl transfer → decreases fatty acid oxidation.
- High energy demand: ↓ ACC2 inhibition → ↑ β-oxidation. Inhibition of ACC1 → ↓ fatty acid synthesis.

## 2.3 SHORT- AND MEDIUM-CHAIN FATTY ACIDS
- Short-chain fatty acids (≤C8, e.g., octanoate) can cross the inner mitochondrial membrane in non-esterified form.
- Activated to acyl-CoA in the matrix for oxidation.
- Peroxisomes oxidize very long-chain fatty acids.

## 2.4 CLINICAL AND PHYSIOLOGICAL SIGNIFICANCE
- Carnitine deficiencies or defects in transport (CPT I, CACT, CPT II) → impaired fatty acid oxidation → hypoketotic hypoglycemia, cardiomyopathy, muscle weakness, exercise intolerance.
- Acylcarnitine profiles in plasma/tissues are diagnostic markers for inborn errors of metabolism.
- Supports energy homeostasis, insulin sensitivity and regulation of lipid and carbohydrate metabolism.
- Neuroprotective, anti-inflammatory, and involved in gene regulation and cell signalling.

## 2.5 SUMMARY
- Carnitine is essential for mitochondrial uptake of long-chain fatty acyl groups.
- Forms acylcarnitines to transport acyl groups into mitochondria and remove excess.
- Maintains acyl-CoA/CoA balance and CoA homeostasis.
- Works with three key proteins: CPT I, CACT, CPT II.
- Regulated by malonyl-CoA and the cell's energy status.
- Short- and medium-chain fatty acids use different transport routes.
- Carnitine is vital for energy production and metabolic health.
