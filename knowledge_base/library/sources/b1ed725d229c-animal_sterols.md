# 7. OTHER ANIMAL STEROLS

## (untitled panel)
Many sterols other than cholesterol occur in small amounts in tissues, most of which are intermediates in the biosynthetic pathway from lanosterol, and some have distinct functions of their own. Lanosterol, the first sterol intermediate in cholesterol biosynthesis, is found in wool wax (still the main commercial source) and at low levels in animal tissues (~0.1% of the cholesterol concentration). Its biosynthesis requires oxygen, hence its absence from prokaryotes, and its appearance is thought to have been a key event in the evolution of eukaryotes. Evolutionary 'streamlining' from lanosterol to cholesterol removes protruding methyl groups to improve interaction with phospholipids in membranes.

## LANOSTEROL (first intermediate) (4,4,14-trimethyl-cholesta-8,24-dien-3β-ol)
Source: wool wax (free and esterified); ~0.1% of cholesterol in tissues.

Role: first sterol in biosynthesis of cholesterol and other sterols.

## DESMOSTEROL (5,24-cholestadien-3β-ol)
Last intermediate in Bloch pathway (Δ24-sterol).

Functions:
- Involved in myelination (abundant in brains of young animals).
- Present in testes and spermatozoa; essential for reproduction.
- Stimulates genes for lipid biosynthetic enzymes via LXR; anti-inflammatory.
- Desmosterolosis: genetic block in conversion to cholesterol → severe neurodevelopmental defects.

## LATHOSTEROL (5α-cholest-7-en-3β-ol)
Intermediate after demethylation (7-dehydro-lanosterol → lathosterol).

Significance:
- Serum level inversely related to size of bile acid pool.
- Strongly correlates with cholesterol balance under dietary conditions.
- Biomarker of endogenous cholesterol synthesis.

## 7-DEHYDROCHOLESTEROL (cholesta-5,7-dien-3β-ol)
Final precursor in Kandutsch-Russell pathway to cholesterol.

Functions:
- In skin, UV light converts to vitamin D3 (cholecalciferol).
- Precursor of 'spiral steroids' that regulate Na+/K+-ATPases.
- Protects plasma & mitochondrial membranes from phospholipid autoxidation.
- In insects, converted back from cholesterol in pathway to ecdysone hormones.

## CHOLESTEROL (cholest-5-en-3β-ol)
Major sterol in animal tissues.

Functions:
- Structural component of cell membranes.
- Precursor of steroid hormones, bile acids, vitamin D3 and oxysterols.
- Essential for development, signalling, transport, nerve function and gene regulation.

## DIHYDROCHOLESTEROL (5α-cholestan-3β-ol)
Fully saturated sterol. Present in ruminant brain and adrenal glands. May influence membrane fluidity and steroid metabolism.

## 24S-HYDROXYCHOLESTEROL (cholest-5-en-3β,24S-diol)
Oxysterol formed in cells by CYP46A1 (mainly in brain). During cholesterol turnover. Involved in cholesterol transport across BBB and signalling.

## 27-HYDROXYCHOLESTEROL (cholest-5-en-3β,27-hydroxy)
Formed in many tissues. Regulates cholesterol homeostasis and gene expression via LXR. Can be converted to bile acids.

## TESTIS MEIOSIS-ACTIVATING STEROL (TMAS)
Related sterol present in testes and spermatozoa. Essential for meiosis and fertility in mammals.

## 25-HYDROXYCHOLESTEROL (cholest-5-en-3β,25-diol)
Produced in liver and brain. Regulates cholesterol synthesis and inflammatory responses. Precursor of bile acids.

## MARINE INVERTEBRATES: NOVEL STEROLS
- Many marine invertebrates contain unusual sterols with novel ring structures and/or side chains.
- At least 80 distinct sterols have been isolated from echinoderms and ~100 from sponges.
- Some are derived from cholesterol; others from plant sterols or alternative biosynthetic intermediates.

## OXIDIZED (OXY-) STEROLS
- Cholesterol can be oxidized in tissues or foods to form oxysterols with hydroperoxy, epoxy, hydroxy or keto groups.
- Enter body via diet or are formed in tissues by microsomal/mitochondrial oxidations.
- Associated with inhibition of cholesterol and bile acid synthesis and other abnormalities in lipid metabolism.
- Some oxysterols (e.g., 7-ketocholesterol, 25-hydroxycholesterol) have signalling roles; excessive levels may be toxic.
- Oxysterols in membranes, especially with conjugated diene systems (e.g., 7-dehydrocholesterol), may contribute to ferroptosis.

## HYDROLYSIS OF CHOLESTEROL ESTERS
- Cholesterol ester hydrolases (CEHs) liberate cholesterol and fatty acids on demand.
- Provide cholesterol for membrane turnover, lipoprotein remodeling and steroid hormone synthesis (e.g., adrenal cortex).
- Major CEHs in mammals:
- Neutral cholesterol ester hydrolase (nCEH) – macrophages, liver; important in preventing foam cell formation.
- Lysosomal acid lipase (LAL) – lysosomes.
- Hormone-sensitive lipase (HSL) – adrenal, adipose tissue.
- Hepatic cytosolic CE hydrolase (HCEH).
- Carboxyl ester hydrolase (CEH).

## Key points
Other animal sterols are present in small amounts but have important roles in development, reproduction, cholesterol regulation and signalling.
Some (e.g., desmosterol, 7-dehydrocholesterol) are biosynthetic intermediates with distinct functions.
Oxidized sterols and their esters are biologically active and implicated in disease.
