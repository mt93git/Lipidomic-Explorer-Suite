# 5. N-ACYL PHOSPHATIDYLETHANOLAMINE (NAPE)

## Structure
N-acyl phosphatidylethanolamine (NAPE)

Diagram labels: sn-1; sn-2; sn-3; (N-acyl chain); R1, R2 = fatty acyl chains (common N-acyl chains: 16:0, 18:0, 18:1, 18:2, 20:4)

## Occurrence and importance in animals
- Detected in several animal tissues (~0.01% of total phospholipids); abundant in brain, nervous tissues, epidermis.
- Precursor of bioactive N-acyl ethanolamides, especially anandamide.

## Biosynthesis (N-acyl transfer)
Diagram labels: Donor: PC (sn-1 acyl); Acceptor: PE; (diacyl or alkenylacyl); PLA2ε (Ca2+ dependent); Lyso-PC; N-acyl-PE; PE

## Physiological roles under stress
- Accumulates in ischemic injury, infarction or cancer.
- Neuroprotection, anti-inflammation, membrane stabilization.
- Stimulates lactate efflux via monocarboxylate transporters.
- Present in plasma after high-fat diet; crosses BBB and accumulates in hypothalamus.

## In plants
- Common in cereal grains (1.9% of cotton seeds, 10–12% of oats); low in other tissues.
- Increases under physiological stress.
- Hydrolysed to N-acyl ethanolamides and phosphatidic acid.
- Functions: root elongation, seed germination, seedling growth, pathogen defence.

## In microorganisms
- Found in many microorganisms.
- Absidia corymbifera: ~6% of membrane lipids; also contains phospho(N-ethoxycarbonyl-PE.
- Bacillus subtilis: aminoacyl PEs (alanine or lysine).

## C. Retinal reactions in the visual cycle
Diagram labels: PE + all-trans-retinal; (Schiff base); N-retinylidene-PE (transport intermediate); Bis-retinoid PE condensation product

## (untitled panel)
May accumulate with age in retinal pigment epithelial cells → involved in Stargardt disease and other retinal disorders.

# 6. MONO- AND DIMETHYLPHOSPHATIDYLETHANOLAMINES

## (untitled panel)
Diagram labels: Phosphatidylethanolamine (PE); PE N-methyltransferase (SAM → SAH); Monomethyl-phosphatidylethanolamine (MMPE); PE N-methyltransferase (SAM → SAH); Dimethyl-phosphatidylethanolamine (DMPE)

## Role in lipid metabolism
- MMPE and DMPE are intermediates in the Kennedy pathway for phosphatidylcholine (PC) synthesis.
- Sequential N-methylation → PC.

## Occurrence
- Always present only in trace amounts in animal and plant tissues.
- Major pathway for PC synthesis in yeasts and many bacteria.
- Intermediates are not essential structural components of yeast membranes.

## (untitled panel)
Functions: not well defined; presumed roles limited to their function as biosynthetic intermediates.

Diagram labels: PE–HNE adduct; NAPE-PLD; Phosphatidic acid; Amine adduct

## D. Reaction with levuglandins and isolevuglandins
Diagram labels: PE; Levuglandin (e.g., from AA); Hydroxy-lactam PE adduct (cytotoxic)

## Functions and significance
- NAPE-PLD also hydrolyses N-acyl PEs (e.g., anandamide) + phosphatidic acid.
- Michael adducts accelerate lipid peroxidation → oxidative stress.
- In vivo: inflammatory mediators; implicated in atherogenesis, diabetes, aging.
- Alkyl PE adducts induce negative membrane curvature; can alter function of transporters, channels, receptors and enzymes.

## E. Schiff base formation and Amadori rearrangement
Diagram labels: PE (headgroup); Glucose; Schiff base; Amadori product (deoxy-D-fructosyl-PE)

## Consequences
- Further oxidation → carboxymethyl- and carboxyethyl-PE adducts.
- Induce pro-inflammatory cytokines; promote apoptosis.
- Alter membrane biophysical properties → membrane disintegration.
- Potential biomarker for early hyperglycaemia.

## (untitled panel)
Note: PS can also form Schiff/Amadori adducts, but such species are harder to detect in vivo.

# 8. PHOSPHATIDYLETHANOL (PEtOH)

## Structure
Phosphatidylethanol (PEtOH)

Diagram labels: sn-1; sn-2; sn-3

## Formation
Slow transphosphatidylation of phosphatidylcholine (PC) by phospholipase D (PLD) in the presence of ethanol.

Diagram labels: PC + EtOH; PLD; PEtOH + Choline

## Key features
- Formed slowly in cell membranes, notably erythrocytes.
- Long half-life in serum: ~2–3 weeks.
- Useful biochemical and forensic marker for alcohol abuse.

## Occurrence
- Higher levels in chronic alcoholics than in moderate drinkers.
- Levels correlate with amount and duration of alcohol intake.

## Biological effects (evidence emerging)
- Traditionally considered physiologically inert.
- In Drosophila, affects lipid-regulated potassium channels.
- May contribute to ethanol tolerance.
- Some evidence suggests a role in colon cancer.

AA, arachidonic acid; BBB, blood–brain barrier; SAM, S-adenosylmethionine; SAH, S-adenosylhomocysteine.
