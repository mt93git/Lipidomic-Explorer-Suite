# 2.4. BROWN ADIPOSE TISSUE

## 2.4. BROWN ADIPOSE TISSUE
Brown adipose tissue (BAT) is multilocular, highly vascularized and rich in mitochondria with iron-containing pigments that give the tissue its colour. It is located in characteristic regions (around the heart, kidney, pancreas, liver, neck) where it can deliver warm venous blood to the spinal cord and brain. BAT arises from progenitors closer to skeletal muscle than white adipocytes and generates heat by rapid oxidation of fat (“non-shivering thermogenesis”), a feature critical in young animals and those recovering from hibernation.

- In cold exposure, noradrenaline release and β-adrenergic stimulation initiate lipolysis of stored TAGs, releasing free fatty acids (FFAs) and transiently activating PPARγ.
- FFAs activate uncoupling protein-1 (UCP1) in the inner mitochondrial membrane, which uncouples electron transport from ATP synthesis. Protons re-enter the matrix via UCP1, and energy is released as heat instead of ATP.
- Free long-chain FFAs drive UCP1 activity and act as self-regulating second messengers; purine nucleotides inhibit UCP1. Dense vasculature delivers substrates (FFAs, glucose) and warms blood.
- BAT metabolism is indispensable in hibernating mammals and can account for ~50% of dietary lipids and glucose in cold-exposed mice. BAT activity is acutely induced by cold via the sympathetic nervous system.
- BAT is increasingly recognized as an endocrine organ secreting “batokines” that may influence cardiovascular and metabolic health.

## Brown vs White vs Beige Adipocytes
### Brown adipocyte
- Multilocular lipid droplets
- Very many mitochondria
- High UCP1 expression
- Dedicated organ (in animals)

### White adipocyte
- Unilocular large lipid droplet
- Fewer mitochondria
- Low UCP1
- Storage & endocrine

### Beige (brite) adipocyte
- Multilocular droplets
- Inducible UCP1
- Emerges in white fat
- Thermogenic capacity

## Additional regulatory factors
- Cardiolipin in mitochondria is essential for optimal oxidative phosphorylation and thermogenesis.
- EBF2 transcription factor maintains mitochondrial phospholipid integrity.
- Peroxisomal pathways: plasmenyl-phospholipids and monomethyl branched-chain FA β-oxidation (ACOX2) contribute (UCP1-independent).
- Sphingomyelin accumulates during BAT differentiation.
- Acyl-CoA synthetases & thioesterases control substrate availability for β-oxidation.
- GPR3 (Gs-coupled receptor; ligand oleic acid) → ↑ cAMP → PKA → thermogenesis.
- SCD1 (Δ9 desaturase) ↑ in cold → ↑ NE release.

## Beige (brite) adipocytes
- Present within white adipose tissue; arise from multipotent pre-adipocytes.
- Use same machinery (e.g., UCP1) to release heat under β-adrenergic stimulation.
- Three additional thermogenic mechanisms: Ca2+ cycling, creatine substrate cycle, and TAG/FA cycling.
- In adult humans, most classical “brown fat” depots are actually beige fat.

## 2.5. OTHER FUNCTIONS OF TRIACYLGLYCEROL DEPOTS
### Cushioning & insulation
- Subcutaneous fat cushions joints and organs.
- Acts as insulation against cold (e.g., marine mammals, pigs).
- Skin-associated adipocytes support hair follicles and skin regeneration.

### Defense & cryoprotection
- Adipose tissue responds to bacterial infection (barrier and metabolic defence).
- Some freeze-tolerant insects synthesize TAGs with acetic acid → remain liquid at low temperatures → interact with water → cryoprotection.

### Buoyancy
- Fat depots are less dense than water → aid buoyancy.
- Reduces energy expenditure during swimming.

### Sonar lens in echolocation (odontocetes)
Diagram labels: Melon (acoustic lens); Layered lipid structures

- TAGs, glyceryl ether diesters, and wax esters form the acoustic lens.
- Unique TAGs contain two 3-methylbutyric (isovaleric) acids + one long-chain FA.
- 3D topographical arrangement focuses sound waves.

### Metabolic reservoir
- Reserve of bioactive lipids (eicosanoids, fat-soluble vitamins) and structural components (FA, cholesterol, retinol) for membranes.
- Provides rapid energy and essential metabolites.

## Clinical relevance and therapeutic interest
### Why BAT matters
- Enhances energy expenditure and protects against obesity.
- Improves glucose metabolism and insulin sensitivity.
- May reduce cardiovascular risk factors.
- Target for combating obesity, type 2 diabetes and metabolic syndrome.

### Strategies under investigation
- Cold exposure and β-adrenergic agonists.
- Activators of UCP1 and thermogenic pathways.
- Modulators of batokines (e.g., 12,13-diHOME).
- Targeting lipid metabolism (SCD1, CPT1, ACOX2).
- Drugs to promote browning of white fat (beige adipogenesis).

### Associations with disease when BAT is reduced
- Obesity
- Insulin resistance & type 2 diabetes
- Dyslipidemia
- Cardiovascular disease
- Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease
- Increased inflammatory risk

### Key take-home message
Brown and beige adipose tissues are specialized fat depots that convert stored lipids into heat via regulated mitochondrial uncoupling and related mechanisms. They play critical roles in energy homeostasis and represent targets for treating metabolic diseases.
