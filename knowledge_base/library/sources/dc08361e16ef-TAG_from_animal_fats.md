# 1.3. TRIACYLGLYCEROLS FROM ANIMAL FATS – ADIPOSE TISSUE

## (untitled panel)
Much of the triacylglycerols in animal tissues, including some of the commercially important fats such as lard or tallow, are contained within various adipose tissue sites in cells known as adipocytes (or in lipid droplets within cells), where they serve mainly as an energy store, but also cushion tissues such as liver. The subcutaneous fats help to insulate animals, while fat stores in fish and marine animals help to maintain buoyancy. While some fatty acids are synthesised de novo in tissues (saturated and monoenoic acids), the composition of the triacylglycerols reflects that of the diet to an appreciable extent. In terrestrial animals, the composition tends to be quite simple, with C16 (mainly 16:0) and C18 fatty acids predominating. All the essential fatty acids, such as linoleic and α-linolenic acids, must come from the diet of course, although polyunsaturated fatty acids tend to be relatively minor components of triacylglycerols in comparison to the complex lipids. Ruminant animals have relatively saturated fats because the dietary unsaturated fatty acids are subjected to biohydrogenation in the rumen, a process that generates trans fatty acids as by-products and odd- and branched-chain fatty acids. Non-ruminant herbivores can have more linolenic acid from grass, while marine mammals are characterized by high concentrations of long-chain mono- and polyenoic fatty acids because of their diet of fish. Fish oils have their own section below.

## GENERAL PATTERNS IN MOST SPECIES
- Saturated fatty acids predominate in position sn-1.
- Position sn-2 contains mainly unsaturated fatty acids, especially linoleic acid (18:2).
- Longer-chain fatty acids tend to be enriched in position sn-3.
- The pig and related species are major exceptions to these general rules (see text).

## FACTORS AFFECTING COMPOSITION & STRUCTURE
- Diet composition is a major influence.
- Site of adipose tissue (subcutaneous vs internal) affects overall fatty acid composition but not positional distribution.
- Species differences are marked.
- Enzymatic specificity of acyltransferases determines positional distribution during TAG synthesis.

## Biosynthetic insights from in vitro studies (rat adipocytes)
- Fatty acids of extracelular origin esterified to all three positions in similar, but not identical, proportions to natural distributions.
- Oleic acid synthesised from exogenous stearic acid is esterified mainly in position sn-3.
- Fatty acids synthesised de novo are esterified only to sn-1 and sn-2.

(Henderson, R.J. et al., BBA Lipids, 574, 8–17 (1979))

## Other noteworthy examples
### Freeze-tolerant insect Eurosta solidaginis
- Produces a high proportion of acetylated triacylglycerols in winter for storage.
- Remain liquid at low temperatures, available for energy during dormancy.

### Aphids (plant sap feeders)
- Secrete distinctive TAGs onto their external surfaces.
- sn-1 and sn-3: myristic acid (14:0)
- sn-2: high short-chain fatty acids, incl. sorbic acid (2,4-hexadienoic acid)
