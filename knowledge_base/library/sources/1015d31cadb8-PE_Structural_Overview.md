# PHOSPHATIDYLETHANOLAMINE (PE)

## (untitled panel)
Phosphatidylethanolamine or 1,2-diacyl-sn-glycero-3-phosphoethanolamine (once given the trivial name ‘cephalin’) is usually the second most abundant phospholipid in animals and plants after phosphatidylcholine, and it is often the main lipid component of microbial membranes. It can amount to 20% of liver phospholipids and as much as 45% of those of brain; higher proportions are found in mitochondria than in other organelles. As such, it is obviously a key building block of membrane bilayers, where it is present exclusively in the inner leaflet of the plasma membrane in animal cells. It is a neutral or zwitterionic phospholipid (at least in the pH range 2 to 7).

## 1. STRUCTURE OF PHOSPHATIDYLETHANOLAMINE
## A. Diacyl (most common)
Diagram labels: sn-1; sn-2; sn-3; Head group: phosphoethanolamine (Net charge ~ 0 at pH 2–7; zwitterionic)

## B. Other structural types in animal tissues
- Alkylacyl-PE (ether linkage at sn-1)
- Alkenylacyl (plasmalogen)-PE (O-alk-1’-enyl-PE)
- Diacyl-PE

Ether-linked species may account for up to ~70% of PE in some cells (inflammatory cells, neurons, tumour cells).

## C. Occurrence and distribution
Animals
- 2nd most abundant phospholipid after phosphatidylcholine (PC)
- ~20% of liver phospholipids
- Up to ~45% of brain phospholipids
- Higher proportion in mitochondria than in other organelles
- Located exclusively in the inner leaflet of the plasma membrane

Diagram labels: Mitochondrion (high PE); Endoplasmic reticulum; Plasma membrane (inner leaflet only); PE; PC; Other lipids

## Plants
- Important structural phospholipid in chloroplast and other membranes
- Major component of thylakoid membranes (photosynthetic tissues)

## Microorganisms
- Main phospholipid of bacterial membranes
- Essential for membrane integrity and function

## Key features of PE
- Neutral/zwitterionic phospholipid
- Abundant in all kingdoms of life
- Key structural lipid of membranes
- High content of polyunsaturated fatty acids, especially at the sn-2 position

## Functional importance
- Maintains membrane curvature and fluidity
- Involved in membrane fusion, autophagy, and protein function
- Precursor for signalling lipids (e.g., N-acyl PE, lyso-PE) and for PC (via methylation)

## (untitled panel)
Ether-linked (alkylacyl or alkenylacyl) PE species often have 16:0, 18:0 or 18:1 at sn-1 (ether bond), while 20:4 and 22:6 are predominant at sn-2.

## General features in animals
- Saturated fatty acids concentrated at sn-1 position
- Polyunsaturated (20:4, 22:6) highly enriched at sn-2 position
- sn-1 usually contains 16:0 or 18:0 (saturated)
- sn-2 enriched in 20:4 (arachidonic acid) and 22:6 (docosahexaenoic acid)

For molecular species data (mouse brain): Blevins, M.S. et al., Anal. Chem., 94, 12621–12629 (2022).

## 4. SUMMARY OF KEY POINTS
- Phosphatidylethanolamine (PE) is a major phospholipid in all domains of life.
- It is especially abundant in mitochondria and the inner leaflet of the plasma membrane (in animals).
- PE species are diverse: diacyl, alkylacyl, and alkenylacyl (plasmalogen).
- Fatty acid distribution is conserved: saturated at sn-1; polyunsaturated (especially 20:4, 22:6) at sn-2.
- PE is essential for membrane structure, function, and as a precursor for lipid metabolism and signalling.

## Biological roles of PE
- Structural component of membranes
- Maintains membrane curvature and fluidity
- Important in fusion, autophagy, and protein activity
- Precursor for N-acyl PE, lyso-PE and for phosphatidylcholine (via methylation pathway)
- Essential for cell growth, signalling and survival
