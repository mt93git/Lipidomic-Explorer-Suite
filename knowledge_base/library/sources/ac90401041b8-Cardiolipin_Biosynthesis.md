# 2. BIOSYNTHESIS AND METABOLISM OF CARDIOLIPIN

## A. BIOSYNTHESIS IN EUKARYOTES (mitochondrial inner membrane)
Two different precursors are required: (i) phosphatidylglycerol (PG) and (ii) CDP-diacylglycerol (CDP-DG). All enzymes face the matrix side.

Diagram labels: Phosphatidic acid (PA); Phosphatidylglycerol phosphate (PGP); Phosphatidylglycerol (PG); CDP-diacylglycerol (CDP-DG); Cardiolipin (CL); PGP synthase (PgsA); PGP phosphatase (Pgp1); CDP-DG synthase (TAM41/TAMM41); Cardiolipin synthase (CLS, Tafazzin); CTP; PPi; Pi; CMP

PA is synthesized on the ER and imported to mitochondria by protein complexes (e.g., [illegible] ASSOCIATED MEMBRANES, MAMs).

CDP-DG synthase in mitochondria (TAM41/TAMM41) is structurally distinct from the ER CDP-DG synthases. Cardiolipin synthase is a phosphatidyltransferase belonging to the CDP-alcohol phosphatidyltransferase superfamily (in animals and plants).

## B. BIOSYNTHESIS IN PROKARYOTES (bacterial inner membrane)
(i) Main pathway – transphosphatidylation (energy independent)

Diagram labels: 2 Phosphatidylglycerol (PG); Cardiolipin (CL); Cardiolipin synthase (ClsA or ClsB); – Glycerol; PG + Phosphatidylethanolamine (PE); ClsB or ClsC; – Ethanolamine; Cardiolipin (CL)

- ClsA is the major synthase in E. coli during exponential growth (ClsB/C are minor).
- Cls enzymes belong to the phospholipase D superfamily.
- Some bacteria (e.g., Acinetobacter baumannii) have ClsO on the outer membrane that decorates LPS with cardiolipin.
- Archaea use a distinct cardiolipin synthase that employs archaetidylglycerol (diether PG analogue).

## C. REMODELLING OF CARDIOLIPIN IN EUKARYOTES – UNIQUE TAFAzzin PATHWAY
Diagram labels: Newly synthesized cardiolipin (CLprecursor) (heterogeneous acyl chains); PLA2 (calcium-independent) or iPLA2γ; – Fatty acid; Monolysocardiolipin (MLCL); Tafazzin (TAZ); Acyl donor phospholipid (e.g., phosphatidylcholine, phosphatidylethanolamine); Lyso-phospholipid (acceptor); Mature cardiolipin (CL) (symmetrical, enriched in linoleate)

- Tafazzin catalyzes a CoA-independent transacylation between MLCL and other phospholipids.
- The process yields the characteristic symmetrical molecular species (e.g., tetra-linolooyl CL). It is essential for optimal function.

## ENERGY COST (EUKARYOTIC PATHWAY)
3 ATP (for PGS, CDP-DG synthase, tafazzin recycling) + CTP + myo-inositol → 2 PPi + 2 MP (hydrolysis)
Net consumption per cycle: 3 ATP + CTP + myo-inositol

## 3. FUNCTIONS AND PATHOPHYSIOLOGICAL SIGNIFICANCE

## A. KEY FUNCTIONS OF CARDIOLIPIN
Mitochondrial bioenergetics
- Essential for assembly, stability and activity of respiratory chain complexes (esp. III and IV) and ATP synthase (V).
- Optimizes electron transport and ATP production.
- Modulates membrane curvature and supercomplex formation.

Membrane structure
- Promotes negative curvature and tight packing.
- Stabilizes mitochondrial inner membrane and cristae architecture.
- Contributes to membrane fluidity and domain formation.

Protein binding and signalling scaffold
- Binds cytochrome c, tBid, kinases, and other proteins.
- Acts as a scaffold for signalling platforms.
- Interacts with proteins via electrostatic and hydrophobic contacts.

Apoptosis regulation
- Peroxidized or oxidized CL releases cytochrome c.
- Facilitates apoptosome formation and caspase activation.
- Central in intrinsic (mitochondrial) apoptotic pathway.

Oxidative stress defence
- Scavenges ROS and limits lipid peroxidation.
- Contributes to mitochondrial redox homeostasis.
- Protects against oxidative damage.

## B. CONSEQUENCES OF CARDIOLIPIN DYSFUNCTION
Cardiolipin depletion or remodelling defects
- Impaired respiratory chain activity and lower ATP.
- Mitochondrial fragmentation and structural defects.
- Increased ROS production.

Peroxidized cardiolipin
- Promotes cytochrome c release.
- Triggers apoptosis.
- Associated with aging and degenerative conditions.

Clinical and disease associations
- Barth syndrome (tafazzin deficiency).
- Cardiomyopathies and heart failure.
- Neurodegenerative diseases (Alzheimer's, Parkinson's, Huntington's).
- Metabolic disorders.

Cancer
- Altered CL metabolism supports tumour growth and survival.
- Affects mitochondrial fusion, bioenergetics and apoptosis resistance.

Other
- Ischemia-reperfusion injury.
- Diabetes and obesity.
- Sepsis.
- Liver disease.

## 4. KEY POINTS
- Cardiolipin is a dimeric phospholipid unique to mitochondria and some bacteria.
- Eukaryotes use PA → PG → CDP-DG → cardiolipin (energy-dependent) pathway.
- Bacteria use transphosphatidylation of PG (energy-independent) via Cls enzymes.
- Final composition is achieved by tafazzin-dependent remodelling.
- Cardiolipin is vital for mitochondrial structure, respiration, apoptosis regulation and stress defence.
- Dysfunction or oxidation of cardiolipin is linked to many human diseases.
- Cardiolipin is indispensable for cellular energy production and integrity.
