# 3. FUNCTION OF CARDIOLIPIN

## (untitled panel)
Cardiolipin (CL) is the signature lipid of mitochondria (and some bacteria), located almost exclusively in the inner mitochondrial membrane (IMM). Its unique dimeric structure with two phosphate groups and four acyl chains enables diverse interactions that are essential for mitochondrial structure, bioenergetics, metabolism and quality control.

Diagram labels: Outer membrane; Intermembrane space; Inner membrane (cardiolipin rich); Matrix; Cristae

## A. CARDIOLIPIN–PROTEIN INTERACTIONS
- CL binds hundreds of unrelated proteins.
- No universal sequence motif; three key interaction regions in proteins:
  1. Phosphate binding patches (basic residues: Arg, Lys, His)
  2. Hydroxyl binding patches (aromatic/polar residues: Trp, Tyr)
  3. Acyl chain (hydrophobic) patches (Ile, Leu, Val, Phe)
- Head groups interact via electrostatic and H-bonds; acyl chains via van der Waals contacts.
- CL adapts its conformation to different protein surfaces; promotes membrane curvature and domain formation.

Diagram labels: Cardiolipin interacting with a protein; Protein surface; Electrostatic / H-bond; Hydrogen bond; Hydrophobic interaction

Examples of CL-binding proteins
- Cytochrome c (haemoprotein)
- Elamipretide (cell-permeable peptide)
- Snake venom cardiotoxins
- α-Synuclein
- ABC transporter ABCB10
- Plasma protein β2-glycoprotein I
- Many respiratory chain proteins and enzymes

CL promotes non-bilayer structures and lateral organization of the IMM.

## B. ROLE IN OXIDATIVE PHOSPHORYLATION (OXPHOS)
Diagram labels: Electron transport chain in the inner mitochondrial membrane; Matrix (side); 4 H+; 4 H+; 2 H+; H+; I; II; Q; III; Cyt c; IV; NADH dehydrogenase; Succinate dehydrogenase; Cytochrome bc1 complex; Cytochrome c oxidase; Intermembrane space; NADH; NAD+; Succinate; Fumarate; 1/2 O2; H2O; ADP + Pi; ATP; Cardiolipin

One respirasome (example): I + (III2) + (IV) – can contain up to ~200 CL molecules.

1 Respiratory chain complexes
- CL is required for assembly, stability and optimal activity of OXPHOS complexes.
- Integral component in structures of complexes III (bc1), IV (cytochrome c oxidase), I, and probably II, and the ADP/ATP carrier.
- "Glue" that organizes components into supercomplexes (respirasomes).
- Displacement of linoleate by DHA in CL reduces respiratory complex activities.

2 Enzymes and metabolic pathway components
- Required for optimal activity of pyruvate dehydrogenase (PDH) – links glycolysis to TCA cycle.
- Stabilizes mitochondrial calcium uniporter (MCU) – couples Ca2+ signalling to bioenergetics.
- Essential for assembly and activity of many matrix and membrane enzymes.

3 Uncoupling and energy metabolism
- Three CL molecules bind tightly to uncoupling protein 1 (UCP1) in brown adipose tissue – crucial for thermogenesis.
- Supports proper function of ATP synthase – degree of oligomerization and order of assemblies.

4 Mitochondrial dynamics and quality control
- Regulates fusion–fission balance (interacts with Drp1, OPA1, Mfn2).
- CL externalization to outer membrane is a signal for mitophagy.
- Maintains cristae structure and IMM integrity.

## Overall roles
- Bioenergetics and ATP production
- Maintains membrane structure and cristae morphology
- Regulates proteins and enzymes
- Couples metabolism and signalling
- Protects against stress and oxidative damage
- Controls mitochondrial dynamics and quality

## C. OTHER IMPORTANT FUNCTIONS
Cristae biogenesis and membrane morphology
- High CL content favors negative curvature.
- Essential for cristae junctions and stability.

Endoplasmic reticulum–mitochondria contact sites (MAMs)
- CL regulates MAM formation and function.
- Important for Ca2+ transfer, lipid exchange and signalling.

Antioxidant and stress protection
- Scavenges reactive oxygen species (ROS).
- Protects proteins, lipids and mtDNA from oxidative damage.

Apoptosis signalling
- Diagram labels: tBid; CL externalization (outer membrane); ROS; Cytochrome c release
- Externalization of CL promotes cytochrome c release.
- Key step in intrinsic apoptotic pathway.

Lipid raft / domain formation
- CL forms microdomains.
- Recruits and concentrates proteins and lipids for efficient function.

## D. CONSEQUENCES OF CARDIOLIPIN DYSFUNCTION
Energy failure
- Reduced OXPHOS efficiency
- Low ATP production
- Exercise intolerance

Oxidative stress
- Increased ROS
- Lipid peroxidation
- mtDNA damage

Mitochondrial dysfunction
- Disrupted cristae
- Impaired dynamics
- Mitophagy defects

Cellular outcomes
- Apoptosis
- Necrosis
- Inflammation

Diseases linked
- Barth syndrome (tafazzin defect)
- Cardiomyopathies
- Neurodegenerative diseases (Alzheimer's, Parkinson's, Huntington's)
- Ischemia–reperfusion injury
- Diabetes and obesity
- Cancer
- Aging

## E. KEY POINTS
- Unique dimeric phospholipid of mitochondria and some bacteria.
- Four acyl chains and two phosphates enable diverse functions.
- Essential for OXPHOS, membrane structure and mitochondrial dynamics.
- Interacts with many proteins and forms supercomplexes.
- Protects against oxidative stress and regulates signalling.
- Remodelling to symmetrical linoleoyl-species is crucial for function.
- Dysfunction is strongly associated with many human diseases.

## KEY MESSAGE
Cardiolipin is an irreplaceable mitochondrial phospholipid whose unique dimeric structure enables it to act as a structural scaffold, a molecular glue for protein complexes, and a dynamic regulator of mitochondrial bioenergetics, metabolism, signalling and quality control. Its dysfunction is linked to a wide range of human diseases.
