# 1. PHOSPHATIDYLINOSITOL (PI)

## (untitled panel)
Phosphatidylinositol is an anionic phospholipid that is both a membrane constituent and a crucial participant in metabolic and signalling processes. It is concentrated in the inner leaflet of eukaryotic membranes and is the precursor of many important inositol phospholipids and second messengers.

## A. STRUCTURE
Diagram labels: sn-1; sn-2; sn-3; Illustrated species: sn-1-stearoyl-2-arachidonoyl-glycerophosphorylinositol (18:0/20:4)
- R1 = stearoyl (18:0)
- R2 = arachidonoyl (20:4n-6)

## B. OCCURRENCE
- Present in all tissues, cell types and membranes of eukaryotes at relatively low levels.
- Abundant in brain (~10% of phospholipids).
- Entirely in the inner leaflet of the plasma membrane of nucleated cells and of the erythrocyte membrane.
- Not found in most prokaryotes (exceptions: some Mycobacteria, Actinomycetales; thermo-philic Rhodothermus marinus has dialkylether forms).
- In humans, kidney synthesises ~2 g/day from glucose.

- Stearic acid is almost exclusively at sn-1.
- Arachidonic acid is almost exclusively at sn-2.
- Up to ~70% of PI can be the single species 18:0/20:4.
- Plant PI: palmitic acid (16:0) in sn-1; linoleic (18:2) and linolenic (18:3) acids in sn-2.

Inositol headgroup is bulky and highly hydrated → less crowded packing of acyl chains → increased water penetration and membrane fluidity → important for metabolism, signalling and protein transport.

## D. BIOSYNTHESIS IN EUKARYOTES
Diagram labels: Glucose; Glucose-6-phosphate; myo-Inositol; Phosphatidic acid (PA); CDP-diacylglycerol synthase (rate-limiting); CTP; PPi; CDP-diacylglycerol (CDP-DAG); PI synthase (PIS); CMP; Phosphatidylinositol (PI)
- PIS is located in the endoplasmic reticulum (ER) and mitochondria-associated membranes (MAM).
- PIS can catalyse the reverse reaction; net synthesis depends on concentrations and product removal.
- PI is distributed to other membranes by vesicular transport and by phosphatidylinositol transfer proteins (PITPs).

## E. FUNCTIONS OF PHOSPHATIDYLINOSITOL

## Membrane properties
- PI concentrated in inner leaflet → membrane asymmetry.
- Contributes negative charge to membrane surface.
- Regulates membrane fluidity and protein function.

## Precursor of important lipids
- Phosphoinositides (PIP, PIP2, PIP3)
- Diacylglycerol (DAG)
- Inositol trisphosphate (IP3)
- Phosphatidic acid
- Glycosylphosphatidyl-inositol (GPI) anchors

## Cell signalling
Diagram labels: Receptor; PIP2; PLC; DAG; IP3; Ca2+ release; PKC activation
## Protein anchoring and trafficking
Diagram labels: GPI-anchored protein
- PI is the lipid part of GPI anchors.
- Involved in vesicular trafficking and membrane contact sites.

## Metabolic integration
- Links lipid metabolism with signal transduction.
- Interacts with enzymes and scaffolding proteins.
- Essential for growth, immunity and many other cellular processes.

## F. METABOLIC FATE AND DERIVED SIGNALS

## Hydrolysis by phospholipases
- PI is hydrolysed by PLC or PLD.
- Generates key second messengers.

1. Phospholipase C (PLC)

PI → (PLC) DAG + IP3
→ Ca2+ mobilization
→ Protein kinase C (PKC) activation

2. Phospholipase D (PLD)

PI → (PLD) Phosphatidic acid (PA) + Inositol
PA → mTOR signalling, PI 4-phosphate 5-kinase activation

## G. BIOLOGICAL AND PATHOLOGICAL RELEVANCE

## Physiological roles
- Essential for cell signalling, membrane dynamics and protein anchoring.
- Vital for neuronal function, immunity, fertilization and development.
- Regulates cytoskeletal organization and vesicular transport.

## Disease associations
- Altered PI metabolism linked to cancer, diabetes, mood disorders, and neurodegenerative diseases.
- Mutations in enzymes of PI pathway (e.g., PIP3 synthesis) cause immunodeficiencies and developmental disorders.

## Therapeutic targets
- Kinases and phosphatases of the PI pathway are important drug targets (e.g., PI3K inhibitors in cancer therapy).
- Modulation of PI signalling has potential in many diseases.

## Key message
Although a minor membrane component, phosphatidylinositol is at the heart of cell signalling and metabolic regulation in all eukaryotes.
