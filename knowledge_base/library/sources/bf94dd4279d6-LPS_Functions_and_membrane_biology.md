# 1. Structure: Lysophosphatidylserine (LPS)

Name: 1-acyl-sn-glycero-3-phospho-L-serine
Abbrev.: LPS

## 3. LPS Signalling and Functions

### Receptors for LPS
GPR34 (LPS1); P2Y10 (LPS2); GPR174 (LPS3)

Diagram labels: G protein; Ca2+ flux ↑; Immune cell activation

### Special case
1-(11Z-eicosenoyl)-glycero-3-phosphoserine

True agonist of TLR2/6 heterodimer

### Key biological actions

#### Proinflammatory
- Augments mast cell degranulation
- Mast cell–dependent anaphylactic shock

#### In injured/damaged cells
NADPH oxidase activation
LPS generation
Mast cell
Enhances clearance of neutrophils → Resolution of inflammation

#### Immunomodulatory
GPR174 mediates suppression of T-cell proliferation

LPS (sn-2, often polyunsaturated acyl chain)

## 4. Roles in Health and Disease

### Generated after tissue injury
Tumour growth, graft rejection, burns, etc.

### Associated disorders
- Cancers
- Cardio-metabolic disorders
- Night blindness
- PHARC (genetic neurological disorder)

### Enzyme PLA1A (PS-PLA1A)
High serum levels linked to autoimmune diseases
- Graves' disease
- Systemic lupus erythematosus

Increased expression in metastatic melanomas

### Infections & viruses
- Schistosome-derived LPS participates in host metabolism
- Necessary for hepatitis C virus assembly
- Involved in antiviral innate immune response

## 5. Biophysical Property
Negatively charged LPS tends to form non-bilayer structures (e.g., micelles, inverted hexagonal phases).

These may facilitate folding of certain membrane proteins in situ better than bilayer-forming lipids.

Diagram labels: Bilayer-forming lipid; LPS (non-bilayer forming); Inverted hexagonal phase (HII)
