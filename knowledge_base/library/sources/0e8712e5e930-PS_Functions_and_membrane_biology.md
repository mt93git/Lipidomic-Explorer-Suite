# 1. PS Distribution and Membrane Asymmetry

## 1. PS Distribution and Membrane Asymmetry
Intracellular trafficking of PS

Diagram labels: ER; Golgi; Plasma membrane; PS synthesized on lumenal leaflet; ATP-independent flippases/scramblases at the trans Golgi network; Outer leaflet (extracellular); Inner leaflet (cytosolic); Phosphatidylserine (PS); Zwitterionic lipids (e.g., PC, PE, SM)

PS is highly enriched on the inner (cytosolic) leaflet of the plasma membrane, contributing to membrane charge, protein recruitment, and cellular organization.

## 2. Enzymes that Regulate PS Translocation
Enzymes controlling PS distribution

- Flippases (move PS inward): P4-ATPases (e.g., ATP11C, ATP8B1)
- Floppases (move PS outward): ABC transporters (e.g., ABCA1)
- Scramblases (bidirectional): TMEM16 family (e.g., TMEM16F); XKR family (e.g., XKR8)

Diagram labels: Outer leaflet; Inner leaflet; ATP; ADP + Pi

Maintain asymmetry: PS in inner leaflet
Collapse asymmetry: PS appears on outer leaflet

Scramblases have the greatest capacity and relative importance.

Floppase ABCA1 is vital for lipid efflux and plasma membrane remodelling; transports PS and cholesterol and helps retain cholesterol in the cytosolic leaflet to protect it from oxidation.

## 3. PS Functions

### Membrane charge & organization
- PS provides negative charge
- Recruits cations and proteins
- Organizes metabolic processes and membrane proteins

### Cofactor & enzyme activation
- Binds and activates many proteins
- Required for enzymes (e.g., nSMase, Na+/K+ ATPase)
- Spectrin and other PS-binding proteins dock on PS

Diagram labels: Ca2+

### Signaling platform
Diagram labels: PI3K pathway; PKC; PKC pathway
- PS binds C2 domains (with Ca2+) of PKC
- Works with DAG (C1 domain) to activate PKC
- In neurons, DHA-rich PS domains promote kinase activation, development and survival
- PS in inner nuclear membrane regulates enzymes for PC biosynthesis

### Membrane trafficking
PS regulates exocytosis and endocytosis

## 4. PS Externalization in Apoptosis (Efferocytosis)

### 1. Healthy cell (asymmetric membrane)
Low Ca2+
PS confined to inner leaflet (scramblase suppressed by membrane cholesterol)

### 2. Cell damage / Ca2+ increase
Ca2+
Elevated Ca2+ activates scramblases (TMEM16, XKR) and inhibits flippases (aminophospholipid translocases)

### 3. PS externalization
PS moves to outer leaflet and membrane asymmetry collapses

### 4. 'Eat-me' signal
Bridging molecules
Phagocyte
External PS is recognized by phagocytes via PS receptors

### 5. Clearance
Apoptotic cell is engulfed and removed without inflammation
