# LYSOPHOSPHATIDIC ACID (LPA)

## A simple glycerophospholipid and potent lipid mediator
Lysophosphatidic acid (1-acyl-sn-glycero-3-phosphate, LPA) is one of the simplest glycerophospholipids. Although present at very low levels in tissues, it is a central regulator and pleiotropic signalling molecule that controls proliferation, survival, migration, immune modulation and tissue remodelling in health and disease.

## Key features
- One fatty acyl chain esterified to glycerol (sn-1 or sn-2)
- Highly polar; exists as many molecular species (16:0–22:6; saturated or unsaturated; ester or ether-linked)
- Acts as a growth factor–like lipid mediator via specific receptors
- Biosynthetic precursor of phosphatidic acid (PA)
- Target for therapeutic drug development

## Features
- More hydrophilic than PA; additional –OH increases hydrogen bonding in membranes
- sn-1-acyl isomer is thermodynamically more stable
- Exists as many molecular species (e.g., 16:0, 18:0, 18:1, 20:4, 22:6; ether analogues)

## 2. BIOSYNTHESIS OF LPA

## A. Extracellular source: Autotaxin (ATX) pathway (in circulation)
Diagram labels: Lysophosphatidylcholine (LPC) (~200 µM in plasma); Autotaxin (ATX) (lysophospholipase D); + H2O; Lysophosphatidic acid (LPA) (~1–5 µM in serum; ~100 nM in plasma); + Choline

- ATX is secreted by platelets, adipocytes and many cell types
- LPA circulates mainly bound to albumin
- More abundant in serum due to platelet release of LPC during coagulation
- Binds to cells via integrins and heparan sulfate proteoglycans

## B. Intracellular biosynthetic pathways
Diagram labels: Phosphatidic acid (PA); PLA1 (PA-specific) → sn-2 LPA; PLA2 (PA hydrolase) → sn-1 LPA; 2-Acyl-LPA; 1-Acyl-LPA; Lipid phosphate phosphatase(s); Monoacylglycerol kinase (MGK); Monoacylglycerol (MAG); Glycerol-3-phosphate acyltransferase (GPAT) (first step in PA synthesis); LPA; sPLA2-IIA; Extracellular vesicle / platelet; LPA

- In platelets, PA hydrolysis by cytosolic PLA2 (cPLA2) is the major route
- Secretory PLA2-IIA (sPLA2-IIA) can release LPA from PA at the surface of extracellular vesicles (e.g., from platelets, Ca2+-loaded erythrocytes)
- Cytokines (e.g., TNF-α, IL-1β) stimulate LPA release

## 3. GENERAL FUNCTIONS OF LPA

## Broad biological actions
- Cell proliferation & survival
- Cell migration & invasion
- Tissue remodelling (fibroblast & smooth muscle contraction)
- Cytoskeletal rearrangement
- Secretion of cytokines & chemokines
- Immune modulation
- Neuronal development
- Angiogenesis

## (untitled panel)
1-O-alkyl/alkenyl-LPA analogues (e.g., from platelet-activating factor) exert similar activities.

## Physical & biochemical properties underlying function
- Cone-shaped; promotes negative membrane curvature and non-bilayer phases
- Facilitates membrane fusion, vesicle trafficking, secretion and endocytosis
- Net negative charge; interacts electrostatically with basic residues of target proteins
- Acts as a cellular pH sensor (binding depends on protonation state of phosphate)
- Fatty acyl composition affects activity e.g., 16:0 LPA inhibits insulin aggregation (protective) whereas 18:0 LPA promotes it; 18:1 LPA has different effects.

## Fate of LPA in cells
- Precursor of phosphatidic acid (acylation by LPAAT)
- Deacylated by lipid phosphate phosphatases
- Both LPA and diacylglycerol (DG) can be converted back to PA by DGKs

## 5. ROLE IN PHYSIOLOGY AND DISEASE

## Physiological roles
- Embryonic development (vascular, neural, skeletal)
- Wound healing & tissue repair
- Vascular homeostasis & blood pressure regulation
- Immune cell trafficking & resolution of inflammation
- Reproductive functions (ovulation, fertility)

## Associated with disease
- Cancer (proliferation, invasion, metastasis, angiogenesis)
- Fibrosis
- Inflammatory diseases (e.g., arthritis, asthma, colitis)
- Neurological disorders
- Cardiovascular disease
- Pulmonary hypertension
- Kidney disease

## Therapeutic implications
- Inhibitors of autotaxin (ATX) in clinical trials for cancer, fibrosis and inflammation
- Antagonists of LPA receptors (LPARs) under investigation
- Modulation of DG kinases, LPA acyltransferases and phosphatases as strategies
