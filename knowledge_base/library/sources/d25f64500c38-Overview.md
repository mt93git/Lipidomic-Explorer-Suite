# SPHINGOMYELIN

## (untitled panel)
Sphingomyelin (SM) or ceramide 1-phosphocholine is the sphingolipid analogue of phosphatidylcholine. It is zwitterionic and the most abundant sphingolipid in animal cells. It serves as a reservoir for signalling molecules upon stimulation.

## STRUCTURE OF SPHINGOMYELIN (SM)
Diagram labels: Ceramide; Representative molecular species d18:1/16:0 (Lipid Maps: SM(1) 18:1(4E):3OH/16:0); Sphingoid base (d18:1); Fatty acid (16:0); Phosphorylcholine (head group)
- Zwitterionic at physiological pH: positively charged choline head group and negatively charged phosphate.
- Most stable sphingolipid in membranes; precursor for important signalling molecules.

## 1. OCCURRENCE AND COMPOSITION OF SPHINGOMYELIN
- Can comprise up to 50% or more of total lipids in some tissues.
- ~10% of brain lipids (major myelin constituent); ~70% of phospholipids in human lens.
- Highest in plasma membrane, lysosomes, trans Golgi network and nucleus.
- All SM in erythrocytes is in the outer leaflet; ~90% in nucleated cell plasma membranes.
- Present in all lipoprotein fractions; higher in VLDL/LDL.
- Composed of sphingoid bases (mainly sphingosine, sphinganine, C20 homologues) linked to very-long-chain fatty acids.
- ~100 molecular species identified in human plasma.
- Polyunsaturated fatty acids are rare (exception: testes, spermatozoa).

## Fatty acid characteristics
- Palmitic acid (16:0) most common in peripheral tissues.
- Stearic acid (18:0) abundant in neural tissue.
- Brain (grey matter): ~60% 18:0.
- Brain (white matter): 60% 24:0 + 24:1; increases during first 2 years of life as 18:0 decreases (82% → 33%).
- Testes and spermatozoa: very-long-chain polyunsaturated FAs (up to C34), mainly 28:4(n-6) and 30:5(n-6); some 2-hydroxy FAs.

## Sphingoid base characteristics
- Sphingosine (d18:1) usually most abundant.
- Sphinganine (d18:0) major base in human lens (mainly 16:0).
- Bases range from C16 to C26.
- Ruminants: contain unusual bases (e.g., d17:1, d17:1-methyl).
- Nematode C. elegans: iso-methyl branched bases with long/very-long-chain saturated FAs (often methyl-branched).

## BIOSYNTHETIC RELATIONSHIPS
Diagram labels: Ceramide; Sphingomyelin synthase (SMS); CDP-choline; CMP; Sphingomyelin (SM); SMases (sphingomyelinases); H2O; Ceramide + Phosphorylcholine

SMases hydrolyse sphingomyelin → ceramide + phosphorylcholine.
Ceramide is further metabolized to sphingosine, ceramide-1-phosphate, sphingosine-1-phosphate, etc.

## KEY FUNCTIONS
- Major structural lipid of cell membranes. Contributes to membrane stability, integrity and mechanical resilience.
- Forms lipid rafts with cholesterol; organizes membrane microdomains for signalling.
- Important for myelin sheath formation and nuclear chromatin structure.
- Reservoir for signalling molecules (ceramide, sphingosine, S1P) upon stress or stimulation.

## WHERE FOUND (relative abundance)
- Plasma membrane (10–20% of lipids)
- Lysosomes / endocytic recycling (high)
- Trans Golgi network (high)
- Nucleus (chromatin) (main phospholipid)
- Endoplasmic reticulum (low, 2–4%)
- Mitochondria (very low)

## EVOLUTION AND DISTRIBUTION
- Ubiquitous in animals (mammals, birds, fish, nematodes, and some protozoa).
- Not synthesized in plants, fungi or bacteria.
- Many bacteria and viruses utilize sphingomyelin or its metabolites from animal hosts.
- In ruminant erythrocytes, SM replaces phosphatidylcholine almost entirely.
- Evolutionary advantage: confers membrane stability and specialized signalling.

## SUMMARY OF ROLES AND IMPORTANCE

## Membrane structure and stability
- Tight packing with cholesterol.
- Reduces permeability to water and solutes.
- Essential for mechanical strength of membranes.

## Myelin and neural function
- Major lipid of myelin sheath.
- Important for nerve conduction and protection.

## Lipid raft organization and signalling
- Forms rafts with cholesterol.
- Organizes receptors and signalling proteins.

## Reservoir for signalling molecules
- Source of ceramide, sphingosine, C1P, S1P after stimulation.
- Involved in apoptosis, inflammation, stress responses.

## Metabolic integration
- Synthesized de novo (ER, Golgi).
- Interconverts with ceramide.
- Transported in lipoproteins.

## KEY TAKEAWAY MESSAGE
Sphingomyelin is a ubiquitous, zwitterionic sphingophospholipid that is essential for membrane integrity, myelin formation, and signalling. It acts as both a structural lipid and a metabolic reservoir whose composition is highly regulated and tissue– and species-specific.

## Clinical relevance
- Altered SM metabolism linked to neurodegeneration (e.g., Niemann–Pick disease), cardiovascular disease, infection, cancer, and metabolic disorders.
- Target of therapeutic strategies (SMase inhibitors, etc.).
