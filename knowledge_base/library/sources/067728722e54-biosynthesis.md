# Biosynthesis of Monoglycosylceramides

## (untitled panel)
Ceramides are synthesized in the endoplasmic reticulum (ER) de novo or by sphingomyelin catabolism. Monoglycosylceramides are formed by transfer of a sugar from a UDP-sugar to ceramide by specific glycosyltransferases with inversion of configuration (α → β).

## A. GALACTOSYLCERAMIDE BIOSYNTHESIS
Diagram labels: UDP-galactose; Ceramide; Endoplasmic reticulum (luminal surface); UDP-galactose:ceramide galactosyltransferase (GalCer synthase); β-D-Galactosylceramide (GalCer); Lumen; Cytosol; Flip-flop (energy-independent); Transport to trans-Golgi; Golgi

GalCer synthase is expressed in oligodendrocytes, Schwann cells, kidneys and testes.

Further modification

- Sulfatide
- GM4 ganglioside
- Gal2Cer and other gala-series

## B. GLUCOSYLCERAMIDE BIOSYNTHESIS
Diagram labels: Endoplasmic reticulum (luminal surface); Ceramide; CERT; Transfer of ceramide to cytosolic leaflet of early Golgi (CERT protein); Early Golgi (cytosolic leaflet); UDP-glucose; Glucosylceramide synthase (GlcCer synthase); β-D-Glucosylceramide (GlcCer); Cytosol; Translocation to luminal leaflet of trans-Golgi; GLTP; FAPP2; ATP10A/ATP10D; ATP; ADP + Pi; Transport to plasma membrane; Trans-Golgi (luminal leaflet); Plasma membrane (outer leaflet)

GlcCer synthase is present in early Golgi membranes (except possibly neuronal tissues).

Distinct enzymes and pathways

- GalCer and GlcCer synthases have no sequence homology.
- Human flippases ATP10A/D are specific for GlcCer, not GalCer.

Non-vesicular transport by P4-ATPases (ATP10A, ATP10D) with FAPP2 and GLTP conserves energy from ATP to flip GlcCer to luminal leaflet.

## C. BIOSYNTHESIS IN PLANTS AND FUNGI
### PLANTS
Diagram labels: ER; Golgi; Plasma membrane (outer leaflet)

- GlcCer is synthesized in the ER by an evolutionarily conserved glucosylceramide synthase (GCS1) using UDP-glucose and ceramide.
- Alternative pathway: sterol glucoside can donate glucose to ceramide.
- After transport through the Golgi, GlcCer is delivered to the outer leaflet of the plasma membrane.
- Expression of GCS1 increases during phosphate deficiency.
- Ceramides with Δ4 trans-double bonds are preferred for GlcCer synthesis in some species.

### FUNGI (e.g., yeast)
Diagram labels: ER; Golgi; Vesicles; Plasma membrane and cell wall; Secretion (extracellular vesicles)

- In yeast (Pichia pastoris):
  - Ceramide synthase 1 produces ceramides specifically for GlcCer.
  - Ceramide synthase 2 produces ceramides for ceramide phosphorylinositol (contains phytosphingosine).
- GlcCer synthases (e.g., Hsx11 in Candida albicans) synthesize GlcCer, which is exported by vesicular trafficking to plasma membrane and cell wall, or secreted in vesicles.
- A galactosylceramide synthase has not yet been identified.

## KEY POINTS
- Monoglycosylceramides are synthesized by direct transfer of a sugar from UDP-sugars to ceramide with inversion of configuration (α → β).
- Galactosylceramide is made in the ER (luminal side) by GalCer synthase and trafficked to the trans-Golgi for further modification.
- Glucosylceramide is made on the cytosolic side of early Golgi by GlcCer synthase after ceramide transfer via CERT; it must be flipped to the luminal side of trans-Golgi by ATP10A/D–FAPP2–GLTP.
- Both GalCer and GlcCer are transported to and across the plasma membrane for function and signaling; some GlcCer circulates on lipoproteins (VLDL, LDL, HDL).
- In plants and fungi, GlcCer is synthesized in the ER and delivered to the plasma membrane (and cell wall in fungi); no GalCer synthase has been identified.
- Specific enzymes and transport systems ensure the distinct localization and handling of GalCer and GlcCer in cells.
