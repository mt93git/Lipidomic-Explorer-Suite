# 5. THE PHOSPHATIDYLINOSITOL CYCLE

## (untitled panel)
Phosphatidylinositol (PI) is at the centre of a dynamic cycle of inter-converting lipids that drives cell signalling and membrane homeostasis. Enzymes in the endoplasmic reticulum (ER) and plasma membrane (PM) work together, and phosphoinositides are shuttled between membranes by phosphatidylinositol transfer proteins (PITPs) to complete the cycle.

## (untitled panel)
Diagram labels: PLASMA MEMBRANE; PI(45)P2; PI4P5K (5-kinase); PI(4,5)P2; DAG; CYTOSOL; IP3 (soluble); PITP (Nir2); DGKε (at contact sites); ENDOPLASMIC RETICULUM; PI; CDP-DAG; PI synthase (PIS); CMP; CDP-diacylglycerol synthase (CDS2); CDP; PPi; PA; sn-1; sn-2

## ENERGY REQUIREMENT PER TURN OF THE CYCLE
Diagram labels: 3 ATP (for PI4K, PI4P5K, DGK); CTP; myo-Inositol; Energetic diphosphate (PPi); Pyrophosphatase; 2 Pi (inorganic phosphate)

## KEY POINTS
- PLC and PI4P5K are in the plasma membrane; CDS2 and PI synthase are in the ER.
- PITPs shuttle phosphoinositides between membranes to maintain the cycle.
- Each turn consumes 3 ATP, CTP and inositol; hydrolysis of PPi drives the cycle forward.
- DAG can be converted to PA, which returns to the ER for reuse in PI synthesis.
- Numerous branches link to other lipid pathways and signalling cascades.

## MAJOR BRANCHES AND OUTPUTS
- DAG → activates PKC; precursor of TAG.
- IP3 → releases Ca2+ from ER stores.
- PA → precursor of PC, PE, PS.
- Arachidonic acid release → eicosanoids.

## (untitled panel)
The phosphatidylinositol cycle integrates membrane trafficking, lipid remodelling, second messenger production and cross-talk with other lipid pathways to sustain signalling and membrane composition.

# 6. LYSO-PHOSPHOINOSITIDES AND OTHER RELEVANT LIPIDS

## A. Lysophosphatidylinositols (LPI)
Diagram labels: sn-1; sn-2; sn-3
- Intermediates in remodelling by PLA1 or PLA2 (e.g., cPLA2α).
- sn-2-Arachidonoyl-LPI is an endogenous ligand for GPR55. → activates kinases → promotes cancer cell proliferation, migration and metastasis.
- Biomarker for poor prognosis in cancer (↑ in ascites).
- Present in brain, intestine, pancreatic islets (may trigger insulin release); involved in inflammation and obesity.
- Hydrolysed by glycerophosphodiesterase 3 (lysophospholipase C) → 2-arachidonoylglycerol (endocannabinoid).
- LPI induces insulin release from pancreatic β-cells.

LPI species with phosphates (e.g., LPI(4)P, LPI(3,4,5)P3) may also act as signalling molecules.

## B. Glycerophosphoinositol (GPI)
- Formed by removal of both acyl chains from PI by PLA2IVα.
- Can be dephosphorylated to inositol 1-phosphate.
- Possesses anti-inflammatory activity.
- Inhibits inflammatory and thrombotic responses to bacterial lipopolysaccharides (endotoxins).

## C. Diol-containing phosphoinositides (in bacteria)
Diagram labels: Inositol
- Found in Thermomicrobia species.
- 1,2-Diol backbone has same stereochemistry as sn-glycerol-3-phosphate.
- C17–C23 straight or branched-chain saturated fatty acids at position 2.

## D. Other relevant lipids

## GPI-anchored proteins
Diagram labels: Protein; PI anchor
- PI anchors link a wide variety of proteins to the external leaflet via a glycosyl bridge.

## Lipophosphoglycans
Diagram labels: Man; Ara; Gal; Inositol
- Include lipoarabinomannans and arabinogalactans.
- Components of protozoan and bacterial membranes.

## PI mannosides
Diagram labels: Man
- Glycanated PI lipids in parasites and bacteria.
- Important for cell structure, adherence and virulence.

## OVERALL SIGNIFICANCE
Lysophosphoinositides, glycerophosphoinositol and related lipids are not merely by-products of PI metabolism. They act as signalling molecules, modulators of inflammation and metabolism, and structural anchors that influence cell communication, growth and host–pathogen interactions.
