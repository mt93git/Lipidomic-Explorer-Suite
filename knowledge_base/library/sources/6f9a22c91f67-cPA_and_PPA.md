# 6. CYCLIC PHOSPHATIDIC ACID (cPA)

## A. Structure
Cyclic phosphatidic acid (cPA) (cyclic lysophosphatidic acid)

Diagram labels: sn-1; sn-2; sn-3; R = fatty acyl chain (main species in humans = 16:0 with 18:0, 18:1, 18:2); Cyclic phosphate linking sn-2 and sn-3 positions is essential for activity.

## B. Occurrence
- Isolated originally from a slime mould.
- Detected in many organisms including humans.
- In humans: brain and serum (albumin-bound) – concentration ~10-7 M (≈ one tenth of LPA)
- Most abundant in tissues subject to injury.
- Unusual plasmenyl-cPA isolated from Bifidobacterium longum subs. infantis.

Diagram labels: Brain; Serum (albumin-bound); Injury tissues

## C. Biosynthesis

### 1. Enzymes involved
In humans (in cells)
GDE7 (Ca2+-dependent lysophospholipase D)

Diagram labels: Luminal side of ER

In serum / extracellular
Autotaxin (ATX) (lysophospholipase D)

Substrates
- Lysophosphatidylcholine (LPC)
- (Also sphingomyelin in some microorganisms)

Diagram labels: Lysophosphatidylcholine (LPC); Intramolecular transphosphatidylation (cyclization); Cyclic phosphatidic acid (cPA)

### Other organisms
- Bacterial, fungal and spider enzymes akin to sphingomyelinases D catalyse cyclization.
- Strong acid treatment of serum can generate cPA artefactually.

## D. Biological activities and functions

### Similarities to LPA
Binds to some of the same receptors (e.g., LPAR1–3)

Activates G protein signalling pathways

### Distinct and unique functions
- Inhibits DNA polymerase α
- Inhibits platelet aggregation induced by LPA (possibly by inhibiting ATX)
- High-affinity ligand for PPARγ
- Inhibits cancer cell invasion & metastasis
- Enhances cell survival & neurite extension
- Neuroprotective (against apoptosis)
- Beneficial in animal model of multiple sclerosis
- Attenuates neuropathic pain & relieves osteoarthritis

### Other beneficial effects
- Improves skin hydration
- Stimulates hyaluronic acid synthesis (extracellular matrix)

## Therapeutic potential
- Derivatives ('2- and 3-carba-cPA', methylene analogues) are stable and in clinical trials.
- Promising target for treatment of cancer, neurological disorders and inflammation.

# 7. PYROPHOSPHATIDIC ACID (PPA)

## A. Structure
Pyrophosphatidic acid (PPA) (sn-1,2-diacylglycero-3-pyrophosphate)

Diagram labels: Pyrophosphate at sn-3 position (phosphoanhydride bond)

## B. Occurrence
- First identified as a minor component in yeasts.
- Present in mushrooms and higher plants.
- Due to phosphatidic acid kinase (PAK), an enzyme present in all plant tissues, especially in the plasma membrane.

Diagram labels: Yeasts; Mushrooms; Higher plants

## C. Metabolism and role
Diagram labels: Phosphatidic acid (PA); PA kinase (PAK); Pyrophosphatidic acid (PPA); Phosphatase; Phosphatidic acid (PA); End product Diacylglycerol (DG)

### Functions and significance
- Rapidly metabolized back to PA by a phosphatase and thence to diacylglycerols (DG).
- May be part of the phospholipase D signalling cascade in plants.
- Possibly attenuates the effects of phosphatidic acid.

## D. Role as a stress signal in plants

### Stress-induced increase
Barely detectable under normal conditions, but its concentration increases very rapidly in response to stress situations:
- Osmotic stress (drought, salinity)
- Pathogen attack
- Wounding / mechanical stress

### Physiological roles
- Acts as a signalling molecule in plants under stress.
- Often linked to abscisic acid (ABA) responses.

Diagram labels: Abscisic acid (ABA)

### Possible downstream effects
- Modulates gene expression
- Regulates stomatal closure
- Enhances stress tolerance
- Adjusts lipid metabolism for membrane adaptation

## E. Role in yeasts and other organisms
- May have a role in the regulation of synthesis and metabolism of phospholipids, e.g., phosphatidylserine (PS) in yeasts.
- Detected in some bacteria; function less well understood.

## Key points
- PPA is an unusual phospholipid with a phosphoanhydride bond.
- Formed by PA kinase; rapidly dephosphorylated to PA.
- Functions as a stress-responsive signalling lipid in plants.
- Very low levels under normal conditions.
