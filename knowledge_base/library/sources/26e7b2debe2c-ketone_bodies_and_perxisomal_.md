# 4. KETONE BODIES

## (untitled panel)
During fasting, prolonged exercise, low-carbohydrate intake and uncontrolled type 1 diabetes, the liver converts acetyl-CoA (from fatty acid β-oxidation) into ketone bodies. These water-soluble molecules are exported to extrahepatic tissues where they are reconverted to acetyl-CoA for energy production and other biosynthetic processes.

## KETONE BODIES
Diagram labels: Acetoacetate (3-oxobutanoate); β-Hydroxybutyrate (3-hydroxybutyrate); Acetone (propanone)

Acetone is produced spontaneously from acetoacetate (decarboxylation).

## SOURCES AND PHYSIOLOGICAL ROLES
- Produced in liver mitochondria from acetyl-CoA during fasting, starvation, prolonged exercise, low-carbohydrate diet, or uncontrolled diabetes mellitus (ketosis).
- Major fuel for brain, heart, skeletal muscle and kidney during low glucose availability.
- Provide acetyl-CoA for fatty acid and cholesterol synthesis in extrahepatic tissues.
- Transported in blood as water-soluble anions (acetoacetate, β-hydroxybutyrate).
- Excessive production → ketoacidosis (low blood pH, dangerous).

## KETOGENESIS (in liver mitochondria)

## UTILIZATION OF KETONE BODIES (extrahepatic tissues)
Diagram labels: Acetoacetate; Acetoacetyl-CoA synthetase; CoA-SH + ATP; AMP + PPi; Acetoacetyl-CoA; Thiolase; Acetyl-CoA + Acetyl-CoA

- Acetyl-CoA enters tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle → ATP.
- Acetyl-CoA can also be used for fatty acid or cholesterol synthesis.

## KEY POINTS
- Ketone bodies are produced only in liver mitochondria.
- Extrahepatic tissues lack HMG-CoA lyase → cannot synthesise ketone bodies but can use them.
- Transport form: acetoacetate (anion) and β-hydroxybutyrate (anion); acetone is volatile and excreted in breath.
- Ketogenic diets ↑ ketone production; therapeutic uses in epilepsy and some metabolic disorders.
- Signalling roles: ligands for GPCRs; regulate gene expression (epigenetic modifications); influence inflammation and immunity.

## CLINICAL RELEVANCE
- Elevated ketones (ketosis) in fasting, diabetes, alcoholism, pregnancy.
- Excess → ketoacidosis: high ketones, low bicarbonate, metabolic acidosis.
- Urine/blood ketone tests used clinically.
- Defects in ketone body utilisation: e.g., SCOT deficiency → ketosis with hypoketotic hypoglycaemia.

## 5. PEROXISOMAL β-OXIDATION IN ANIMALS
Peroxisomes oxidize very-long-chain (≥C24), methyl-branched and dicarboxylic fatty acids and perform the first steps of α-oxidation. They also carry out an essential step in ether lipid biosynthesis and detoxify reactive oxygen species.

## KEY FEATURES
- Occurs in peroxisome matrix; not coupled to ATP synthesis.
- Uses acyl-CoA oxidase (ACOX1/2/3) which transfers electrons directly to O2 → H2O2.
- Two bifunctional proteins (DBP, LBP) catalyse hydration and dehydrogenation steps.
- β-Ketothiolase (with CoA-acyltransferase activity) performs thiolysis.
- Shortens acyl chains to ~C8 (octanoate) → exported to mitochondria for further β-oxidation.
- Generates acetyl-CoA, which cannot enter TCA cycle in peroxisome; exported as acetylcarnitine or acetate.
- Major roles in lipid metabolism, ROS detoxification and plasmalogen (ether lipid) synthesis.

## Export of products
- Acetyl-CoA → converted to acetylcarnitine (by CrAT) or acetate → to mitochondria for oxidation.
- H2O2 → detoxified by catalase in peroxisome.

## Substrates and functions
- Preferred substrates: ≥C24 fatty acids, pristanic acid, phytanic acid precursors, dicarboxylic acids.
- Monomethyl branched fatty acids → rapid turnover in brown adipose tissue (thermogenesis).
- Essential for α-oxidation of phytanic acid (Refsum disease if impaired).

## CLINICAL AND DIAGNOSTIC ASPECTS
- Peroxisomal β-oxidation disorders → accumulation of VLCFAs.
- Biomarkers: ↑ plasma 24:0- and 26:0-lysophosphatidylcholines.
- Associated diseases: Zellweger spectrum disorders, X-linked adrenoleukodystrophy.
- Peroxisome biogenesis disorders → ↑ dicarboxylic acid carnitine esters in plasma.
- Protects against lipid accumulation in neurological diseases.

## ADDITIONAL PATHWAYS IN PEROXISOMES
- α-Oxidation: removes methyl group at α-carbon → shortens methyl-branched fatty acids (e.g., phytanic acid).
- ω-Oxidation (minor): fatty acids → ω-hydroxy fatty acids → dicarboxylic acids can be esterified with carnitine.
- Ether lipid biosynthesis: dihydroxyacetone phosphate acyltransferase step occurs in peroxisomes.
- ROS metabolism: catalase converts H2O2 → H2O + O2.
