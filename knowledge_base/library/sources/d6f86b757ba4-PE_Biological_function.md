# 3. PHOSPHATIDYLETHANOLAMINE – BIOLOGICAL FUNCTION

## (untitled panel)
Phosphatidylethanolamine (PE) is a major membrane phospholipid with unique physical properties and diverse biological functions. It plays essential roles in membrane organization, protein interactions, organelle function, lipid metabolism and cellular homeostasis.

## A. PHYSICAL PROPERTIES OF PE
## 1. Structure and shape
PE has a small head group (phosphoethanolamine) → cone shape

Diagram labels: Choline (PC); Ethanolamine (PE)

## 2. Membrane behavior
- Does not form bilayers by itself; favors inverted hexagonal (HII) phases.
- In bilayers, PE exerts lateral pressure that promotes curvature.
- Stabilizes membrane proteins in their optimal conformations.
- Forms hydrogen bonds:
  – To adjacent lipid acyl chains
  – To proteins via head group (phosphate interacts with Lys, Arg or Tyr side chains)

Diagram labels: Cone shape → negative curvature; Inverted hexagonal (HII) phase

## 3. Asymmetric distribution
- Enriched in the inner leaflet of plasma membrane (with PS).
- Present in both leaflets of mitochondrial membranes; highly enriched in inner mitochondrial membrane.

Diagram labels: Plasma membrane; Outer leaflet (PC, SM); Inner leaflet (PE, PS, PI); Mitochondrial membranes; Outer membrane (PE present); Inner membrane (high PE); PC; PE; PS; PI; SM; Cholesterol; Hydrogen bond; Electrostatic interaction

## B. PROTEIN INTERACTIONS AND FUNCTIONS
## 1. Positive-inside rule and charge balance
- Positively charged residues are more abundant on cytosolic side of membrane proteins.
- PE helps balance charge density at the cytosolic surface.
- PE can inhibit location of negative residues on cytosolic side, but can allow them when required for function.

Diagram labels: Outer (non-cytosolic) side; Cytosolic (inner) side

## 2. Protein binding to PE
Diagram labels: Lys (NH3+); Arg (NH2+); Tyr (OH); Hydrogen bond; Electrostatic interaction

## 3. Specific protein targets and roles
Mitochondrial proteins
Essential for oxidative phosphorylation (e.g., cytochrome bc1 complex, complex III). PE binds a conserved site in the bc1 complex subunit.

PE-binding proteins (PEBPs)
Superfamily (>400 members from bacteria to humans)
Functions: lipid binding, neuronal development, serine protease inhibition, signalling regulation.
PEBP4 is highly expressed in many cancers → increases therapy resistance.
BLTP2 (Vps13-like) transport protein also implicated in cancer resistance.

Ca2+-ATPase
One PE molecule binds in cavity between two transmembrane helices → acts as a wedge. Displaced when Ca2+ binds.

Other PE-binding proteins
Rhodopsin, aquaporins, Annexin V (binds PE when translocated to outer leaflet and can inhibit blood coagulation by competing with PS).

## C. ROLES IN LIPID METABOLISM AND MEMBRANE DYNAMICS
## 1. Lipoprotein metabolism
- PE content is high in nascent VLDL and in apo B-containing particles in the Golgi lumen.
- Participates in VLDL assembly and/or secretion.
- Rapidly and efficiently removed from circulating VLDL.

## 2. Lipid droplet dynamics
- PE promotes coalescence of small lipid droplets into larger ones.
- PE synthesis increases in brown adipocytes (and liver) during cold exposure.

## 3. Membrane fusion and fission
- PE promotes negative membrane curvature.
- Facilitates membrane fusion, vesicle trafficking, and fission events.
- Important in exocytosis and endocytosis.

## 4. Membrane contact sites (ER–mitochondria)
- PE is enriched at mitochondria-associated membranes (MAMs).
- Involved in lipid transfer and organelle communication.

Diagram labels: ER

## 5. Prion propagation
- PE is the primary lipid factor required in brain for propagation and infectivity of mammalian prions.
- Proposed to promote conformational conversion of prion protein.

## D. OTHER BIOLOGICAL ROLES
## 1. Antimicrobial peptides (host defence)
- Many host defence peptides preferentially target membranes rich in PE.
- PE enriched in bacterial inner (cytoplasmic) leaflet.
- Contributes to antimicrobial activity.

## 2. Autophagy
- PE is essential for the expansion and closure of autophagosome membranes.
- Especially critical during starvation.

## 3. Calcium signalling
- PE modulates Ca2+ channel activity.
- Influences intracellular Ca2+ signalling and homeostasis.

Diagram labels: Ca2+

## 4. Membrane fluidity and rigidity
- Like cholesterol, PE increases membrane rigidity in eukaryotic cells (e.g., insects) to maintain optimal fluidity.

## 5. Stress and disease
- PE composition and distribution change in stress, ischemia, and many diseases.
- Important in heart during ischemia-reperfusion.

## E. SUMMARY OF KEY FUNCTIONS
Diagram labels: PE; Structural role: Major component of membranes; regulates curvature and fluidity; Organelle function: Critical for mitochondria, autophagy, and ER–mitochondria contact site integrity; Protein interactions: Binds many proteins; essential for mitochondrial protein function; Cell signalling: Modulates Ca2+ signalling, membrane proteins, and signal transduction; Lipid metabolism: Required for VLDL assembly, lipid droplet dynamics, and membrane trafficking; Host defence & disease: Important in antimicrobial activity, prion propagation, and disease responses

## Key take-home messages
- PE has unique physical properties that influence membrane structure and protein function.
- It is indispensable for mitochondrial efficiency and membrane trafficking.
- It interacts with a wide range of proteins, including PE-binding proteins with roles in health and disease.
- It is crucial for lipid metabolism, autophagy, immunity, and cellular homeostasis.
- Alterations in PE metabolism and distribution are linked to many human diseases.
