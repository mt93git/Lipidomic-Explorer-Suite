## FUNCTIONS OF TRIACYLGLYCEROLS
- Energy store (released as fatty acids)
- Reserve of fatty acids for membranes and precursors of lipid mediators
- Protective sequestration of potentially harmful lipids (FA, DAG, oxylipins, cholesterol esters, retinol esters, CoA esters, etc.)

## EXCESS ACCUMULATION & HEALTH RISKS
- Obesity
- Insulin resistance
- Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease
- Cardiomyopathy
- Some cancers

## KEY POINTS
- Triacylglycerols are triesters of glycerol.
- Positions sn-1 and sn-3 are primary (α and α′); sn-2 is secondary (β).
- Positional distribution of fatty acids differs among species and is influenced by diet and biosynthesis.
- sn-2 fatty acid is released as 2-monoacylglycerol during digestion.
- Triacylglycerols are essential for energy storage but can cause disease when in excess.

- Present in most cell types, including brain, liver, intestine, adipose tissue, seeds, yeasts and mycobacteria.

- Participate in lipid metabolism

## 2.1. BIOSYNTHESIS OF TRIACYLGLYCEROLS
Three main pathways: (1) sn-glycerol-3-phosphate (Kennedy), (2) dihydroxyacetone phosphate, (3) monoacylglycerol (intestine). A fourth route via DGAT is found in seeds and some tissues.

## Lipins (PAPs)
- Three related cytosolic proteins: lipin-1, lipin-2, lipin-3.
- Translocate to ER membranes when fatty acid levels are elevated.
- Lipin-1 (main in adipose tissue & muscle); lipin-2 (abundant in liver); lipin-3 (GI tract, liver, overlaps with others).
- Lipin-1α can activate PGC-1α → ↑ mitochondrial biogenesis and fatty acid oxidation → maintains lipid homeostasis.

## GENERAL PATTERNS
- Saturated fatty acids mainly in position sn-1.
- Unsaturated fatty acids (especially 18:2) mainly in position sn-2.
- Longer-chain fatty acids enriched in position sn-3.
- Pig and related species are major exceptions.

Diagram labels: 1; 2; 3; Mostly sat. FA; Mostly unsat. FA; Longer-chain FA

## DIETARY AND PHYSIOLOGICAL INFLUENCES
- Diet composition strongly influences fatty acid composition.
- Subcutaneous fats contain more unsaturation than internal depot fats.
- Ruminants: biohydrogenation in rumen → more saturated & trans FA; more odd- and branched-chain FA from microflora.
- Marine animals: high long-chain mono- and polyenoic FA.
- In vitro (rat adipocytes): exogenous FA esterified to all positions; de novo FA → sn-1 and sn-2; desaturated FA → mainly sn-3.

## SPECIAL NOTES
- Freeze-tolerant insect Eurosta solidaginis synthesizes acetylated TAGs in winter → remain liquid at low temperatures for energy storage.
- Aphids secrete TAGs on body surface:
  - sn-1 and sn-3: myristic acid (14:0)
  - sn-2: short-chain FA, incl. sorbic acid (2,4-hexadienoic acid)

## KEY TAKE-HOME MESSAGE
Triacylglycerols are the major energy storage lipids in animals. The positional distribution of fatty acids is species-specific, influenced by diet and biosynthesis, and crucial for lipid metabolism and health.
