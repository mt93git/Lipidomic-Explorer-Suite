# CARDIOLIPIN (DIPHOSPHATIDYLGLYCEROL)

## (untitled panel)
Cardiolipin is a unique phospholipid with a dimeric structure containing four acyl chains, five chiral centres and up to two negative charges. It is essential for the function of mitochondria and certain bacterial membranes.

## 1. STRUCTURE AND COMPOSITION
Structure of cardiolipin (tetralinoleoyl species)

Diagram labels: sn-1 (1A); sn-2 (2A); sn-2' (2B); sn-1' (1B)

R = R' = linoleoyl (18:2Δ9,12)

Cardiolipin = 1,3-bis(sn-3'-phosphatidyl)-sn-glycerol (trivial name for diphosphatidylglycerol)

## Occurrence
- Found almost exclusively in mitochondrial inner membrane of eukaryotes (20% of mitochondrial phospholipids).
- Also in bacterial plasma membranes and hydrogenosomes; in some Archaea (halocarchaea).
- Significant in peroxisomes; occasionally in other organelles and body fluids.

## Compositional features
- Four acyl chains → potential for great complexity, but compositions are remarkably simple and symmetric.
- Most mammals: ~80% linoleic acid (18:2) in total; 18-carbon fatty acids predominate.
- Relatively resistant to dietary manipulation.

## Fatty acid composition in selected organisms (major features)
- Mammals (general): Mostly 18-carbon; ~80% linoleic acid (18:2).
- Bovine heart: Enriched in α-linolenic acid (18:3 n-3).
- Testis: Mainly palmitic acid (16:0).
- Central nervous system: Many fatty acids: 16:0, 18:0, 18:1, 18:2, 20:4, 22:6 (DHA); >100 molecular species.
- Marine animals: Contain DHA (22:6 n-3) or 24:6; e.g., zebrafish ~45% DHA; some molluscs ~80% DHA.
- Fruit fly (Drosophila melanogaster): 16:1 and 18:2 are major fatty acids.
- Higher plants: Linolenic (18:3) and linoleic (18:2) predominate; Arabidopsis leaf/seed ~61/58% 18:3; 35/37% 18:2 (major species with 10–11 double bonds).
- Yeast (S. cerevisiae): Mainly 16:1 and 18:1.
- Fungus (A. fumigatus): Mainly 18:2, 16:0 and 18:1.
- Bacteria (e.g., E. coli, B. drentensis): Saturated and monoenoic (14:0–18:1); some have plasmalogen (alk-1-enyl) forms.
- Sulfate-reducing bacterium (D. alkenivorans): Contains mono- to tetraether forms with saturated straight or methyl-branched chains.

## Functions (overview)
- Stabilizes mitochondrial structure and curvature.
- Essential for oxidative phosphorylation.
- Binds and organizes respiratory supercomplexes.
- Involved in apoptosis signalling.
- Required for mitochondrial dynamics (fusion/fission) and mitophagy.

## 2. BIOSYNTHESIS IN EUKARYOTES (MITOCHONDRIAL INNER MEMBRANE)
Diagram labels: Phosphatidic acid (PA); CDP-diacylglycerol (CDP-DAG); Phosphatidylglycerol phosphate (PGP); CDP-diacylglycerol synthase (CDS2); Phosphatidylglycerol phosphate synthase (PGPS); Cardiolipin synthase (CLS, Tafazzin); Cardiolipin (CL); PPi (→ 2 Pi); Pi

Key notes
- All enzymes are on the inner mitochondrial membrane (matrix side).
- Tafazzin remodels newly made CL to mature, symmetrical species.
- Bacteria use related pathways (PGP → CL via PgsA/Cls).

## 3. MAJOR FUNCTIONS OF CARDIOLIPIN
Stabilizes mitochondrial structure
- Forms non-bilayer structure in cristae; promotes membrane curvature and fusion.

Organizes respiratory supercomplexes
- Binds and stabilizes complexes I, III, IV and ATP synthase → efficient electron transport and ATP production.

Required for oxidative phosphorylation
- Essential for proton gradient maintenance and ATP synthesis.

Role in apoptosis
- Peroxidized CL releases cytochrome c → apoptotic signalling.

Mitochondrial quality control
- Important for fusion/fission balance and mitophagy.

## 5. DISEASES ASSOCIATED WITH CARDIOLIPIN DYSFUNCTION
Cardiolipin depletion
- Barth syndrome (tafazzin mutation): reduced CL remodelling → cardiomyopathy, skeletal myopathy, neutropenia.
- Mitochondrial diseases and developmental disorders.

Peroxidized cardiolipin
- Oxidative damage to CL promotes apoptosis and mitochondrial dysfunction.
- Linked to neurodegenerative diseases (e.g., Alzheimer's, Parkinson's), ischemia-reperfusion injury and aging.

Cancer
- Altered CL levels and remodelling enzymes (tafazzin, ALCAT1) implicated in tumour metabolism and therapy resistance.

Other
- Heart failure
- Diabetes
- Liver disease
- Sepsis

## 4. REMODELING AND CATABOLISM
Remodelling (acyl chain editing)
- Phospholipase A2 removes sn-2 fatty acid → monolysocardiolipin (MLCL).
- Lysocardiolipin acyltransferases (ALCAT1, tafazzin) re-acylate → mature CL.
- Lands' cycle maintains fatty acid composition and symmetry.

Diagram labels: PLA2; Monolysocardiolipin (MLCL); ALCAT1/Tafazzin

Catabolism
- Phospholipase A2/PLD → lysocardiolipin or phosphatidic acid (PA).
- Phospholipase A1 + PLA2 → glycerol + phosphatidic acid.
- Hydrolysis products are recycled or used for signalling.

## 6. KEY POINTS
- Cardiolipin is a dimeric phospholipid unique to mitochondria and some bacteria.
- Has a simple, highly conserved fatty acid composition (mostly 18:2).
- Essential for mitochondrial structure, respiratory supercomplex function and bioenergetics.
- Synthesized in mitochondria from PA via CDP-DAG and PGP.
- Continuously remodelled and degraded; its turnover links to signalling and quality control.
- Dysregulation is implicated in many human diseases.

## Key message
Cardiolipin is indispensable for mitochondrial energy production and integrity. Its unique structure, tightly controlled metabolism and multifunctional roles make it central to cellular homeostasis and a target in many diseases.
