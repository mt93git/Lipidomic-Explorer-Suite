# 8. CHOLESTEROL AND DISEASE

## (untitled panel)
Cholesterol is essential for life, but when its homeostasis is disturbed, or when sterol metabolism is genetically or environmentally altered, disease can result. Below are key human disorders and health issues in which cholesterol and its metabolism play major roles.

## 1. CARDIOVASCULAR DISEASE
- Hypercholesterolemia is a major risk factor for atherosclerosis, MI and stroke.
- Excess cholesterol in intima is taken up by macrophages → foam cells → fatty streaks → atherosclerotic plaques.
- Statins inhibit HMG-CoA reductase (the rate-limiting enzyme in sterol synthesis).
- Other potential drug targets: squalene monooxygenase, lanosterol synthase, ACAT1.
- Mediterranean-type diet and healthy lifestyle are preferred preventive strategies.

## Key points
- Elevated LDL cholesterol and cholesterol esters promote atherosclerotic plaque formation.
- Plaque rupture can cause myocardial infarction and stroke.
- Risk increased by hypertension, diabetes, smoking, inflammation, abnormalities, genetic factors and lifestyle.

## 2. CHOLELITHIASIS (GALLSTONES)
- Cholesterol supersaturation of bile → nucleation of cholesterol crystals.
- Stones may form in gallbladder or bile ducts.
- Can cause biliary colic, cholecystitis, pancreatitis and other complications.
- High prevalence and high healthcare cost in developed countries.

## Key points
- Gallstones consist mainly of cholesterol (~85%) with smaller amounts of bile pigments and calcium salts.
- Primary cause: excessive hepatic secretion of cholesterol into bile.
- Genetic, hormonal and dietary factors also contribute.

## 3. INHERITED DISORDERS OF CHOLESTEROL METABOLISM
- ≥8 congenital disorders of cholesterol biosynthesis cause developmental malformations and neurologic defects.
- Limited placental transfer of cholesterol → fetus depends on endogenous synthesis.
- Impaired hedgehog signalling (cholesterol-modified proteins) in many cases.

Diagram labels: Lanosterol → Desmosterol; (7-DHCR); Cholesterol

Example: Smith–Lemli–Opitz syndrome (7-dehydrocholesterol reductase deficiency)
- Accumulation of 7-dehydrocholesterol and 27-hydroxy metabolite in brain.
- Symptoms range from mild autism to severe, often fatal, abnormalities.
- Dietary or peripheral cholesterol cannot cross the blood–brain barrier.

## 4. LYSOSOMAL STORAGE DISEASES
Niemann–Pick type C disease
- Defective intracellular cholesterol transport due to mutations in NPC1 (or NPC2).
- Accumulation of unesterified cholesterol and other lipids in late endosomal/lysosomal compartments.
- Leads to neurodegeneration and multi-organ dysfunction.
- Distinct from Niemann–Pick types A and B (sphingomyelinase deficiency), though secondary cholesterol storage can occur.

Diagram labels: Late endosome/lysosome

Other storage disorders
- Deficiencies of cholesterol ester hydrolases:
- Wolman disease (lysosomal acid lipase deficiency)
- Cholesteryl ester storage disease
- Accumulation of cholesterol esters in lysosomes.
- Causes hepatosplenomegaly, steatosis, failure to thrive.

## 5. NEUROLOGIC AND EYE DISEASES
- Cholesterol is vital for myelin, synapse function and neuronal membranes.
- Altered cholesterol metabolism implicated in:
- Neurodegenerative diseases (Alzheimer's, Huntington's).
- Niemann–Pick type C disease.
- Cognitive decline with aging.
- Eye disorders linked to cholesterol dysregulation:
- Age-related macular degeneration
- Diabetic retinopathy
- Cataracts, corneal disease, dry eye syndrome
- Oxysterols and cholesterol metabolites may contribute to pathology.

## 6. IMMUNITY AND INFLAMMATION
Diagram labels: Macrophage; T cell; Cholesterol; αβTCR

- Cholesterol and sterols bind immune receptors in macrophages and T cells.
- Dynamic control of cholesterol synthesis affects innate and adaptive immune responses.
- Cholesterol binds to αβ T cell receptor (αβTCR) in transmembrane region, maintaining an inert state until needed → receptor nanoclustering and antigen recognition.
- Sterol regulatory pathways (SREBP, LXR, etc.) modulate inflammatory signalling.
- Dysregulated cholesterol homeostasis can promote chronic inflammation and autoimmunity.

## 7. CANCER
- Cancer cells require high cholesterol for membrane biogenesis, signalling and proliferation.
- Enhanced synthesis, increased uptake and impaired efflux drive malignant phenotype.
- Crosstalk between SREBP2 and oncogenic signalling pathways promotes tumorigenesis.
- Overexpression of squalene epoxidase and ACAT1 reported in some cancers.
- Inhibitors of HMG-CoA reductase (statins) and other enzymes (e.g., squalene epoxidase, ACAT1) are under clinical investigation.

## Impact
Perturbation of reverse cholesterol transport and sterol metabolism supports tumor growth and resistance to therapy.

## 8. STEROL DISORDERS AND BIOMARKERS
Diagram labels: Cholestanol; Campesterol; Sitosterol

- Elevated non-cholesterol sterols in plasma are markers of metabolic disease.
- Cerebrotendinous xanthomatosis: accumulation of cholestanol in tissues.
- Sitosterolemia: accumulation of dietary plant sterols (sitosterol, campesterol).
- Xanthomas and premature atherosclerosis may occur.
- Appearance of precursor sterols can reflect inhibited cholesterol synthesis.

## 9. INFECTION
Diagram labels: M. tuberculosis; C. trachomatis; HIV

- Many pathogens exploit host cholesterol for survival and replication.
- Mycobacterium tuberculosis uses host cholesterol as a carbon and energy source in macrophages.
- Chlamydia trachomatis requires host cholesterol for growth.
- HIV envelope protein binds cholesterol in lipid rafts to mediate viral entry and membrane fusion.
- Reducing cellular cholesterol may have antiviral potential, but effects are context-dependent.

## 10. SUMMARY
- Cholesterol is essential but potentially harmful in excess or when misregulated.
- Dysregulation contributes to cardiovascular disease, gallstones, genetic disorders, lysosomal storage diseases, neurodegeneration, cancer, immune dysfunction and infections.
- Many diseases involve either excess cholesterol/esters, defective metabolism, or aberrant sterol signalling.
- Management includes lifestyle, diet, and pharmacologic therapies targeting cholesterol synthesis, uptake and transport.
- Continued research on sterol biology is critical for improved treatments.
