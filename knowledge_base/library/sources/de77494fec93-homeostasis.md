# 4. REGULATION OF CHOLESTEROL HOMEOSTASIS

## (untitled panel)
Cholesterol cannot be catabolized for energy. Its levels are maintained by a dynamic equilibrium of synthesis, uptake, efflux and conversion. Cholesterol in the plasma membrane (PM) regulates gene expression and enzyme activity in the endoplasmic reticulum (ER) to ensure an adequate but not excessive supply.

## 4.1 CHOLESTEROL POOLS IN THE PLASMA MEMBRANE
Diagram labels: Extracellular; Cytosol; 1. Accessible (active) pool; 2. Sphingomyelin-sequestered pool; 3. Structural (residual) pool

1. Accessible (active) pool: Readily available to receptors and transport proteins; moves to ER or to nascent HDL. ~16 mol% of PM lipids

2. Sphingomyelin-sequestered pool: Bound by sphingomyelin in lipid rafts; released by sphingomyelinase when needed. ~15 mol% of PM lipids

3. Structural (residual) pool: Essential for membrane integrity and barrier function; not mobilized under normal conditions. ~12 mol% of PM lipids

When cellular cholesterol is in excess
- ↑ Accessible cholesterol (after LDL uptake, diet, etc.)
- Some cholesterol → nascent HDL
- Most cholesterol → returned to ER
- Inhibits cholesterol biosynthesis and LDL receptor expression
- Cholesterol homeostasis is restored

Transport to ER via vesicular and non-vesicular pathways using carriers (e.g., ABC transporters, Aster proteins A, B, C).

Values are approximate for cholesterol-replete cells. Pools are dynamic, not fixed compartments.

## 4.2 SREBP–SCAP REGULATORY PATHWAY
LOW CHOLESTEROL IN ER
1. SCAP senses low cholesterol (binds glutamine).
2. SCAP–SREBP-2 complex moves to Golgi.
3. Sequential cleavage by Site-1 and Site-2 proteases.
4. N-terminal fragment (NTF) translocates to nucleus.
5. Activates genes: LDL receptor; HMG-CoA reductase; Other cholesterol biosynthetic genes
↑ Cholesterol uptake and synthesis

HIGH CHOLESTEROL IN ER
1. ER cholesterol binds SCAP.
2. SCAP–SREBP-2 is retained in ER.
3. No cleavage; SREBP-2 cannot enter nucleus.
4. Transcription of target genes is repressed.
↓ Cholesterol synthesis and ↓ LDL receptor expression

Diagram labels: SCAP; SREBP-2 (precursor); ER; Golgi; NTF; Feedback inhibition

## 4.3 CHOLESTEROL HOMEOSTASIS: OVERVIEW
- Synthesis occurs in ER; uptake via LDL receptor in PM.
- Plasma membrane cholesterol regulates SREBP pathway.
- Feedback control prevents both deficiency and excess.
- Multiple layers of regulation act on genes, proteins and enzymes.
- Cholesterol is also esterified (ACAT) and exported (ABCA1).

## KEY REGULATORY PLAYERS
SCAP (SREBP cleavage-activating protein)
Cholesterol sensor in ER; escorts SREBP to Golgi when cholesterol is low.

SREBP-2 (sterol regulatory element-binding protein-2)
Master transcription factor for cholesterol homeostasis.

Insig (insulin-induced gene 1 protein)
Binds SCAP–HMGCR complex when cholesterol is high; retains in ER and targets HMGCR for degradation.

LXR (liver X receptor)
Nuclear receptor activated by oxysterols; promotes cholesterol efflux and modulates SREBP transcription.

## TRANSPORT AND EFFLUX
LDL receptor (LDLR): mediates uptake of LDL-cholesterol.

HDL / ABCA1: mediates cholesterol efflux to HDL.

ABC transporters (e.g., ABCG1, ABCG5/G8): export cholesterol and sterols to HDL or bile.

Aster proteins (A, B, C): non-vesicular transport of cholesterol from PM to ER contact sites.

## CONSEQUENCES OF DYSREGULATION
Excess cholesterol
- Hypercholesterolemia
- Atherosclerosis
- Fatty liver disease
- Gallstones
- Some cancers

Low cholesterol
- Impaired membrane structure
- Defective steroid hormone and bile acid synthesis
- Neurological disorders
- Developmental defects

## 4.4 POST-TRANSLATIONAL REGULATION OF BIOSYNTHETIC ENZYMES
HMG-CoA REDUCTASE

Regulation
- Binding of sterols triggers Insig binding.
- Insig holds SCAP–HMGCR complex in ER.
- HMGCR ubiquitinated and degraded by proteasome.
- Also regulated by phosphorylation (AMPK ↑ activity; insulin ↑ activity).

Diagram labels: NH2; COOH; HMG-CoA; NADPH; Mevalonate

Rate-limiting step (statin target)

SQUALENE MONOOXYGENASE (SQM)

Regulation
- Cholesterol binds to SQM.
- 12-aa hydrophobic degron is exposed.
- Ubiquitinated and degraded by proteasome.

Diagram labels: NH2; COOH; Squalene; O2, NADPH; Squalene 2,3-epoxide

Rate-limiting step in committed pathway

OTHER BIOSYNTHETIC ENZYMES
- Many enzymes are rapidly turned over.
- Regulated by sterol intermediates: lanosterol, 24,25-dehydrolanosterol (dimethyl-sterols).
- Cholesterol sulfate and side-chain oxysterols accelerate degradation.
- Enzyme activities also controlled by phosphorylation, acetylation, and allosteric effectors.

Result: rapid tuning of flux through the pathway according to cellular need.

## 4.5 INTEGRATION WITH CELLULAR LIPID CONTROL
AMPK activation (energy stress)
↓ cholesterol synthesis (via HMGCR phosphorylation)

Insulin / mTOR activation
↑ SREBP processing → ↑ synthesis

Oxysterols activate LXR
↑ ABCA1, ABCG1 → ↑ efflux to HDL
↓ SREBP-2 transcription

PPARα activation
Enhances cholesterol catabolism to bile acids in liver

## ENERGY COST
Synthesis of 1 cholesterol molecule consumes: 18 ATP, 16 NADPH, 11 O2 and acetyl-CoA (~100 ATP equivalents).
