# 4. CARDIOLIPIN IN DISEASE

## A. BARTH SYNDROME (X-LINKED INFANTILE CARDIOMYOPATHY)
Normal vs. Barth syndrome cardiolipin profile in blood

Diagram labels: Normal; Barth syndrome; High tetra-linoleoyl cardiolipin (CLtetra-18:2); ↓ CLtetra-18:2; ↑ Monolysocardiolipin (MLCL)

- Caused by mutations in the tafazzin (TAZ) gene → defective remodeling of cardiolipin.
- ↓ Tetra-linoleoyl CL, ↑ monolysocardiolipin (MLCL).
- MLCL binds OXPHOS proteins with much lower affinity → impaired assembly and function of Complex I.
- ↓ Oxidative phosphorylation, ↑ membrane permeability.
- Consequences: ↓ TCA cycle enzyme activities, abnormal cristae morphology → skeletal muscle weakness, dilated cardiomyopathy.
- Yeast tafazzin mutants show similar biochemical defects.

## Pathogenic mechanism (leading hypothesis)
Diagram labels: MLCL + cytochrome c complex; acts as peroxidase; ROS; Oxidizes PUFA in phospholipids; Lipid peroxidation & mitochondrial dysfunction; Energy failure & cellular damage

## Therapeutic approaches
- Mitochondria-targeted anti-peroxidants (in Drosophila model: improved respiration).
- Restore plasmenylcholine (plasmalogen) with linoleate (in vitro model) → improves CL levels.
- Elamipretide (SS-31), a mitochondria-targeted tetrapeptide that binds cardiolipin → clinical trials show improvement in some symptoms.
- Stabilizing mitochondrial calcium uniporter may also be beneficial.

## B. OTHER DISEASES AND PATHOPHYSIOLOGICAL CONSEQUENCES
Cardiovascular diseases
- Reduced CL or altered composition linked to heart failure, ischemia–reperfusion injury.
- Excessive peroxidation of CL in ischemic heart & skeleton muscle → oxidative stress.

Diabetes
- Early diabetes: marked decline in myocardial CL.
- Remodelling to DHA-rich species (very oxidizable).
- Enzyme ALCAT1 (acyl-CoA:lysoCL acyltransferase 1) upregulated by oxidative stress → CL remodelling → lipid hydroperoxides → vicious cycle of oxidative stress, CL depletion and mitochondrial dysfunction in age-related diseases.

Neurodegenerative diseases
- Altered CL metabolism in brain mitochondria in Alzheimer's and Parkinson's diseases.
- α-Synuclein oligomers bind to CL → disrupt membrane integrity and mitochondrial function.
- Brain-type CLs (PUFA-rich) appear in plasma after cardiac arrest or traumatic brain injury → biomarkers of mitochondrial/tissue injury.

Autoimmune & inflammatory conditions
- Anti-cardiolipin antibodies in diseases with tissue damage → danger signals.
- Used in diagnosis of unexplained thrombosis or recurrent miscarriages.
- Unsaturated CLs can compete with LPS for TLR4 binding → anti-inflammatory; saturated CLs activate TLR4 → pro-inflammatory.

Infectious diseases
- Treponema pallidum (syphilis) releases host mitochondrial CL → major stimulus for anti-cardiolipin antibodies.
- Cardiolipin isolation in 1942 by M. Pangborn and structure elucidation in 1964 by LeCocq & Ballou were driven by this serologic property.

## Overall
Cardiolipin deficiency, oxidation, or abnormal remodelling impairs mitochondrial bioenergetics, increases ROS, and contributes to a wide range of human diseases.

## 5. RELATED LIPIDS (CARDIOLIPIN ANALOGUES)
Animals and higher plants contain only cardiolipin (CL). Many bacteria and some Archaea contain cardiolipin analogues.

## Phosphatidylglycerophosphoglycerol (PGPG)
Two phosphatidylglycerol units linked by phosphoglycerol bridge.

## D-Glucopyranosylcardiolipin (GluCL)
Glucose linked to the 2'-hydroxyl on the central glycerol. Molecules with 1–5 glucose units are known.

## D-Alanylcardiolipin (AlaCL)
D-Alanine linked to the 2'-hydroxyl of central glycerol.

## L-Lysylcardiolipin (LysCL)
L-Lysine linked to the 2'-hydroxyl of central glycerol. Major lipid of Listeria species (up to ~30% of total phospholipids).

## Plasmenyl cardiolipin (Plasmenyl CL)
One or more alkenyl ether chains (plasmalogen form). Found in many Gram-positive and Gram-negative bacteria.

## Acetal cardiolipin (PG acetal of plasmenyl CL)
Phosphatidylglycerol acetal of plasmenyl diphosphatidylglycerol.

## Cyclohexyl cardiolipin (e.g., Alicyclobacillus acidoterrestris)
Fatty acids contain terminal cyclohexyl group. Found in thermophilic bacteria.

## Glycocardiolipin (Archaea) (Halobacterium salinarum)
Diagram labels: (SO3-)

Sulfo-triglycosyl-diether ester linked to phosphatidic acid. Induced by osmotic shock.

## Diethanolamine cardiolipin (Morganella morganii)
Diethanolamine replaces central glycerol. Mainly 16:0 at sn-1; sn-2 has mono-enoic and cyclopropanoid FAs. Associated with major depressive disorder (human gut microbiome).

## Functions of cardiolipin analogues
- Synthesised by analogous enzymes to cardiolipin.
- Participate in membrane organization, bioenergetics, stress response and pathogenesis.
- Structural diversity allows adaptation to different organisms and environments.

## Abbreviations
CL: cardiolipin
PG: phosphatidylglycerol
PUFA: polyunsaturated fatty acid
S: sulfate
