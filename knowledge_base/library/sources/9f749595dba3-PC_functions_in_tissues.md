# 5. PHOSPHATIDYLCHOLINE (PC) – FUNCTIONS IN TISSUES

## (untitled panel)
Phosphatidylcholine is the most abundant phospholipid in animals and plants and a key structural component of membranes and lipoproteins. It also serves as a precursor of many bioactive lipids and is essential for numerous physiological processes.

## A. STRUCTURAL ROLES IN MEMBRANES
Bilayer formation
- Cylindrical shape → forms stable bilayers.
- Makes up 80–90% of the outer leaflet of the plasma membrane of nucleated cells.
- Unsaturated acyl chains (kinked) confer fluidity.

Diagram labels: Outer leaflet (PC-rich); Inner leaflet (PE, PS-rich)

Specialized roles
- Does not induce curvature but can be metabolically converted to lipids that do.
- Microdomain (raft) organization is influenced by PC.

Lung surfactant
- Dipalmitoyl-PC (DPPC) is the main surface-active component in humans.
- Lowers surface tension during inspiration and prevents alveolar collapse at expiration.

Diagram labels: Alveolar air; Alveolar lining fluid

Nuclear membranes
- Internal nuclear membranes are enriched in disaturated PC.
- Synthesised entirely in nucleus and composition is resistant to dietary manipulation.
- May stabilize/regulate chromatin structure and provide DAG for signalling.

Diagram labels: Nucleus

Unique molecular species
- 1-Oleoyl-2-palmitoyl-PC: at tips of neuronal protrusions.
- 1-Palmitoyl-2-arachidonoyl-PC: required for cell cycle progression and proliferation.

## B. INTERACTIONS WITH PROTEINS
- PC is bound non-covalently in many membrane protein structures (e.g., cytochrome c oxidase, yeast cytochrome bc1).
- Both headgroup and acyl chains can participate in binding.
- 3-Hydroxybutyrate dehydrogenase requires PC binding for optimal activity.
- ADP/ATP carrier has two PC binding sites (one on each side).

Diagram labels: Cytochrome c oxidase; Cytochrome bc1 (yeast); ADP/ATP carrier (two PC sites)

## C. MAJOR PHYSIOLOGICAL FUNCTIONS
## Lipoprotein assembly and secretion
- PC (especially with PUFA in sn-2) is indispensable for assembly and secretion of VLDL and chylomicrons in liver and intestine.
- Stabilizes surface of lipid droplets.
- Inhibits endocytosis when abundant in outer membrane.

Diagram labels: VLDL

## Bile and lipid digestion
- PC is secreted into bile with bile acids.
- Emulsifies dietary TAGs in intestinal lumen.
- After hydrolysis to LPC, it is absorbed and contributes to chylomicron formation.
- PC in mucus layer protects intestinal surface from gut microbiota.

Diagram labels: Bile (PC + bile acids)

## Lipid droplet metabolism
- PC is required to stabilize lipid droplets that store triacylglycerols.

## Signalling functions
- PLD → phosphatidic acid (PA): regulates PI4P 5-kinase → PIP2 (second messenger).
- PLC → diacylglycerols (DAGs): act in signal transduction.
- Plasmenyl PC can release arachidonic acid (PLA2) for eicosanoid synthesis.

Diagram labels: PC; PLC; PLD; PLA2; DAG; PA; Arachidonic acid; Signalling

## Biosynthetic precursor
PC is the precursor of many important lipids.
- Sphingomyelin
- Phosphatidic acid
- Lysophosphatidylcholine
- Platelet-activating factor (PAF)
- Phosphatidylserine

## Other physiological roles
- PC in plasma strongly influences levels of all lipoprotein classes.
- Necessary for proper function of mitochondria and many enzymes.
- Contributes to membrane integrity, fluidity and protein function.

## Disease relevance
- Altered PC levels or species are biomarkers for liver, cardiovascular, kidney, lung, brain diseases, cancer, sepsis and diabetes.
- Impaired PC biosynthesis in liver is linked to NAFLD, liver failure and poor regeneration.

## Role in cancer
- PC, DAG and LPC promote tumour growth by enhancing membrane biogenesis, activating signalling, suppressing apoptosis and aiding immune evasion.
- ChoK and LPCAT3 are potential therapeutic targets.

## D. PHOSPHATIDYLCHOLINE IN DIFFERENT TISSUES
## Plasma and lipoproteins
Major phospholipid in all lipoproteins; highest in HDL.

Diagram labels: HDL

## Liver
- Both Kennedy and methylation pathways are required for normal lipoprotein secretion.
- Some PC → TAGs via DAG intermediates.

## Kidney
Choline can be oxidized to betaine in kidney.

## Nervous tissue
Essential for membrane function, synaptic signalling and myelin.

## Erythrocytes
PC is almost exclusively in outer leaflet.

## E. ROLES IN PLANTS AND BACTERIA
## Plants
- Structural role in membranes.
- PC level at shoot apex correlates with flowering time; may bind to Flowering Locus T (FT).
- NPCs (non-specific PLCs) hydrolyse PC to DAG – important under phosphate deprivation.
- PC-Derived PA (via PLD) is a key signalling lipid.

## Bacteria
- PC is present in ~10% of bacterial species.
- Crucial for symbiotic and pathogenic microbe–host interactions.
- Required for full virulence in pathogens (e.g., Brucella abortus, Legionella pneumophila, Agrobacterium tumefaciens).
- Important for beneficial symbiosis (e.g., Bradyrhizobium japonicum forms root nodules).

## F. KEY POINTS
- PC is the most abundant phospholipid in animals and plants.
- Forms bilayers and is crucial for membrane structure and function.
- Essential for lipoprotein metabolism, lipid droplet stability and bile secretion.
- Acts in signalling and is a precursor of many bioactive lipids.
- Alterations in PC metabolism are linked to many diseases and cancers.
- Important in plant stress responses and microbe–host interactions.

## SUMMARY
Phosphatidylcholine is a fundamental lipid in all living systems. It provides structural stability to membranes, enables specialised functions in surfactant and nuclei, participates in lipid transport and metabolism, acts in signalling, and serves as a precursor of bioactive lipids. Its metabolism is tightly linked to health and disease.
