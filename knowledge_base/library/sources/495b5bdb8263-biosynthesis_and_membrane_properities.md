# 2. BIOSYNTHESIS AND MEMBRANE PROPERTIES OF SPHINGOMYELIN

## OVERVIEW
Sphingomyelin (SM) is synthesised by transfer of phosphorylcholine (P-Cho) from phosphatidylcholine (PC) to ceramide by sphingomyelin synthases (SMS). It is enriched in the trans Golgi and outer leaflet of the plasma membrane.

## A. BIOSYNTHESIS OF SPHINGOMYELIN
Diagram labels: PHOSPHATIDYLCHOLINE (PC); CERAMIDE; SMS (Sphingomyelin synthase); SPHINGOMYELIN (SM); DIACYLGLYCEROL (DAG)
- Reversible reaction. In absence of ceramide, SMS1 and SMS2 show phospholipase C activity → PC + DAG.
- Occurs on the luminal side of trans Golgi (SMS1) and in plasma membrane (SMS2).

## REGULATION BY LIPIDS AND PROTEINS
- DAG activates protein kinase D (PKD). PKD phosphorylates CERT → conformational change → reduced ceramide transfer.
- Phosphoinositides and OSBP1 (oxysterol-binding protein 1, cholesterol transfer protein) also inhibit CERT → repress SM synthesis.
- Molecular species of SM differ from ceramide precursors → SMS enzymes show substrate specificity.

## FUNCTIONAL ROLES OF ISOFORMS
- SMS1 (Golgi): requires CERT-delivered ceramide.
- SMS2 (plasma membrane): not CERT-dependent; recycles ceramide (e.g., from SMase action) back to SM → protective mechanism.
- SMS2 present in nuclear membranes (rat liver).
- Some synthesis occurs in hepatic lipid droplets.

## ALTERNATIVE PATHWAY (in ER)
Ceramide + Phosphatidylethanolamine (PE) → Ceramide phosphoethanolamine (CPE) → stepwise methylation → Sphingomyelin (SM)

## KEY POINTS (BIOSYNTHESIS)
- SM is made by transfer of phosphorylcholine from PC to ceramide.
- SMS1 acts in the trans Golgi (requires CERT); SMS2 acts in the plasma membrane (does not require CERT).
- Reaction is reversible; SM can be hydrolysed to generate ceramide for signalling.
- Diacylglycerol (DAG) from the reaction participates in negative feedback regulation via PKD.

## C. MEMBRANE PROPERTIES OF SPHINGOMYELIN

## Structural features that confer unique properties
- Amide bond at position 2 and hydroxyl at position 3 → extensive hydrogen bonding.
- Trans double bond (4E) in sphingoid base → promotes tight packing and intermolecular interactions.
- Sphingoid motif (C1–C5 region) interacts with lipids and proteins via H-bonding, charge-pairing, hydrophobic and van der Waals forces.
- Zwitterionic head group (P-Cho) like PC, but packing and H-bonding are very different.

## Physical behaviour
- Cylindrical shape → geometric stability of bilayers.
- High melting temperature (Tm usually >37 °C) due to long saturated/monoenoic chains → remains in gel (ordered) phase at 37 °C.
- Increases membrane order and rigidity.
- Less permeable to small molecules and O2.

## Differences from phosphatidylcholine (PC)
- PC has two ester carbonyls (H-bond acceptors only); SM has amide + hydroxyl (donor & acceptor).
- Different acyl chain composition (SM more saturated/very-long-chain).
- Pack in very different ways → different biophysical behaviour.

## Lipid raft (microdomain) organisation
- SM has strong affinity for cholesterol and certain proteins → forms lipid rafts (ordered nano-domains).
- Initially thought SM + cholesterol always form liquid-ordered phase; current view: cholesterol important but not sole determinant in vivo.
- SM and cholesterol metabolism are closely linked (reciprocal regulation).
- Ceramide generated by SM hydrolysis displaces cholesterol from SM-rich domains (ceramide has higher affinity).

## Functional consequences
- Rafts concentrate receptors and signalling proteins (e.g., GPCRs, RTKs, Src-family kinases).
- Facilitate signal initiation and amplification.
- Important in endocytosis, cell adhesion, immune responses, myelin stability and viral entry.
- Contribute to membrane mechanical stability, especially under stress.

## Chain-length dependence
- Very-long-chain SMs (20–24 carbons) → strongest ordering and raft association.
- Short-chain SMs (≤16 carbons) → less ordered, less raft-promoting.
- Tissue- and species-specific molecular species determine membrane behaviour.

## KEY TAKEAWAY MESSAGE
Sphingomyelin is synthesized by SMS enzymes using PC as the phosphorylcholine donor.
It is enriched in the outer leaflet of the plasma membrane and trans Golgi.
Its unique structure enables tight packing, strong interactions with cholesterol and proteins, and the formation of ordered lipid rafts critical for signalling and membrane stability.
