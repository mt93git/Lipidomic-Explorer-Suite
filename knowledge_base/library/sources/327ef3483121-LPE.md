# 4. LYSOPHOSPHATIDYLETHANOLAMINE (LPE)

## A. Structure and occurrence
Lysophosphatidylethanolamine (LPE) or 1-acyl-sn-glycero-3-phosphoethanolamine

Diagram labels: sn-1; sn-2; sn-3; R = fatty acyl chain (common: 16:0, 18:1, 20:4, 22:6, etc.)

## Occurrence
- Found in trace amounts in animal tissues.
- Most abundant in plasma (10–50 µM, ~1% of total serum phospholipids).
- Formed mainly by hydrolysis of phosphatidylethanolamine (PE) by phospholipase A2 (PLA2).

## Source and metabolism
Diagram labels: Phosphatidylethanolamine (PE); PLA2; Lysophosphatidylethanolamine (LPE); Free fatty acid

## Remodelling cycle (Lands’ cycle)
LPE is re-acylated by acyl-CoA:lysophospholipid acyltransferases to restore PE and maintain its molecular composition.

## B. Functions in animal tissues
## Neuronal and bone cell functions
- Involved in differentiation and migration.
- Oleoyl-LPE in brain stimulates neurite outgrowth and protects against glutamate toxicity.
- Greatly reduced levels of 22:6-LPE observed in Alzheimer’s disease brains (post-mortem).

## Possible mechanisms
- Effects may depend on the acyl chain (chain length and degree of unsaturation).
- May act as an agonist for the LPA receptor LPAR1 (G-protein–coupled receptor).

## Cardiovascular / aging
- Administration of LPE to aging rats improves diastolic dysfunction.
- Alleviates mitochondrial injury.

## Inflammation and signalling
- Alkenyl (18:1) LPE acts as a signalling molecule.
- Induced by inflammatory stimuli; transcriptional regulation of nuclear receptor RORγt (retinoic-related orphan receptor gamma).

Diagram labels: LPE (18:1); Nucleus; RORγt

## Cancer
- Reported to influence proliferation, migration and invasion of various cancer cells.
- Acyl chain composition may modulate activity.

## Other reported effects (in vitro)
- Influences cell proliferation, survival and motility.
- May act via LPA receptor LPAR1.

## C. Functions in plants
Lysophosphatidylethanolamine inhibits phospholipase D (PLD), a key enzyme in the early stages of plant senescence.

Diagram labels: LPE; PLD; Membrane phospholipids; Lipid degradation products

## Biological effects
- Retards senescence of leaves, flowers and post-harvest fruits.
- Delays yellowing, maintains membrane integrity and photosynthetic capacity.
- Extends shelf-life of fruit and vase life of cut flowers.

## Applications in horticulture
Diagram labels: Fruit ripening; Shelf-life extension; Delay of senescence; Flower vase life extension

## (untitled panel)
Applied externally (e.g., as a spray or dip) at very low concentrations.

## D. Functions in bacteria
## 1. Chaperone-like activity
Diagram labels: LPE; Unfolded protein (e.g., citrate synthase); Properly folded protein (enzyme active)

## 2. Role in membrane homeostasis and recycling
Diagram labels: Outer leaflet (periplasmic side); LPE; Flippases (transport LPE inward); Inner leaflet (cytoplasmic side); Acyl-ACP synthetase / LPL acyltransferase; PE (diacyl form) restored

- LPE is generated in envelope membranes by endogenous and exogenous factors.
- Transported back into the cell by flippases.
- Re-acylated to PE to protect membrane integrity.
- Acts as a signalling molecule to coordinate the process in E. coli.

## 3. Communication and multicellularity
Diagram labels: Certain bacteria; LPE; Sulfonolipid rosette-inducing factors (RIFs); Choanoflagellates; Multicellular state

LPE from certain bacteria acts synergistically with RIFs to induce choanoflagellates to transition from a unicellular to a multicellular state.

## Key points
- Present at low levels but biologically active.
- Acyl chain structure influences function.
- Acts in diverse roles: signalling, protection, metabolism, development and communication.
